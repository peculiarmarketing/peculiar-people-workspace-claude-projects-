## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Three Names: Miller's Hollow after Henry Miller's encampment (B, C, D; I: "after early bishop Henry Miller"). Kanesville for Thomas L. Kane, a non-member friend who helped muster the battalion (B, C, D; J). Became Council Bluffs after the Saints left (B: "the remaining citizens renamed the town Council Bluffs"; C: December 1853, non-Mormon residents incorporated and renamed it; J: 1853 by special charter of the legislature). Year left out of copy; see Dropped.
- [T1] The Kanesville Years: 1846 to 1852 (A, I). "A place of rest, preparation, organization, and ministering" (A), paraphrased.

What Happened Here

- [T1] 14 June 1846 arrival at the Missouri (A; H timeline). Temporary headquarters at Mosquito Creek on Pottawattamie land; landings and a ferry boat built in about two weeks (H). J agrees on camps near Mosquito Creek.
- [T1] 16 July 1846: battalion created, nearly 500 men mustered at Kanesville (A); G confirms the 16 July 1846 muster date. Allen arrived late June (A). Brigham Young, "This is the first offer we have ever had from the government to benefit us" (H, citing Manuscript History of Brigham Young, p. 205). K: four companies of 100, plus a fifth; war with Mexico (A).
- [T1] 5 December 1847: nine of the Twelve met at Orson Hyde's home and sustained Brigham Young (B); F gives 5 December as the date the Twelve agreed. 27 December 1847: First Presidency re-established before more than 1,000 (B); about 1,000 (C, D); Kimball and Richards as counselors (F, A). Tabernacle 60 by 40 feet, built by about 200 men in three weeks under Henry Miller (B, C, D). Middle initial W. from E (statue of Henry W. Miller).
- [T1] Oliver Cowdery: separated 10 years, came to Kanesville in 1848 and rejoined (E); rebaptized by Orson Hyde (L; I). A: rebaptized, reconfirmed and reordained in Kanesville.
- [T1] 1852: remaining Saints gathered to Utah under First Presidency direction (A); B dates the end to July 1852.

On the Map

- [T1] Kanesville Memorial, 222 East Broadway (A). Gardens, visitors' center, statues of Brigham Young with Kimball and Richards, Henry W. Miller at the garden entrance, Sophronia Standage whose husband served in the battalion (E). Dedicated 28 Sept 2024 by Elder Kyle S. McKay (E). Replica stood near the original tabernacle site (C).
- [T1/BYU] Battalion muster marker on the Iowa School for the Deaf campus, dedicated 16 July 1988, "exactly 142 years" after the muster (G). Muster site about three miles southeast of the memorial on the ISD campus (I); J agrees ("near what is now the Iowa School for the Deaf"). Grand Encampment site on the ISD grounds (C: telecast "at the Grand Encampment site ... on the grounds of the Iowa School for the Deaf").

Trivia

- [T1] Original tabernacle of cottonwood, soft and easy to tool (C, Hinckley; D: a harder wood would have taken longer). Spring runoffs 1848 and 1849, dismantled fall 1849 (C); D: dismantled 1849 after flooding.
- [T1/BYU] Replica dedicated by President Gordon B. Hinckley 13 July 1996 (C, contemporaneous; I agrees). Cottonwood beams from Plattsmouth, Nebraska, hand-chipped to look hand-hewn (C). Church took over 1999 (D, I). Closed 2020 (I). Demolition began 4 April 2022 (D); removed April 2022 (I). Memorial garden replaced it (D, E).

## Dropped

- Date of the rename to Council Bluffs. B (Newsroom) says July 1852; C (Church News 1996) says December 1853; J (NPS) says 1853 by special charter; leads give January 1853. Two Church sources disagree, so copy says only "after the Saints left."
- Date Kanesville was named. C: townsite laid out December 1847 and named Kanesville; I: renamed 1848. Omitted.
- Replica dedication date. D (2022) says 13 June 1996; C (1996, written the week of the event) and I say 13 July 1996. Used 13 July.
- "Said to be the largest log cabin in the world" (C). Hedged claim; omitted.
- Battalion departure date. K: 20 July; H: march began 21 July. Omitted.
- Oliver Cowdery's exact rebaptism date (I: October 1848; not confirmed elsewhere among fetched pages; L fetch did not give a date). Copy says 1848 only.
- About 70,000 passed through the Winter Quarters and Kanesville area (B). Includes the Nebraska side; omitted.
- 350 log cabins and two newspapers at its height (C). Single source; left out for length. Frontier Guardian 1849 to 1852 (J). Left out for length.
- Some 80 communities and 50 branches in southwest Iowa; Pottawattamie County petition (Church News, 13 July 1996, "Iowa crossing prepared way for others"). Left out for length.
- Mormon Battalion 2,000-mile march (E, K). Not Council Bluffs history beyond the muster.
- Musical "Come Home to Kanesville" (B). Not Church-run; volatile; omitted.
- Winter Quarters, Cutler's Park and anything in Omaha/Florence. Outside the Iowa-only frame.

## Markers (9 Oct 2026)

Frame lat 41.1313 to 41.3267, lon -95.9359 to -95.7424 (Iowa side only); merge distance 420 m; no temple. Two markers, about 4.7 km apart.

1. Iowa School for the Deaf grounds: Grand Encampment, battalion muster marker, Mosquito Creek camp area (merged). 41.22622, -95.82020. Coordinate source: OSM node 354358556 "Grand Encampment Marker" (Nominatim); school building OSM way 854240638 at 41.22483, -95.82027 (about 155 m away, under the 420 m merge distance). Certainty: approximate. Why: the campus markers are real and placed, but the first 1846 camp on Mosquito Creek is located only to the school's general area. [T1] Ensign, Sept 1979 (https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1979/09/the-mormon-pioneer-trail-1846-47?lang=eng): "The first Mormon encampment was along Mosquito Creek near the present Iowa School for the Deaf on Highway 275." [NPS] Historic Resource Study ch. 7 (https://npshistory.com/publications/mopi/hrs/chap7.htm), site 14: "Near the Iowa School for the Deaf ... is the general area of the first Mormon camp," with the battalion marker there. The 1988 muster marker's own position on the campus is not separately pinned; the point is the OSM Grand Encampment marker. In frame: yes.
2. Kanesville Memorial and log tabernacle site. 41.26483, -95.84210. Coordinate source: Church location page A map pin (41.264834, -95.842104); OSM way 1328902136 "Kanesville Tabernacle", 41.26491, -95.84192. Certainty: approximate. Why: the memorial is exact, but the 1847 tabernacle stood only "near" it. [T1] C (1996): replica "near the original site." [T1] Church News, 18 Dec 1993, "Trails group plans to rebuild church's 1847 log tabernacle" (https://www.thechurchnews.com/1993/12/18/23257631/trails-group-plans-to-rebuild-churchs-1847-log-tabernacle): "The original building, which was north of what is now Council Bluff's First Avenue near Indian Creek." [NPS] Kanesville Memorial Historic Site (https://www.nps.gov/places/kanesville-memorial-historic-site.htm) says the tabernacle was built "at this site"; less precise than the Church sources and not used for the wording. In frame: yes.

New facts used in copy: first camp on Mosquito Creek in the general area of the school (Ensign 1979, T1; NPS HRS); tabernacle north of today's First Avenue near Indian Creek (Church News 1993, T1).

Edit to existing copy: "near where the log tabernacle stood" moved out of the memorial's first sentence into the closing certainty sentence. No facts dropped.

Volatile: the 1993 location description was written before the replica site was chosen; re-check if the Church publishes a footprint.
