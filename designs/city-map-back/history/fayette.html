Fayette, New York

The Whitmers
Peter and Mary Whitmer came out of the German-speaking community that had settled in Pennsylvania, and moved to Fayette in the early 1800s. Their farm lay in the township between Seneca and Cayuga Lakes, about 30 miles from Palmyra.

In Fayette or Manchester?
The heading to Doctrine and Covenants 21 places the organization of the Church in Peter Whitmer Sr.'s home, and the Joseph Smith Papers put it in Fayette. People who were there did not all remember it that way. Joseph Smith's 1839 history says Fayette, and an 1842 article he wrote says Manchester. The Church's own history of the meeting says there is "some question" about the exact spot.

What Happened Here

  • June 1829: Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery moved in with the Whitmers to finish translating the Book of Mormon, and Emma joined them. That month, before they saw the plates, Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer and Martin Harris received the revelation now in section 17. Mary Whitmer saw the plates too, in a private visitation.
  • Tuesday, April 6, 1830: The Church was organized, with six men who had already been baptized taking part. Joseph and Oliver ordained each other elders, the group took the sacrament, and some of those present were baptized in Seneca Lake.
  • June 9, 1830: The first conference met at the Whitmer home. Three men were ordained, others received licenses for their offices, and the conference approved the Articles and Covenants.
  • December 1830: A revelation received near Fayette, now section 37, told the Church to gather to Ohio. The Whitmers sold the farm the next April.
  • May 1831: Lucy Mack Smith led about eighty members of the Fayette branch out of Seneca County, bound for Kirtland.
  • April 6, 1980: For the Church's 150th anniversary, President Spencer W. Kimball spoke from a reconstructed Whitmer farmhouse during general conference, broadcast by satellite, and dedicated it. The original house was long gone.

On the Map

  • The Whitmer farm on Aunkst Road has the rebuilt log home and a visitors' center. Its mail goes through Waterloo.
  • Seneca Lake, at the west edge of the map, where some of the first members were baptized.
  • Lucy Mack Smith's company started out on the Cayuga-Seneca Canal near Waterloo. The canal ran about 20 miles to the Erie Canal at Montezuma, and some of the old stone lining can still be seen.

Trivia

  • Joseph Smith began his translation of the Bible in Fayette township, and the first known licenses for Church ministers were issued here.
  • Fayette township had about 3,200 people in 1830. By 1840 it had about 3,700.
