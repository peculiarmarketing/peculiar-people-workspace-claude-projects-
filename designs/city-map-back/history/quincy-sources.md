## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Settled 1821, Adams County seat 1825, on high limestone bluffs east of the Mississippi (B).
- [T1] Population that winter: about 1,500 (C) and about 1,600 (H, I). Both Church sources; copy gives both. B gives about 800 in 1835 and 2,300 in 1840.
- [T1] More than 5,000 Saints sheltered (C, H, I); relief committee for "at least 5,000" (B). Thousands walked east across the frozen Mississippi (A). Housing and jobs, cash, clothing and supplies from townspeople and local groups (C).
- [T1] During the next year most moved 45 miles upriver to Commerce, founded Nauvoo (A). B: Quincy about 45 miles south of Nauvoo. (F's ch. 34 says "fifty miles" and H says about 55; copy uses A and B.)

What Happened Here

- [T1] Emma left Far West 7 Feb 1839 with Stephen Markham, arrived in Illinois mid-February (D). Walked across the frozen Mississippi with her four children (C); "two infants with two other young children clinging to her skirts" (E). Ann Scott sewed two cotton bags on a waistband to hide the Bible translation papers under a skirt (E). Living "at Judge [John] Cleveland's four miles from the village of Quincy" with John and Sarah Kingsley Cleveland (D). [BYU] G gives the arrival on the west bank as 15 Feb 1839; not used as a date in copy. "With Joseph still jailed in Missouri" (C: imprisoned in Liberty).
- [BYU + T1] Democratic Association: Feb 25, 1839 resolution and eight-person committee chaired by J. W. Whitney; Feb 27 report and Sidney Rigdon's address; Feb 28 resolutions condemning Missouri (G). T1 for the substance: the association publicly denounced Missouri's injustice and pledged help (A); its relief committee was to provide clothing, food and shelter for at least 5,000 Saints (B). Copy states the dates and Whitney from G, the substance from A and B.
- [T1] April 22, 1839: Joseph rejoined the Saints at Quincy (B). Ferry landing, wide-brimmed hat, upturned collar, Dimick Huntington on the riverbank, back streets, Emma "embraced him halfway to the gate" at the Cleveland home (F). "After escaping custody in Missouri": F describes the escape from the guards; G dates it April 16.
- [T1] May 1839 general conference approved land purchases and the Twelve's missions to Europe (B). [BYU] May 4 to 6 at a Presbyterian camp ground just beyond the city limits (G). May 10, 1839: Joseph and family left Quincy for Commerce (B).
- [T1] June 28, 2002: President Hinckley and the choir in Quincy (J). Choir in the area for the Nauvoo Temple dedication services (C). $75,000 raised to help the city start a community foundation (H, I); C: for the Quincy Area Community Foundation.

On the Map

- [ISHS + T1] Washington Park marker "The Mormons in Quincy," north side of Maine Street between 4th and 5th, dedicated January 1, 1976, by the Church and the Illinois State Historical Society (K). Quoted text "were made welcome by the people here" read from the page's raw HTML. J: a monument in Washington Park honors the history.
- [T1] Clat Adams Bicentennial Park, rendering of the Church's "Mercy on the Mississippi" panel unveiled May 10, 2025, for the Adams County bicentennial; to be installed at the park; background image by Julie Rogers of Saints huddled on the shore waiting to cross the icy river (I; H agrees on title, place, date).

Trivia

- [T1] Eliza R. Snow, "To the Citizens of Quincy," Quincy Whig, May 11, 1839, "Sons and Daughters of Benevolence" (A).
- [T1] Branch organized 21 June 1840; stake 25 October 1840; reduced to a branch in 1841 (B).
- [T1] As the weather warmed, skiffs, canoes or small boats until ferries opened (A). Some families waited up to two weeks for the river to thaw enough to cross (D, footnote).

- "Mercy on the Mississippi" panel: as of the May 2025 sources only a rendering had been unveiled and the panel was "to be installed." Copy says only that the rendering was unveiled for the park. No source found confirming installation. If installed, the line could say so.

## Dropped

- "Today's Quincy Illinois Stake." Not found. No Church source located for a current stake by that name. L shows Quincy's congregation was part of the Nauvoo Illinois Stake when it was organized in February 1979 (first stake president Gene Lee Roy Mann of Quincy), and the 2025 sources mention only Nauvoo stake leaders. The meetinghouse locator returned a server error. Left out; the 1840 stake is used instead.
- Wandle Mace and the Bean family cabins. Mace appears only in G (single source for that detail). The Bean journal line (H, I) is verified but cut for length.
- The 1845 Quincy committee that asked the Saints to leave Illinois (A, B). True, but late and outside the refuge story; left out to keep the section focused, not to soften it.
- Lyman Wight's letters and the First Presidency's May 17 disavowal (search summary only, G). Not fetched in detail; omitted.
- "A lasting disgrace" (Feb 28 resolution on Boggs, G). Verified in G but quoting it adds blame language; copy says "condemning Missouri's treatment of the Saints."
- Washington Park as where the Saints "reportedly camped" (J's hedge). Hedged; omitted.
- A monument "at the water's edge where the Latter-day Saints arrived" (J). Location not identified; omitted.
- First meetinghouse at 201 South 3rd Street, now a hotel (seequincy.com tourism PDF, lead only). Not tiered.
- Joseph's 1834 and earlier crossings at Quincy and the Zion's Camp contingent (B). Verified; cut for length.
- "City of Refuge" nickname (I). Verified, but it bundles other history (Underground Railroad, Potawatomi); not used.
- Hinckley asking choir members with Quincy-sheltered ancestors to stand (H, I; recalled by Charles Scholz in 2025). Verified; cut for length.
- Distances: 45 miles (A, B), "fifty miles" (Saints ch. 34), about 55 (H). Copy uses 45, "some 45 miles."
