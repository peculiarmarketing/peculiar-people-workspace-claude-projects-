Haun's Mill, Missouri

On Shoal Creek
Jacob Hawn built a gristmill on a bend of Shoal Creek in 1835. He never joined the Church, but he stayed on good terms with the Latter-day Saints who settled around him and worked at his mill.

By 1838
About a dozen cabins and a blacksmith shop stood near the mill, with more Latter-day Saint families farming nearby. Far West, the main settlement of the Saints in Caldwell County, was about 15 miles off.

What Happened Here

  • 1836: Latter-day Saints began moving from Clay County into newly created Caldwell County, and some found land and work near the mill.
  • October 28, 1838: With more families arriving from Ohio and tension rising across northern Missouri, the people at the mill made a peace agreement with the nearby militia.
  • October 30, 1838: An armed mob attacked the settlement in the afternoon. Women and children ran for the woods, and men and boys took shelter in the blacksmith shop. Seventeen Latter-day Saints were killed and more than a dozen wounded. Survivors buried the dead in an unfinished well.
  • November and December 1838: Amanda Barnes Smith lost her husband, Warren, and her son Sardius. Her son Alma was gravely wounded at the hip. She prayed for guidance, cleaned the wound with a mild lye made from fireplace ashes and dressed it with a poultice of slippery elm root. Alma lay still for five weeks and then walked. Because he could not be moved, the family stayed on near the mill through those weeks.
  • Winter of 1838 to 1839: The Saints were forced out of the area and then out of Missouri. Jacob Hawn left as well, and in 1843 he and his wife crossed the plains to Oregon.

On the Map

  • The site lies in woods and fields at the bend of Shoal Creek. The seventeen are buried here. Hawn's house and mill are gone, and there are no trails or benches.
  • Northeast Hauns Mill Drive is the gravel road in. It still carries the older spelling.

Notes

  • Church histories long spelled the name "Haun." Jacob Hawn's grave marker in Yamhill, Oregon, reads "Hawn," and newer Church publications use that spelling.
  • A cast-iron face wheel from the mill, dated 1835, lay in the creek bed until a fisherman found it in 1981. Community of Christ gave it to the Church History Museum in Salt Lake City.
  • Community of Christ sold the site to the Church in a purchase made public in May 2012. It is open every day. Heavy rain can flood the roads.
