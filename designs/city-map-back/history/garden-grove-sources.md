## Verified

Spec rows

- [BYU] Who It Was For: Orson Pratt journal, 24 April 1846, "for the benefit of the poor and such as were unable at present to pursue their journey further and also for the benefit of the poor who were yet behind" (B, p. 447 to 448).
- [T1] First permanent camp: A ("the first permanent camp established for the benefit of those to follow"); E ("the first permanent camp in Iowa").
- [T1/NPS] Halfway across Iowa: A ("about halfway across Iowa"). Weldon Fork of the Grand River: C; E ("East (Weldon) Fork of the Grand River").
- [BYU] Brigham Young and Henry G. Sherwood chose the location on 24 April 1846 (B, citing Clayton's journal and Journal History).

What Happened Here

- [T1/NPS] 24 April 1846 arrival: A (timeline and text), C ("On April 24, the lead wagons reached the Weldon Fork"; C calls them the lead wagons of Brigham Young's vanguard company).
- [BYU] Orson Pratt quote, "came to a place which we named Garden Grove" (B, quoting Pratt journal, 24 April).
- [BYU] 27 April: horn sounded, men organized for labor, "well drenched in rain" (B, Pratt journal).
- [T1] 715 acres of prairie sod broken within three weeks (A). B also gives 715 acres under cultivation (citing John R. Young's memoirs).
- [NPS/BYU] 10 May 1846 Orson Pratt journal, "the appearance of having been occupied for years" (D quotes it with the date; B quotes it too).
- [T1/BYU] Branch organized 10 May 1846, Samuel Bent president, David Fullmer and Aaron Johnson counselors (A names the three; B gives the date and roles, citing Journal History 10 May 1846 and Clayton). Bent's 12 May instructions to divide land according to need (B). Main camp left 13 May (C, "On May 13, Brigham Young and the main camp pulled out").
- [T1] Samuel Bent died at Garden Grove 16 August 1846 (A; B agrees).
- [NPS] Winter 1846 to 1847: "sheltered 500 to 600 Latter-day Saints ... as many as 70 of them did not live to see spring" (C). Single NPS source; A independently gives about 600 at Garden Grove in fall 1846. Stated plainly, no detail.
- [NPS/T1/BYU] Used until 1852 (C: "continue to occupy Garden Grove and support passing wagon trains until 1852"; D: way station 1846 to 1852; E/F: "way station until 1852"; B: abandoned 1852, "only a few families declined to go").

On the Map

- [NPS] Pioneer cemetery open to the public in a small county park; managed by the Decatur County Conservation Board with adjoining private landowners; address 30308 Mormon Trail Road (C).
- [NPS] Settlement site on nearby private farmland; present town grew up near the old camp (C, D).

Trivia

- [BYU] Hosea Stout said the farm grew so fast it seemed "a magic city of the woods" (B, citing Stout diary, Brooks ed., 1:156). Quotation marks lost in the PDF text, so the copy paraphrases without quotation marks.
- [BYU] Work list of 27 April: one hundred men to cutting trees, splitting rails and making fence; forty-eight to cutting logs for log houses; others to build a bridge, dig wells, make wood for ploughs, watch the flocks; others sent several days' journey into the Missouri settlements to exchange horses, feather beds and other property for cows and provisions (B, quoting Orson Pratt journal; Pratt's own spelling "ploughs" kept).

NPS, Church News and BYU Studies all give 1852. Used 1852; flagged.
- "Several markers identify the original settlement and the cemetery," 0.8 miles from town (Church "What to Expect When You Visit ... Nebraska and Iowa" page). Seen only in a search summary; the page now 301-redirects to the history landing page. Not used. Failed fetch, not worked around.
- Distance from Nauvoo: 128 miles (Church history site, unfetched), 144 miles (A, NPS), 145 miles (B). Conflict; omitted.
- Name origin "tall oak timber and vast field of wild onions" (C only). Single second-tier source; omitted.
- "Twelve thousand rails left over" from fencing and cabin building (Helen Mar Whitney, Woman's Exponent, 1884, via B footnote 21). One late reminiscence; omitted.
- Two farms of 1,000 and 500 acres (Heman C. Smith, RLDS Journal of History, 1909, via B). Conflicts with 715 acres; omitted.
- "Cow yard" burial lot (Church history site, search summary only; page unreadable). Omitted.
- 559 able-bodied men put to work (B; OCR text ambiguous between 359 and 559). Omitted.
- Hosea Stout's son Hyrum died at Garden Grove, 8 May 1846 (B, quoting his diary). Verified but omitted under the sensitivity rule (personal grief detail).
- Bent's death "intimations ... by three holy messengers" (counselors' letter, via B). Miraculous account; omitted.
- First settlement on Potawatomi lands in territorial Iowa (D). Verified, left out for length.
- Iowa neighbors' donations in October 1847 (B). Verified, left out for length.

## Markers (9 Oct 2026)

Frame lat 40.7911 to 40.8634, lon -93.6428 to -93.5716; merge distance 156 m; no temple. Two markers. The two points are about 1.8 km apart, so no merge.

1. Garden Grove camp / town. 40.82723, -93.60723. Coordinate source: OSM relation 129180 (Garden Grove village) centre point, via Nominatim. Certainty: approximate. Why: no source pins the 1846 campsite. [T1] Ensign, Sept 1979, "The Mormon Pioneer Trail, 1846-47" (https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1979/09/the-mormon-pioneer-trail-1846-47?lang=eng): "a community of the same name still exists on this campsite. A brass marker in a large stone in the center of town honors these early settlers." [NPS] C (current page): "the site itself is on nearby private farmland. The present town of Garden Grove, Iowa, grew up near the old Latter-day Saints camp." The two disagree on whether the town is on or beside the camp; copy names both and says the exact spot is unknown. In frame: yes.
2. Mormon pioneer cemetery, county park. 40.82750, -93.62852. Coordinate source: OSM way 860522813 "Garden Grove Mormon Burial Grounds" (Nominatim); NPS address 30308 Mormon Trail Road (C). Certainty: exact (the marked, public cemetery park itself). In frame: yes. [T1] Ensign 1979 and [NPS] Historic Resource Study ch. 7 (https://npshistory.com/publications/mopi/hrs/chap7.htm) both put it one mile west of the town marker, which matches the OSM points (about 1.8 km).

New facts used in copy: the town on or beside the camp, and the brass plaque on a large stone in the centre of town (Ensign 1979, T1; NPS HRS ch. 7 corroborates a town-park marker dedicated 1956).

Volatile: the brass plaque in the centre of town (1979 description; the NPS study's town-park marker may be the same one). Re-check that it still stands before reprint.

Considered and left out:
- The 1846 camp as its own point on farmland: no source gives a position. Folded into marker 1 as approximate.
- West bank of the East (Weldon) Fork as the camp position (Ensign 1979): a bank, not a spot; not enough to place a marker.
- Overpass API (OSM) requests failed with connection resets through the proxy; not retried beyond one attempt per endpoint. Nominatim used instead.
