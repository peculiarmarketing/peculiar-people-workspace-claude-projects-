## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] The Whitmers and Where They Farmed (split into two rows 9 Oct): "members of a large German-speaking community that had migrated from central Europe to Pennsylvania"; moved to Fayette "in the early 1800s" (A). Township "located in northern part of county between Seneca and Cayuga lakes" (E). Whitmer home "about 30 miles from Palmyra" (I).
- (Removed 9 Oct, Evan: "skip it if you aren't sure".) The spec row "In Fayette or Manchester?" is gone from the copy; the April 6 bullet states the organization without naming the house or town. Record kept:  D&C 21 heading, "in the home of Peter Whitmer Sr." at Fayette (D). E: "Church officially organized in Fayette, 6 Apr. 1830." C: "Surviving firsthand accounts from attendees contradict one another on the location of the founding meeting, some reporting Fayette and others Manchester"; 1839 history mentioned Fayette; 1842 article mentioned Manchester; "there is some question regarding the specific location in western New York." L gives more of the record (Evening and Morning Star 1833 and Orson Pratt 1840 said Manchester; David Whitmer 1887 said his father's house in Fayette). Both positions are Church sources, so per the brief both are stated.

What Happened Here

- [T1] June 1829: Joseph and Oliver lived with the Whitmers to complete the translation; Emma joined them (A, B; D&C 14 heading: residence at the Whitmer home "until the work of translation was carried to completion"). D&C 17 heading: Fayette, June 1829, to Cowdery, Whitmer and Harris "prior to their viewing the engraved plates." Mary Whitmer: "the angel Moroni showed them to her during a private visitation" (A).
- [T1] 6 April 1830, a Tuesday (C). "Six men, who had previously been baptized, participated"; Joseph ordained Oliver an elder and Oliver ordained Joseph (D&C 21 heading; M; C). Sacrament (C, M). "Some of those present were baptized in nearby Seneca Lake" (A). L: "several were baptized that day."
- [T1] 9 June 1830 first conference at Peter Whitmer Sr.'s home in Fayette; three men ordained; licenses issued; Articles and Covenants endorsed (F).
- [T1] D&C 37, "near Fayette, New York, December 1830," the first commandment concerning a gathering; Saints called to gather at the Ohio (D). Whitmers sold the farm "the following April" (A).
- [T1] May 1831: Lucy Mack Smith "Led company of approximately eighty Fayette, Seneca Co., branch members from Seneca Co. to Kirtland, Geauga Co., Ohio, May 1831" (G).
- [T1] 6 April 1980: President Spencer W. Kimball spoke from the reconstructed Whitmer farmhouse during the general conference session carried by satellite, and dedicated it; original house "no longer standing" (J, K). 150th anniversary (J, K).

On the Map

- [T1] Whitmer farm at 1451 Aunkst Road, Waterloo, NY 13165; reconstructed log home and visitors' center (A, B).
- [T1] Seneca Lake baptisms (A).
- [T1] Lucy's group began "near present-day Waterloo, New York," on the Cayuga-Seneca Canal; "After about 20 miles, that canal joined the Erie Canal at Montezuma"; "Remnants of the stones that lined those canals can still be seen" (H).

Trivia

- [T1] "Joseph Smith began translating the Bible in the township" and "first known ministerial licenses were issued in the township" (E).
- [T1] "Population in 1830 about 3,200." "Population in 1840 about 3,700." (E)
