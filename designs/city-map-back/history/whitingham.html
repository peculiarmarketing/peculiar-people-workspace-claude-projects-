Whitingham, Vermont

Born Here
Brigham Young, on June 1, 1801. He was the ninth child of John and Abigail Howe Young, who had moved the family up from Hopkinton, Massachusetts, that January.

How Long They Stayed
About three years. Brigham was not quite three when the Youngs left for New York, and he grew up there.

On Town Hill
A 12-foot monument of light Barre granite stands in the town park. Its inscription says it was "erected by descendants of Brigham Young in cooperation with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints."

What Happened Here

  • January 1801: John and Abigail, called Nabby, brought their children to Whitingham. They were devout Methodists.
  • 1804: The family moved on to Sherburne, in Chenango County, New York.
  • May 28, 1950: President George Albert Smith dedicated the monument on Town Hill before a crowd the Church News put at about 1,000. Four days later, in Washington, he dedicated the Brigham Young statue in the U.S. Capitol, carved by his grandson Mahonri Young.
  • June 1, 2001: For the 200th birthday, about 180 people from around New England met at the small Whitingham Municipal Center in Jacksonville.

On the Map

  • The 1950 monument is in the park on Town Hill. Four of Brigham's grandsons drew the original sketch for it.
  • Jacksonville, the village in the northeast part of town, is home to the Whitingham Municipal Center, where the 2001 birthday program was held.

Trivia

  • The Whitingham circuit was one of the first three Methodist circuits organized in Vermont.
  • A roadside marker on Route 8, described in the Friend in 1971, pointed visitors "southward up the steep hill" to the birthplace.
  • Colonel Nathan Whiting petitioned New York for a township "by the Name of Whitingham," and the patent came through on March 12, 1770. The hill Brigham was born on has its own name now: Brigham's Hill.
