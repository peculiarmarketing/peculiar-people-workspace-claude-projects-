## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] The Township: settled from 1785; formed from Windsor Township April 1821; about 2,400 people in 1830; villages Harpursville, Nineveh and Colesville; Susquehanna River ran through the eastern portion. A.
- [T1] Joseph Knight Sr.: farmer and miller in Colesville (G; F adds grain dealer). Moved to Windsor (later in Colesville), Broome County, 1811 (H). Hired Joseph Smith as a day laborer in 1826 (F, G). Provided food and writing materials during the translation (D); "money, food, and other resources" (F).
- [T1] The Branch: "the nucleus of one of the first (if not the first) branches organized in the Church" (D). Copy says "one of the Church's first branches, possibly the very first." Hyrum Smith stayed in the area in late 1830 and presided for several months; successor Newel Knight (D). A: Hyrum presided five months from October 1830.

What Happened Here

- [T1] April 1830: after the Fayette baptisms following the organization, Joseph stopped at Joseph and Polly Knight's farm, stayed a few days preaching to the Knights and their friends (B). Newel prayed alone in the woods, came home with his face and limbs contorting; Joseph said "In the name of Jesus Christ, depart from him"; contortions stopped; Joseph told them it was the first miracle performed in the church (B). C: JS visited the Knights near Colesville in April 1830.
- [T1] June 28, 1830: dam built on a nearby stream; wrecked overnight; Sabbath meeting; believers returned early the next day and repaired it; Oliver baptized Emma, Joseph and Polly Knight, and ten others (B). Date June 28, 1830 for Emma's baptism by Oliver Cowdery in Colesville (J, K). Men on the bank heckled, followed the group back to the Knight farm, swelled to a mob of fifty (B). C: about thirteen baptized in late June; opponents destroyed the dam.
- [T1] June 30, 1830: Constable Ebenezer Hatch arrested JS at the Knight home on the evening of 28 June; Chenango County trial 29 June; a Broome County constable arrested him again during the night of 29 to 30 June and held him at a Colesville tavern; trial 30 June before Justice Joel K. Noble, with at least two other justices according to Reed; the court discharged him (C). B confirms tried, acquitted, arrested and tried again, released.
- [T1] April 1831: December 1830 and January 1831 revelations (D&C 37, 38) to move to Ohio; poor not to be left behind; branch left under Newel Knight in April 1831, arrived May; sent to Thompson on Leman Copley's 759-acre farm (D). Newel sold what he could; Saints worked so the poor could make the journey (M). Partridge sent the Colesville Saints to Copley's farm (E). Copley broke the agreement and evicted them (D, E, I D&C 54 heading).
- [T1] July 26, 1831: "sixty members of the branch reached Kaw Township in Jackson County, Missouri, on July 26 after a journey of about a thousand miles" from Thompson (D). August 2: "Joseph and twelve men from the Colesville Branch ... laid the first log" of the first house in Zion (E). Polly Knight died a few days later, the first Saint buried in Zion (E); D&C 59 heading: died the day the revelation was received, August 7, 1831, "the first Church member to die in Zion" (I). Copy says "Within the week."

On the Map

- [T1] Joseph Knight Sr. Home: not owned or operated by the Church; docents give guided tours; hours via colesville-restoration.org (F). Copy avoids claims about which parts of the house are original.
- [T1] June 1830 baptisms "in a dammed stream running through or close to the Joseph Knight farm" (J, photo caption). Stream not named in any Church source; no coordinates claimed.
- [T1] Joseph worked for Knight in 1826 to 1827, "likely near Nineveh" (A). Copy: "probably."

Trivia

- [T1] Knight "considered Joseph Smith 'the best hand he ever hired'" (F).
- [T1] Joseph borrowed the Knights' sleigh "when snow made it hard to travel to the Hales' house," winter 1826 to 1827 (L).
- [T1] D&C 128:20, quoted on fetch: "The voice of Peter, James, and John in the wilderness between Harmony, Susquehanna county, and Colesville, Broome county, on the Susquehanna river." Called "an 1842 letter to Latter-day Saints" (N). Snow, Ensign Sept 2015 (see Harmony ledger): "We know little about this most sacred event other than the reference in scripture to the general location."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Knight home tours and operator: private group, hours change. Copy says only that the Church does not own or run it and docents give tours.

## Dropped

- "First branch of the Church" as a flat superlative. The Church's history map 3 (study helps) says the first branch was organized at the Knight home, but D (Revelations in Context) hedges "one of the first (if not the first)." Copy uses the hedge.
- Branch organized at a meeting in the Knight home after April 1830 (F). No date given; left out rather than guess.
- 82 members of the branch (Meridian via historyofmormonism.com, lead only). D gives sixty for the Missouri arrival; only that is used.
- Polly Knight's exact death date: D&C 59 heading implies August 7; secondary sources (Lyndon Cook via blogs) give August 6; Saints says "a few days later." Copy says "Within the week."
- Departure from Thompson: D says early June 1831, but D&C 54 is dated June 10 and E says about two weeks after the revelation. Not dated in copy.
- Nathaniel Cole Tavern in Harpursville as the site of the June 30 trial (BYU Journeys: "probable location"; foundation remnants only). Not confirmed by a Church source; C says only "a Colesville tavern." Not used as a landmark.
- Pickerel Pond (photo on A). No event tied to it.
- Hyrum Smith's five months (A) versus "several months" (D). Copy says several.
- Joseph Knight Sr. retrieving-the-plates night (Manchester, 1827) and D&C 12 (received at Harmony, May 1829). Verified (F, G, D&C 12 heading) but they happened outside Colesville.
- The escape from the constable and later threats (BYU Journeys and others). Not needed; no Church source read for details.
- Sally Knight and Emma confirmed together later that summer at Harmony (B). Harmony event; left out of both.
