## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Mill built in 1835 by Jacob Hawn on Shoal Creek (D). Mill on "a bend in Shoal Creek" (A). Hawn not a member, on friendly terms with Latter-day Saint neighbors and mill workers (A, D).
- [T1] By 1838: about a dozen cabins and a blacksmith shop; about 75 other Latter-day Saint families in the area (F). "More families farming nearby" paraphrases F's 75 families in the area.
- [T1] Far West about 15 miles away (D, in the context of militia captain John Killian's advice). B: "about a day's walk."

What Happened Here

- [T1] 1836: Saints move from Clay County into newly created Caldwell County; some found work and land near Hawn's property (A).
- [T1] October 28, 1838: residents made a peace agreement with the nearby militia (D). Hundreds of members arriving from Kirtland in 1838 as tensions grew (D). C also mentions a truce David Evans made with neighbors.
- [T1] October 30, 1838, afternoon (C: "The afternoon of October 30, 1838"). Women and children fled to the woods; men and boys sheltered in the blacksmith shop (B, D). Seventeen killed (A, B, C, F, G). "More than a dozen wounded" (C); wounded counts vary: 14 (A, H), 12 to 15 (B), 13 (G), 15 (D, F), so copy uses C's wording. Survivors buried the dead in an unfinished well (D); A: "a mass grave at the bottom of a dry well"; F: buried in a well.
- [T1] Amanda Barnes Smith: husband Warren and son Sardius killed (C, E). Alma wounded at the hip (C, D, E). Lye from fireplace ashes and slippery elm poultice (C, D: "a mild lye solution from fireplace ashes," "poultices from slippery elm roots"). Alma "lay still for five weeks, then walked" (D). Family stayed because Alma could not be moved (D). Copy dates this bullet "November and December 1838" as the five weeks following October 30.
- [T1] Saints forced to leave the area and eventually the state that winter (A). Hawn also left Missouri and eventually moved to Oregon (A); crossed the plains to Oregon with his wife in 1843 (F).

On the Map

- [T1] Woods and fields near the creek bend; final resting place of the 17; Hawn's house and mill no longer stand; no trails or paths from the parking lot and no benches (A).
- [T1] Address NE Hauns Mill Dr, Braymer (A). Gravel street (A, accessibility section). "Still carries the older spelling" is a plain observation from the street name.

Notes

- [T1] "Haun" long used in Church histories; grave markers of Jacob and Harriett Hawn in Yamhill, Oregon, read "Hawn" (F). Recent scholarship prefers "Hawn" (D); new edition of the scriptures uses "Hawn" on a Church history map (F).
- [T1] Cast-iron face wheel dated 1835, stayed in the creek bed until a local fisherman found it in 1981; gift of Community of Christ to the Church History Museum (D).
- [T1] Church acquired the Haun's Mill historic site from Community of Christ; Church spokesman's statement May 5, 2012; article May 11, 2012 calls the purchase "recent" and gives no closing date (G). Copy says "a purchase made public in May 2012." Open every day (A, hours 12:00 AM to 11:59 PM daily). Heavy rains can flood the roads and make them impassable (A).

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Open hours, no trails or benches, road flooding note (A, updated 25 Sept 2025). Any site development would change "no trails or benches."

## Dropped

- Death toll breakdown. A and H: 14 men and 3 boys. D: 15 men and 2 boys. All agree on 17, so copy gives only the total.
- Wounded count (12 to 15 across Church sources). Copy uses Saints' "more than a dozen."
- Graphic detail of the attack (firing through the gaps in the log walls, boys pulled from under the bellows, the wound described as the hip joint "entirely gone" or "shot away"). In B, C and D, but left out under the sensitivity rule. Copy says "gravely wounded at the hip."
- Number of attackers. G: about 240 armed men. Not in A, B, C; left out.
- Who led the attackers (Livingston County militia, Nehemiah Comstock and others; leads only). Church sources say "a neighboring county" (A) or "rogue militiamen" (B). No names in copy, per no-blame rule.
- The extermination order connection. B: three days after the order; some evidence the attackers did not know of it. F (Baugh): planned days in advance, not caused by the order. Interpretive and contested; left out.
- Joseph Smith's counsel to gather at Far West and whether the attack could have been avoided (B, D, F). Interpretive; left out.
- Isaac Laney and John Walker survival accounts, Willard Smith helping six girls escape (D, F). Verified but read as miracle stories or added drama; left out for length and tone.
- Amanda praying in the cornfield after being forbidden to pray aloud (D, F). Verified; cut for length.
- Alma's age: 6 (C) or 7 (B). Left out.
- Number of families: 15 to 20 nearby families vs 30 to 40 gathered at the mill (D); "a few families" (F). Not stated as a number.
- Mill torn down in 1845 (D). Single source; left out (A only says the mill no longer stands).
- George Edward Anderson's 1907 painted inscription on a millstone, now the Breckenridge monument owned by the town (H). Verified, but the monument sits outside the frame; left out.
- 2003 Community of Christ marker, now reported missing (HMDB, lead only). Not tiered; omitted. No marker wording is quoted in the copy because no Church source gives one.
- Haun's Mill Monument / "Haun's Mill" spelling on maps. No claim made about current on-site signage.
