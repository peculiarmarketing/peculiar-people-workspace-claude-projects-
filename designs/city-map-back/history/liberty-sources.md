## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Clay County Seat: Liberty settled 1820; Clay County seat 1822; after expulsion from Jackson County in 1833 many Latter-day Saints found refuge in Clay County (E, JSP place gloss for Liberty).
- [T1] The Prisoners: Joseph Smith, Hyrum Smith, Sidney Rigdon, Lyman Wight, Caleb Baldwin, Alexander McRae; imprisoned December 1, 1838 (A, B); held until April 6, 1839 (B; A gives April 6 as the day they were escorted out); awaiting trial on treason (C, ch. 31); "just over four months" (E, JSP jail gloss).
- [T1] In Joseph's Words: quoted from A, which cites Joseph Smith to Isaac Galland, March 22, 1839 (Times and Seasons, Feb. 1840). Opening clause and ellipsis as A gives them.

What Happened Here

- [T1] December 1, 1838: all-day wagon journey; through the center of town, past the courthouse, north to the small stone jail; curious crowd; Joseph last off the wagon, raised his hat to the crowd, then descended into the prison (C, ch. 31).
- [T1] February 1839: Mary Fielding Smith and her sister visited with the infant Joseph F. Smith, born in November (C, ch. 32); "the first time Hyrum had met his son" (A). Sidney Rigdon released on bail in February (A); "in early February 1839 because of illness" (B); C ch. 33 says "gravely ill." Emma visited three times, once with Joseph III (A).
- [T1] Letters from Emma, Don Carlos and Bishop Partridge arrived the evening of March 19; Joseph began the epistles the day after (C, ch. 33). First dated March 20, second around March 22; portions are D&C 121 to 123 (D, A, B). D&C 121:1 "O God, where art thou?" (D, verse text fetched). March 20 letter: handwriting of Alexander McRae and Caleb Baldwin, with insertions by JS; seventeen pages (E).
- [T1] April 6, 1839: armed guard escorted them from the jail (A); grand jury in Gallatin (C, ch. 33); change of venue; guards allowed the escape en route to Boone County; they reached Illinois (A).

On the Map

- [T1] Historic Liberty Jail: building declared unsafe in 1856 and fell into disrepair (A; B says a new courthouse of 1856 replaced it). Wilford C. Wood purchased the site June 19, 1939, and transferred title to the Church for the same cost (A). Visitors' center (groundbreaking 1962) houses a reconstruction (A); "full-scale model of the jail"; tour can descend stairs in the rotunda to look closer at the jail (B). Dedicated September 15, 1963, by President Joseph Fielding Smith (A). Address 216 N Main St (OSM). Copy says "Joseph Fielding Smith" without a title (in 1963 he was President of the Quorum of the Twelve, not Church President; title omitted to avoid confusion).
- [T1] Site of Alexander W. Doniphan's 1830s home, "just a few hundred feet south of the Historic Liberty Jail"; marker dedicated April 14, 2002; Doniphan "defended the Church leaders in court when they were unjustly imprisoned" (H). Lawyer (E, JSP bio gloss on B-type pages; H).
- [T1] Eight Witnesses monument on Campbell Road/Drive south of Liberty on the former Michael Arthur farm, near the graves of Christian and Peter Whitmer Jr. (F, G); Arthur farm a gathering place for Church leaders; first Missouri Presidency organized there in July 1834 (G); "1834-36" council meetings (F). Dedicated November 19, 2011 (F, G).

Trivia

- [T1] $4,000 purchase, title transferred to the Church "for the same cost" (A).
- [T1] Jail dimensions: "14 x 14½ foot ... two-story structure ... rough-hewn limestone exterior walls two feet thick and interior walls made of oak. Loose rocks filled the one-foot void between the two walls to discourage escape attempts." Lower section reached only through a trapdoor from the upper floor. "Two narrow windows, 2 feet wide and 6 inches high, with iron bars" (A). B corroborates two rooms stacked, two small barred windows, four-foot-thick walls.

## Dropped

- Ceiling height. A says it is "sometimes asserted that the prisoners could not stand upright"; historians measured the ceiling at 6½ feet in 1888. Verified and a good myth correction, but Salt Lake already ends on a myth correction and the last item here had to be a construction fact; cut for length. Evan may want it back.
- Wall thickness wording. A gives the layers (2 ft limestone, 1 ft rubble, oak); B and C say "four feet thick." Copy gives A's layers and no total.
- Build date. A: built 1833. B: built between 1830 and 1833. JSP gloss: constructed 1833. Copy gives no build date.
- Dedicator of the 1963 visitors' center. B says "President Joseph F. Smith," which cannot be right (he died in 1918); A says President Joseph Fielding Smith. Used A.
- Escape attempts ("at least two occasions," A). Verified; left out for length.
- Rigdon's release month. C (ch. 33) frames the appeal as January with only Rigdon released; A and B say February. Copy says February ("that same month").
- Mary Fielding Smith's visit and whether Hyrum blessed the baby in the jail (A cites Baugh asking the question). Copy says only that he saw his son for the first time.
- Joseph's other jail quotes ("Hell may pour forth its rage like the burning lava of Mount Vesuvius", C). Verified; cut so the section does not end reverent.
- Joseph F. Smith as the infant's later role as Church President. Well known, left implicit.
- Hecklers at the windows, cornbread, matted straw (C). Hardship covered by Joseph's own words.
- Gallatin details (drinking lawyers, wrestling match) (C). Cut for length; the wrestling match would read light next to the imprisonment.
- Wilford Woodruff making bricks on the Arthur farm and Zion's Camp being discharged there (F, G). Verified; cut for length.
- Doniphan's grave in Fairview Cemetery, Liberty (39.24249, -94.42420, inside). F shows a photo of his grave marker "at cemetery in Liberty" without naming it; Fairview name from leads only. Not listed.
- Distance of the Eight Witnesses monument: F says about 3½ miles south of Liberty; the OSM address point is closer to 2½ to 3 miles southwest. Copy says "south of town."
