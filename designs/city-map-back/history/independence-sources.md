## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Center Place: D&C 57 heading dates it "in Zion, Jackson County, Missouri, July 20, 1831" (J). Quotation is D&C 57:3, copied from the fetched text (J); verse 2 has "the place for the city of Zion." A, B call it the "center place."
- [T1] Buying In: "On December 19, 1831, Bishop Edward Partridge purchased 63.27 acres from the Flournoys," including the area dedicated for a temple (D); Jones H. and Clara Flournoy (D). "When the Lord revealed the location of the lot, the land was west of the city" (D). "Between 1831 and 1833, the Church purchased 180 acres in Independence and 1,200 acres in surrounding areas of Jackson County, and some 1,200 Saints gathered to the county" (A).
- [T1] After 1833: Saints refused to sell, hoped to reclaim land with help of state or federal governments; "When their attempts to recover their property failed" they built elsewhere (A). "In 1904, the Church purchased 25 acres of land in Independence, Missouri. A portion of this land was part of the original 63 acres purchased by Edward Partridge" (F).

What Happened Here

- [T1] August 3, 1831: eight elders (named in D) assembled where the temple was to be; Rigdon dedicated the ground; Joseph Smith laid a stone at the northeast corner of the contemplated temple (D quoting John Whitmer's history; E). Plot "west of the courthouse"; someone read from the eighty-seventh psalm (I, ch. 12). August 2: Rigdon dedicated the land of Zion during a ceremony in Kaw Township, 12 miles west of Independence (M); Saints says "several miles west" and that Joseph and twelve Colesville men laid the first log of a building there (I, ch. 12). A dates the land dedication August 2 and the temple site August 3.
- [T1] June 1832: Phelps bought a press in Cincinnati and took it to Missouri; first issue of The Evening and the Morning Star June 1832; "The only religious newspaper in Jackson County" (G). JSP: "Phelps acquired a printing press and type in Cincinnati" (K).
- [T1] June 1833 plat: First Presidency in Kirtland sent the plat in June 1833 (A); 24 temples in the center of the city, each to bear "Holiness to the Lord" (D).
- [T1] July 20, 1833: vigilantes demanded the Saints leave; when leaders refused they attacked the printing office, "throwing the press out the window, scattering the type in the street, and tearing down the walls"; Partridge and Charles Allen tarred and feathered on the public square (B). Second-floor window, press heaved out (I, ch. 16). Mary Elizabeth Rollins, 15, and Caroline, 13, gathered pages of the Book of Commandments and hid in a cornfield (I, ch. 16; G).
- [T1] November 1833: violence resumed October 31; November 4 near the Big Blue River "three people were killed, one of whom was a member of the Church"; next day the militia forced "nearly 150 Church members to surrender their weapons"; most crossed the Missouri River into Clay County (B; ferrying across the Missouri, I ch. 17).

On the Map

- [T1] Temple lot about 300 by 200 feet; grass, a few trees, six small stone markers and a sign; "one of the highest points in the area" (D). Owned by the Church of Christ, "once known as the Church of Christ (Temple Lot)," whose meetinghouse is next to the lot (D, E). "Across the street from Community of Christ Temple" (E). Headquarters: D lists "the headquarters for an international church" among the buildings operated by the three churches around the lot. Copy keeps one neutral line, as the brief asks.
- [T1] Visitors' center: the lot "lies immediately to the northwest" of it (D), so the center is southeast of the lot. Groundbreaking June 1, 1966; N. Eldon Tanner dedicated it in 1971 (F).
- [T1] Printing office site: "a bronze plaque marks the location"; building no longer exists (G). Book of Commandments "almost finished" (I, ch. 16); "nearly complete in July 1833" (C).
- [T1] Gilbert and Whitney store: "close to the town square"; "two-story, redbrick building"; "also served as the bishop's storehouse"; "In July 1833, a mob ransacked Gilbert's store" (H). Copy says "stood" since H does not say whether the original building survives.

Trivia

- [T1] "The courthouse mentioned in this revelation no longer exists. It was replaced in 1836 by the building now known as the Truman Courthouse" (D).
- [T1] Conference decided on 10,000 copies (K, citing Minute Book 2, 1 Nov. 1831; I ch. 13). Literary Firm cut the run to 3,000 in April 1832, "probably based on the amount of paper that had been purchased" (K, citing Minute Book 2, 30 Apr. 1832). "Fewer than three dozen copies of the original 1833 Book of Commandments are known to exist" (C, note 3).
- [T1] Brevier type: "the Book of Commandments was printed in brevier size"; brevier "about 8 point" (K, note 25). Page size "approximately 4½ × 3⅛ inches" (K). Copy says "roughly 4½ by 3 inches."

## Dropped

- The 1833 "trail of blood" from children's feet cut by prairie grass (B). Verified but graphic; the brief bars graphic detail.
- Whippings, the bounty on Church leaders, and William McLellin's hiding (I, ch. 17). Graphic or not essential.
- The editorial on free Black converts as the trigger for July 1833 (B, I ch. 16). Verified, but the copy keeps to what happened rather than why, to stay inside the brief's no-blame line. Evan may expect to see it; it can go back in as one plain sentence from B if wanted.
- D&C 101 explanation of the expulsion ("jarrings, and contentions...") (B, N). Doctrinal interpretation; outside the brand voice.
- Meteor shower of November 13, 1833 seen from the Clay County camps (I, ch. 17). Happened in Clay County, off the map.
- Polly Knight, first Saint buried in Zion (I, ch. 12). Verified; cut for length and because her burial site is not located.
- Flournoy House (moved four blocks in 1989, now on Community of Christ's Heritage Plaza; Church location page). Inside the frame (Church pin 39.09118, -94.42902) but adds a second other-church site; left out.
- Zion's Camp, 1834. Happened outside the frame.
- Printing office dedicated by Partridge on 29 May 1832, Lot 76 on Liberty Street. Seen only in a JSP glossary pop-up fragment inside another page, not on a page read for that fact. Omitted.
- Phelps's claim that 5,000 pounds of type were lost, "about five times the amount normally on hand" (K). Verified; held in reserve as an alternate last trivia item.
- Latter-day Saint population of the town of Independence itself, about 200 by summer 1833 (L, citing a secondary atlas). Not needed; A's county figure of about 1,200 used.
- Exact acreage of the Church's land today. No T1 figure found.
- Courthouse "on the square" in 1831. D only says the original courthouse no longer exists; copy does not place it.
- Names of the eight elders at the August 3 dedication. Verified (D) but cut for length.
