## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] How It Ended: rescuers brought the Martin company into Salt Lake City November 30 (A, B, G). "Out of 576 people in the Martin Company, at least 145 died" (G).
- [T1] Who Sheltered Here: November 4 to 9, 1856, about 500 emigrants from the British Isles; "cold wind and snow made it too dangerous" (A). Hunt and Hodgett(s) wagon companies also sheltered at the cove while awaiting rescue (A, E); B places them at Fort Seminoe in those days, so copy says "nearby." Spelling Hodgetts from B.
- [T1] The Ground: "Although the cove is on Bureau of Land Management land, its only access was across private land"; Church purchased the easement to the cove and to Devil's Gate; deal reached "last summer" (H, dated April 1997, so summer 1996); agreed to buy the whole ranch later (H). I: Church bought about a third of the ranch's 12,000 deeded acres; visitors' center is the remodeled ranch headquarters. K (2006): "BLM land" managed by the Church alongside "Church-owned sites."

What Happened Here

- [T1] October 5, 1856 Brigham Young's plea at Sunday services; October 7 George D. Grant left with the first relief wagons (B; E agrees on the two days).
- [T1] October 19 Martin and Hodgetts companies crossed the Platte for the last time in the first winter storm; October 25 Martin rations cut to 8 ounces for adults and 4 for children (B).
- [T1] November 4: left Devil's Gate in the morning, about two miles to the Sweetwater, crossing in the afternoon; Jaques: about two feet deep, ice three or four inches thick, thirty to forty yards wide; women and children carried first; wagons carried the sick; able-bodied men waded pulling their handcarts; cove about half a mile beyond the river (C). B: crossed with rescuers' help and camped in the cove.
- [T1] November 9: emptied wagons left Fort Seminoe for the cove to pick up Martin members; most handcarts abandoned (B). Hodgetts began unloading November 4, Hunt November 6 (B). Wagon companies emptied wagons for the sick and infirm (A, F).
- [T1] August 15, 1992 Hinckley dedicated the Martin's Cove monument (H; F: monument dedicated 1992). May 3, 1997 Hinckley dedicated the Mormon Handcart Visitors Center at the Sun Ranch (I; L gives May 3).

On the Map

- [T1] Elevation 6,100 feet (H). Handcart for the first 1 1/4 miles; round trip to the upper reaches 5 miles (F). Trek reenactments in summer (A).
- [T1] Devil's Gate: geological formation made by the Sweetwater River, on the National Register of Historic Places; path from the Martin's Cove trail, 1 mile round trip (F).
- [T1] Fort Seminoe: reconstructed abandoned trading post where the Hunt and Hodgetts companies left belongings (F). "Close by the visitors' center" from OSM coordinates (about 150 m apart).
- [T1] Rusty Bowers, three life-size bronzes, "at the base of Martin's Cove, on a rise just off the trail adjacent to the river," unveiled November 4, 2006, the 150th anniversary (J).

Trivia

- [T1] Myth check: Solomon F. Kimball's 1914 Improvement Era account of "three eighteen-year-old boys" carrying nearly the whole company. Orton: "more than three rescuers braved the icy water"; eighteen positively identified at the crossing (plus Ira Nebeker from a biography); ages Huntington 24, Taylor 20, Nebeker and Kimball 17, Grant 16; the rescuers "carried only a portion of the company across"; wagons carried many and some waded (C). D footnote 2: the popular account "appears to have been embellished," citing Orton (2006).
- [T1] Salt Lake City about 330 miles from the cove (A). Rescuers traveled about 380 miles to reach the Martin company and the wagon companies (E). November 9 departure to November 30 arrival is three weeks (B, A).

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Land status. A 25-year BLM lease (2004 to 2029, 933 acres, per WyoFile and Deseret News, leads only) and a land-trade bill being drafted in July 2026 (WyoFile, 11 July 2026, lead only) could change "the cove itself is Bureau of Land Management land." Re-check before every reprint.
- Handcart loan distance, trail length, trek reenactments: visitor-page details (F, A).

## Dropped

- Lease terms, the 2002 House land-transfer bill, the ACLU suit and its 2006 settlement, and the 2026 trade proposal. Verified only in part (K, Church News 2002 via search) and in flux. The copy states only what H, I and K say.
- Kimball's quoted Brigham Young promise of salvation for the three rescuers. Orton calls the statement unclear; per the brief, no light or reverent close. Omitted.
- Death toll at the cove itself. No Church source gives one. WyoHistory's 25% Martin death rate and the 56 deaths near Red Buttes are lead-only.
- The 1933 Martin's Cove marker by the Utah Pioneer Trails and Landmarks Association (F). Verified, but no coordinates found to confirm it is in frame.
- Crowd at the 1997 dedication: a search summary said 5,000, the fetched article (I) says about 10,000. Omitted.
- George Grant carrying "about 150" people (Rogerson, doubted by Orton). Omitted.
- Temperature figures (Harvey Cluff, 10 to 20 below zero) and snow depth. Single-witness recollections; cut for length.
- Total rescuers (363 in the biographical database, maybe over 400) and 200-plus wagons (E). Verified; cut to keep Trivia to two items.
- Weather in Salt Lake City when the call went out (mid-70s, no snow, E). Verified; cut for length.
- Sun Ranch National Historic Landmark and the Peoples of the Sweetwater Museum (F). Verified, left out to keep On the Map at three strong items.
