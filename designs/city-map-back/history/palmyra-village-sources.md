## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Canal Town: Smiths arrived 1816; village "small, with a population of about six hundred" and "on the projected route of the Erie Canal" (H). Canal completed 1825; Palmyra "quickly transformed into a trading center" (B). Village incorporated March 1827, "two years after completion of adjacent Erie Canal" (G, village page).
- [T1] The Printer: Grandin born 30 Mar. 1806 (E, JSP person card); "a printer in Palmyra who was the same age as Joseph" (C). Copy says "about the same age" since Joseph was born December 1805. Grandin bought the Wayne Sentinel in 1827; moved printing and bookselling into Thayer and Grandin Row by November 1828, occupying the westernmost bay: bookstore first floor, bindery second, printshop top floor (D). Main Street location: D gives 217 East Main Street; J calls the row a building in the business center.
- [T1] The First Edition: 5,000 copies "more than twice the average size for a book at that time" (A). "Grandin and a dozen men seven months to print and bind the first copies of the 590-page work" (C). D: "Over a seven-month process."

What Happened Here

- [T1] 1817: family first settled in Palmyra village and rented a house for two years, living on day labor and a small shop (B). JSP: family lived in village briefly, beginning 1817 (G, village). Joseph Sr., Alvin and Hyrum as day laborers; Lucy painted oilcloth coverings for tables and chests while the men saved for land (H).
- [T1] June 1829: Grandin declined at once, believing the book a fraud; Joseph and Martin found a willing printer in a nearby city, returned and asked again (C). Rochester printer Elihu Marshall agreed (A). "In early June 1829 ... agreed to print 5,000 copies of the Book of Mormon for $3,000" (D); $3,000 to print and bind 5,000 (C). Copyright June 11, 1829 (F, A) was checked but cut from copy for length.
- [T1] August 25, 1829: indenture Harris to Grandin, Wayne Co. mortgage records (E, A: "In August 1829, Harris signed over the mortgage to Grandin"). Farm "just north of village" (G, township page). JS-H 1:61 calls Harris "a resident of Palmyra township." Gilbert recalled Grandin would not begin work or buy the type until "after Harris had promised to insure the payment" (E, n6); copy attributes this to the compositor's later account.
- [T1] Autumn 1829: Hyrum carried printer's-manuscript pages to compositor John Gilbert in installments, "sometimes hiding the pages under his buttoned vest"; manuscript had "virtually no punctuation," Gilbert inserted it (A). Printing probably began September (E, n on type purchase; Cowdery letter 6 Nov 1829 says work well under way). Copy dates it "Autumn 1829."
- [T1] January 1830: Abner Cole began printing pages of the Book of Mormon in the Palmyra Reflector in January 1830 using Grandin's press (E). Cole worked at night in the shop; "I have hired the press"; Joseph came from Harmony, found him at the printing office, "the right of publishing it belong[s] to me"; Cole "stopped printing excerpts" (C).
- [T1] March 26, 1830: first copies available for sale on the ground floor of Grandin's printing office, bound in brown calfskin, "Book of Mormon" in gold letters on the spine (C; some also in sheepskin, C n). Sale date also D, J, I.

On the Map (frame lat 43.0419 to 43.0781, lon -77.2485 to -77.2116)

- Grandin building, 217 East Main Street (D's listed address; OSM tags the bookstore door at 219), 43.06386, -77.23056. Inside. [T1] Donated to the Church in 1978; renovation about 2 1/2 years; previously a law office, millinery, tailor and tin shops, hardware store, variety store; dedicated by President Hinckley Thursday evening 26 March 1998, the anniversary of the 1830 first printing (I). J corroborates 2 1/2 years and the March 26 date.
- Erie Canal (Erie Canalway Trail point in the village), 43.06598, -77.23397. Inside. Main Street at the Grandin building is about 43.0639; the canal channel runs about 43.065 to 43.0666 through the village, roughly 200 to 300 m north. Copy says "a few hundred yards north of Main Street." This is a present-day map fact (OSM), not a history claim. The present channel is the enlarged canal; copy does not claim it is the 1825 line.
- Alvin Smith grave, 127 Church St (K's listed address), 43.06457, -77.23366; OSM "Swift Burying Ground" 43.0649, -77.2340. Inside. [T1] Alvin died November 1823 at 25; oldest surviving child (K). Copy says "oldest brother."
- Not on the map: Martin Harris Farm (OSM 43.08473, -77.23962) is north of the village frame (lat max 43.0781). Mentioned in copy only as "his farm north of the village," not listed.

Trivia

- [T1] Thomas Marsh found Martin at Grandin's printshop; "The printer gave him sixteen pages," taken back to Boston. Solomon Chamberlin (Saints spelling) got "sixty-four printed pages" at Grandin's office and continued on to Canada (C).
- [T1] Mortgage term eighteen months, no regular payments required. Grandin sold the mortgage in October 1830 (transfer dated 21 Oct. 1830) for $2,000 cash to Thomas Rogers II; when Harris's property was sold (deed 7 Apr. 1831), Rogers collected the full $3,000 from the buyer, Thomas Lakey (E). A: "a year after publication, Harris's property sold to an investor who had purchased the mortgage from Grandin." Copy says "its new holder."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Grandin building street number: D gives 217 East Main; OSM and the Ensign Peak Foundation give 219 for the bookstore door. Copy uses 217 per the Church page.
- "The Erie Canal still runs through the village": stable, but a present-tense map fact.

## Dropped

- Printing start date. J says "mid-August 1829"; E argues type was unlikely to arrive before September. Left out; copy says "Autumn 1829" for the Hyrum bullet.
- Which Grandin built the row: D says Levi Grandin with Joel and Levi Thayer; J says "Phillip Grandin and several Thayer brothers." Builder not named.
- Ink smudges near the press found in the 1990s restoration (J: "it is believed" they date from the printing). Hedged in the source; omitted.
- "Cake and beer shop" / gingerbread and root beer shop on Main Street. Deseret News and a JSP biography line (search result only) say different things about who ran it and where; the Church topic says only "a small shop." Copy uses "a small shop."
- Harris's acreage (at least 151 acres after deeding 80 to Lucy Harris, E). Verified, cut for length and to avoid the marital detail.
- Harris's "lost his farm" framing. A says he "was eventually paid in full." Copy states only the mortgage sale, no loss narrative.
- Hyrum's Canada copyright venture (A). Not Palmyra-specific; omitted.
- Grandin's later life ("left the printing business shortly after," J). Not sourced precisely; omitted.
- Population "about 3,700 in 1820" (G, village page, from Darby's gazetteer). Conflicts with H's "about six hundred" in 1816 and probably counts the township; omitted.
- Village former names Swift's Landing and Tolland before 1796 (G). Verified; cut for length.
- The 1817 to 1825 Western Presbyterian church in the village (127 Church St site marker in OSM). Another church's site; left out per the brief.
- Martin Harris Farm landmark: outside the village frame (see coordinates).
- Wayne Sentinel advertisement of March 26, 1830 (J, C notes). True, but redundant with the sale bullet.
