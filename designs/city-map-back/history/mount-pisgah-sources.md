## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Who Found It: Parley P. Pratt found and named it (A; B: "Elder Pratt christened the place Mount Pisgah, after the biblical height from which Moses surveyed the promised land"; G). "This is Mount Pisgah" (A, citing Autobiography of Parley P. Pratt; F gives the full passage, "I cried out, this is Mount Pisgah," with "round and sloping hills, grassy and crowned with beautiful groves of timber" and "a main branch of Grand River"). About 25 miles northwest of Garden Grove (A). May 1846 (A, F; exact day not given in copy, see Dropped).
- [T1] Pisgah and Moses: B; J ("to the top of Pisgah").
- [T1] The Second Camp: 1846 to 1852 and 2,000 to 3,000 at its height (C; B gives the same range). Homes and two log meetinghouses on a hillside overlooking the fields (B). "Second permanent camp" / second way station (A).

What Happened Here

- [BYU/T1] 21 May 1846 organization: William D. Huntington's diary, "the camp was called together and organized. A council of presidency was appointed over which I was to preside ... with brother Ezra T. Benson and Charles C. Rich" (F). A also names the three as presiding. B: Huntington was "the community's first branch president." Plowing began the day after the 22 May meeting (F, citing Journal History 22 May 1846).
- [T1] Captain James Allen arrived 26 June 1846 (G; F gives "the call came on 26 June 1846"). Stiff opposition at Mount Pisgah (A). Woodruff "directed them on to Council Bluffs to lay the case before the President" (A, quoting Woodruff journal, 26 June 1846).
- [T1/BYU] Sickness through late July and August 1846 (F). "At least 80 people died within their first year of residence" (B). William Huntington died 19 August 1846 at Pisgah (D; E gives 1846; F gives 19 August 1846). Buried there (B).
- [T1] 1852: remaining Saints called to gather to Utah (C: "The Iowa settlements were abandoned in 1852 when Church leaders called the remaining Saints to gather in Utah").
- [T1] Monument story: Albert C. White, "one of the owners of the land," wrote to President John Taylor; Taylor asked Oliver B. Huntington, son of William; purchase finalized 3 May 1886; Huntington solicited donations in Utah and "drafted the text to be engraved on the marble shaft"; "By the fall of 1888 ... a monument was in place" (B). A: one-acre plot bought 1886, monument erected 1888.

On the Map

- [T1] 12-foot marble shaft, 63 names, cemetery likely holds about 150 (B, C). "The Church's first effort to memorialize its history at the actual places where events unfolded" (B).
- [T1/NPS] Adjacent county park with interpretive markers (C); nine-acre park with wayside exhibits, markers and reconstructed log cabin (I); log cabin replica built by the Des Moines Iowa Stake in 1996 (H).

Trivia

- [T1] Brigham Young celebrated his 45th birthday there on 1 June 1846 (A).
- [T1/BYU] Wilford Woodruff, "I had some reasons to believe them to be spies" (A, quoting Wilford Woodruff Journals, 26 June 1846). Woodruff present at Mt. Pisgah when Allen arrived (F).
- [BYU] Orson Pratt journal, 19 May 1846 inspection: "the ground being more hilly and elevated than the prairies over which we had passed we concluded to call the place Mount Pisgah" (F, quoting Pratt journals p. 349 and "Interesting Items"). Copy quotes only "more hilly and elevated than the prairies."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Log cabin in the county park (1996 build; wooden structure, could be removed as the Kanesville replica was).
- "First effort to memorialize" is a historical first; stable, but note it is the Church's own framing.

## Dropped

- Exact founding date. A's text says Pratt designated the site 18 May; A's timeline says 16 May 1846; F says Pratt rode ahead 16 May and the Twelve inspected 19 May. Copy says only "May 1846" and dates the 21 May organization instead.
- Death totals beyond "at least 80 in the first year": about 150 in six months (Jenson, via F), about 300 (F), 300 to 800 buried 1846 to 1852 (G). Wildly divergent; only the Church's "at least 80" figure and the cemetery's "about 150" (B, C) used.
- Burial conditions (no coffins, common graves; Zina D. Young and Charles C. Rich via F). Graphic; omitted under the sensitivity rule.
- Infant burials named in B (Leonora Charlotte Snow, Isaac Phineas Richards). Verified; omitted as personal grief detail.
- First burial Noah Rogers, 31 May 1846 (A names him; date from Jenson via F). Verified, left out for length.
- Acreage. A: "several thousand acres"; B: "a thousand acres"; F: Rich reported a thousand plowed. Conflict; omitted.
- Peak population "over three thousand" (A) and "over 2,000" (I). Used the Church location page's 2,000 to 3,000 range.
- Hannah Settle Lapish's 1885 rediscovery via a Union County geography book (B). Verified; good story but cut for length. Candidate replacement trivia.
- "Original grave markers no longer exist" (Church history subsection page, search summary only, not fetched). Omitted.
- "Oldest commemorative marker built by Latter-day Saints" (search summary only). Copy uses B's fetched wording instead.
- Lorenzo Snow later presided at Pisgah (F). Verified, left out for length.
- Huntington's tent prayer meeting in temple clothes (F, from his diary). Ordinance-adjacent; omitted.
- Ezra T. Benson quote, "the first place where I felt willing in my heart to stay" (A). Verified; omitted to keep trivia from ending reverent.
