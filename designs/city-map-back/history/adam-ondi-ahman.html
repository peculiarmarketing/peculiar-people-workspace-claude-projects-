Adam-ondi-Ahman, Missouri

In Scripture
Section 107, recorded in 1835, says that three years before he died Adam called his righteous posterity "into the valley of Adam-ondi-Ahman" and gave them his last blessing. Section 116, given at Spring Hill in May or June 1838, reads: "Spring Hill is named by the Lord Adam-ondi-Ahman, because, said he, it is the place where Adam shall come to visit his people, or the Ancient of Days shall sit, as spoken of by Daniel the prophet."

Back in Church Hands
The Church announced the purchase of Spring Hill in March 1944. The final deeds were dated June 27, 1944, a hundred years to the day after Joseph and Hyrum Smith were killed. The seller, Eugene Johnson, came from a family that had held the land for a century.

What Happened Here

  • Early 1838: A few Latter-day Saint families settled below the bluffs along the Grand River in Daviess County and called the place Spring Hill, for the springs running off the bluff. Lyman and Harriet Wight built one of the first homes, and Lyman ran a ferry across the river.
  • May 19, 1838: Joseph Smith and a party of Church leaders rode up the river to Wight's. Joseph wrote of grass-covered prairie and "plenty of wild game in this land, such as deer, turkey, hens, elk." They surveyed a city on the bluffs, and the revelation naming Adam-ondi-Ahman followed.
  • June 28, 1838: Joseph Smith organized a stake here, the Church's third, with his uncle John Smith as president and Lyman Wight and Reynolds Cahoon as counselors. The survey ran to more than 520 blocks over four square miles, and more than 200 homes went up that summer and fall.
  • November 1838: Early in the month Missouri militiamen ordered every Latter-day Saint out of the county, under the governor's extermination order. By November 20 the town was nearly empty. Lyman Wight was arrested and spent five months jailed with Joseph Smith at Liberty.

On the Map

  1. The Wight home site sits at the foot of Tower Hill, where Joseph Smith found Lyman Wight living in 1838. A half-mile round-trip trail climbs to it. The two-story log house is gone, but its spot is marked.
  2. Spring Hill Summit, the high bluff the revelation named, has only a simple sign. It looks down on the valley and a bend of the Grand River.
  3. The Valley Overlook, a short walk southeast of its parking lot, takes in the valley between Spring Hill and the river.

Trivia

  • Residents called the town Diahman for short.
  • Tower Hill got its name from a stack of rocks the settlers took for the ruins of an old tower.
  • Senior missionaries look after the trails and overlooks, which are open daily from dawn to dusk. Much of the Church's land around them is farmed.
