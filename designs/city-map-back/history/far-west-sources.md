## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Then and Now: "From late 1836 to 1839, as many as five thousand Saints established homes in and around Far West" (A). Site "marks the center of what used to be a large city with thousands of residents"; "Restrooms, a picnic area, and farmland surround the fenced area" (C). G gives 3,000 to 5,000 (citing Encyclopedia of Mormonism); copy uses A's "as many as five thousand."
- [T1] A County for the Saints: in 1836, "three years after Latter-day Saints had been driven from Jackson County, the Missouri legislature created Caldwell County ... which they intended as a place for Latter-day Saints to settle"; Saints bought land and built Far West (A). Modeled after the "Plat of the City of Zion" (A).
- [T1] Headquarters, 1838: Joseph, Emma and their three children arrived March 14, 1838 (F ch. 26); H: "March 14: Headquarters of the Church was established in Far West." "Center of Church government until November 1838" (D). Seven revelations later published in the D&C, including the full name and the law of tithing (A). Tithing revelation (D&C 119) dated July 8 at Far West (H).

What Happened Here

- [T1] April 26, 1838: D&C 115 heading "at Far West, Missouri, April 26, 1838, identifying the name of the Church"; verse 4 the name; verse 7 "a holy and consecrated land"; verse 8 build a house; verse 10 "the fourth day of July next" (E).
- [T1] July 4, 1838: parade led by Joseph Smith Sr. and a small military unit; flag raised on a tall pole; Saints circled the excavated temple site; workers set the cornerstones; Sidney Rigdon spoke (F ch. 27). Four cornerstones placed and dedicated July 4, 1838 (A). "Thousands of spectators witnessed the ceremony" (C).
- [T1] October 31, 1838: "Armed fighting lasted two weeks" (B). Boggs ordered the militia to surround Far West and drive the Saints from the state (A). Militia under Major General Samuel D. Lucas laid siege October 31; Lucas arrested Joseph Smith and others (B). Arrested "just outside of Far West, 31 Oct. 1838" (G). "Imprisoned pending a trial"; "No further work was done on the temple" (A).
- [T1] January 29, 1839: Brigham Young urged the Saints in Far West to covenant to help each other evacuate; leaders appointed a committee of seven (F ch. 32). Main body went to Illinois (B). "Aside from a handful of leaders and a few families, the city was empty" in April 1839 (F ch. 33).
- [T1] April 26, 1839: D&C 118:4 to 5 (heading July 8, 1838) directs the Twelve to take leave of the Saints in Far West "on the twenty-sixth day of April next, on the building-spot of my house" and go "over the great waters" (E). Apostles entered Far West "on the moonlit night of April 25"; at sunrise walked to the temple site, sang a hymn; Wilford Woodruff and George A. Smith ordained (F ch. 33; C).

On the Map

- [T1] Fenced area with a monument and the four cornerstones; no visitors' center; "Guests spend about 30 minutes at the site" (C).
- [T1] Town site "high rolling prairie" between Shoal and Goose creeks (G, citing the 1876 Illustrated Historical Atlas of Caldwell County and Warren Foote's autobiography). Copy states it without quotation marks or attribution to a single source.

Trivia

- [T1] "Originally called Shoal Creek" (G, citing Berrett, Sacred Places). H: Saints "relocated on Shoal Creek (later known as Far West)." Two T1 statements agree.
- [T1] "By 1838, town featured 150 houses, four dry-goods stores, three family groceries, six blacksmith shops, two hotels, a printing office (where Elders' Journal was printed), and at least two schoolhouses" (G). Copy says "about 150 houses."
- [T1] Alpheus Cutler, "who had been the temple's master workman," rolled a large stone to the southeast corner of the temple site; Wilford sat on it while the apostles ordained him; George A. Smith took his place; Cutler then "rolled the stone back to where he found it" (F ch. 33).

## Landmark coordinates (frame lat 39.6349 to 39.7073, lon -94.1666 to -94.0966)

- Far West Temple Site: 39.67153, -94.13184 (Church pin from C and D); OSM Nominatim "Far West Temple Site," 39.67136, -94.13062. Inside.
- Goose Creek, Caldwell County: OSM Nominatim point 39.65722, -94.11969. Inside, about 1.5 to 2 km southeast of the temple site. Nominatim gives one point on the stream, not its full line; "just southeast" rests on that point.
- Shoal Creek: Nominatim returned several points (39.69527, -94.17486 is just west of the frame; others are miles away). Named in copy only as the second creek bounding the old town, not as a map landmark.
- Not on the map: Kingston, the county seat, about 39.643, -94.039 (east of the frame); Haun's Mill, east of the frame (12 miles east of Far West, H).

## Dropped

- George M. Hinkle's role in the arrest (A and footnote: Joseph felt betrayed; Hinkle and others said Joseph understood Lucas's terms). Blame is disputed in the sources; left out.
- Lucas's execution order and Alexander Doniphan's refusal (B). Verified; cut to keep the Far West item about Far West. Evan may expect Doniphan; it can be restored from B.
- Haun's Mill (October 30, 1838). Outside the frame and the brief bars light handling; it belongs to its own site if anywhere.
- The "extermination order" by name and the phrase "war of extermination" from Rigdon's July 4 oration (B). The oration is the event most people know from that day; copy says only that Rigdon gave the speech, to avoid a quote that needs paragraphs of context. Evan may expect it.
- Danites (B, F ch. 27). Contested and needs context; out of scope for a map back.
- Crooked River and David W. Patten's death (B). Outside the frame.
- "Last of the Saints left Far West April 20, 1839; about 15,000 expelled" (H). Conflicts with Saints ch. 33 (a few families still there April 26) and with the Wight home site page's "over 8,000" driven from Missouri. Dropped the date and both numbers.
- Number of apostles at the April 26, 1839 meeting. C says five members of the Twelve; Saints ch. 33 names more men arriving (Brigham Young, Orson Pratt, John Taylor, John E. Page, Heber C. Kimball, plus Wilford Woodruff and George A. Smith before their ordinations); a search summary said seven. Copy gives no count.
- Order in which the cornerstones were laid by quorum (seen only in a search summary attributed to the Church location page; not on the fetched page). Omitted.
- Site purchased 8 Aug 1836; one square mile held in trust by W. W. Phelps and John Whitmer (G, citing secondary works). Verified on G but would need the Phelps and Whitmer land dispute (F ch. 25 to 26) for context. Omitted.
- Temple dimensions and the monument's date. No fetched source.
- Cornerstones under protective glass (search summary only). Omitted.
