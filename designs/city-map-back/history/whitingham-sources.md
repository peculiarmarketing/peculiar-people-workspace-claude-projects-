## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Born Here: born 1 June 1801 in Whitingham (A, C, E; inscription in B). Ninth child of John and Abigail Howe Young (A; D "ninth child"; I "ninth of 11 siblings"). Family moved from Hopkinton, Massachusetts, to Whitingham in January 1801 [A, F]; A says "shortly before his birth."
- [T1] How Long They Stayed: left when Brigham was "nearly 3," spring 1804 (A); moved 1804 (E, F). "Grew up there": the family's later homes were all in New York (E, F). Copy says "about three years" and "not quite three."
- [T1] On Town Hill: 12-foot granite monument (A); "light Barre Vermont granite" (B); unveiled in the community park on Town Hill (A); inscription quoted from B: "This monument erected by descendants of Brigham Young in cooperation with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints."

What Happened Here

- [T1/A] January 1801 arrival (F, A). Parents called Nabby: F ("John and Abigail (Nabby) Howe Young"). Devout Methodist Episcopal adherents while in Whitingham, 1801-4 (F).
- [T1] 1804 move to Sherburne, Chenango County, New York (E; F citing "Manuscript History of Brigham Young," 1; G).
- [T1] 28 May 1950 dedication by President George Albert Smith (A gives the date and names him and Elder John A. Widtsoe as attending; B: "A few days before [June 1], President Smith also dedicated a monument in Whitingham"). Attendance "about 1,000" (A). Copy attributes the figure to the Church News. Statue in the Capitol dedicated by President Smith on 1 June 1950 (A, B); statue by grandson Mahonri Young (B).
- [T1] 1 June 2001: about 180 people from across New England at the small Whitingham Municipal Center in Jacksonville (C).

On the Map

- [T1] Monument in the park on Town Hill (A). Original sketch by four grandsons: Don D. Young, George Cannon Young, Lorenzo S. Young, G. Young Cannon (B).
- [T1/P] Jacksonville: village in the northeast part of the town (H); site of the Whitingham Municipal Center and the 2001 program (C).

Trivia

- [A] "The Whitingham circuit was one of the first three formed in the state of Vermont" (F, citing Leonard Brown, History of Whitingham, 169), in the context of the family's Methodist Episcopal affiliation.
- [T1] Route 8 marker text "Southward up the steep hill was the birthplace of Brigham Young, June 1, 1801," "opposite a store on Route 8" (D, 1971).
- [P/T1] Name: petition by "Colonel Nathan WHITING, in behalf of himself and his associates" asking that the land "may be erected into a Township by the Name of Whitingham"; letters patent issued 12 March 1770 (H). Charter from the governor of New York in 1770 (A). Copy states only that Whiting petitioned for the name; it does not claim the town was "named for" him, which the gazetteer implies but does not say.
- [A] "On what is known today as Brigham's Hill, the ninth child and future prophet was born on June 1, 1801" (G). Ensign Peak Foundation (lead) calls the street "Brigham Young Hill, also known as Stimpson Hill."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Route 8 marker: described as of 1971. Copy uses past tense ("pointed").
- "Brigham's Hill" as current local usage: true as of Porter, 1998.

## Dropped

- Exact birth spot. A says the monument "sits about two miles from the site once thought to be his birthplace," which hedges the traditional site; Ensign Peak Foundation calls it the "traditional birth site." Copy never says the monument or the marker stands on the birth spot.
- Designers: A says five grandsons designed the monument; B (contemporary, 1950) says the sketch was by four grandsons and names them. Used B's four.
- Cost of $8,000 (A only). Accurate per A but cut for length.
- Elder John A. Widtsoe at the dedication (A). Verified, cut for length.
- Judge Harrie B. Chase's 1950 remarks (Deseret News "50 years ago" via search only). Not fetched; omitted.
- Log cabin birthplace (L. Tom Perry BYU talk, Dilworth Young talk, via search snippets). Not fetched; omitted.
- John Young homesteaded "51 H acres" bought from his brother-in-law Joseph Mosley Sr. (G). The fraction is garbled on the page (51½?). Omitted.
- "Smyrna, New York" as the 1804 destination (D). E and F say Sherburne; G explains Smyrna was later split off from Sherburne. Used Sherburne.
- Story that the monument vanished before a 1901 centennial (Substack). Unsourced; omitted.
- Whitingham's population and present-day Church members (A, 1998). Dated; omitted.
- Sadawga Pond and its floating island (H). Not Church history.
