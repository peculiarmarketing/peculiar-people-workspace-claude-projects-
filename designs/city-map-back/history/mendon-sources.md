## Verified

Spec rows

- [A] Neighbors: John Young came to Mendon from Tyrone in 1827 (H, citing Manuscript History of Brigham Young, Addenda); Brigham "moved his family to Mendon in the spring of 1829 and located on his father's farm" (H, citing Manuscript History, 10). [T1] Kimball "Blacksmith, potter"; married Vilate Murray 22 Nov 1822 at Mendon (F).
- [T1] A Book Passed Hand to Hand: Phinehas bought his copy from Samuel Smith (D); he read it, passed it to his father John, then to his sister Fanny, who gave it to Brigham (A).
- [T1] Leaving: Kimball moved to Kirtland 1833 (F); Brigham migrated to Kirtland 1833 (E); "The Youngs and Kimballs migrated to Kirtland, Ohio, in 1833" (I). "By September 1833 the Tomlinson Inn's role in Church history had ended" (A).

What Happened Here

- [T1] April 1830, Samuel Smith visited the Tomlinson Inn "in the township of Mendon" and sold "a copy of the book to a young man named Phinehas Young, brother of Brigham Young" (D; A also dates it April 1830). "Younger" dropped from copy because no fetched page stated it.
- [T1/A] Brigham baptized Sunday 15 April 1832 by Eleazer Miller (A, H, I; E gives "9/15 Apr." and Miller). Quote "in my own little mill stream" (I, citing Journal of Discourses 13:211; A: "his own mill stream"). Confirmed at the water's edge (I, citing Manuscript History, 10-11). Kimball baptized by Alpheus Gifford, 15 April (F) or 16 April (A); copy says "the same week." Miriam baptized about three weeks later (H).
- [T1] Spring 1832 branch with about 30 members, 11 of them Youngs (A; B also gives 30). Met in the upper room of the inn (A); B says the inn's dance hall became the meetinghouse.
- [T1/A] Miriam died 8 Sept 1832 (E); tuberculosis (G "died of tuberculosis"; I "consumption (tuberculosis)"). Buried in Tomlinson Corners Cemetery (I; A "the little Tomlinson Corners graveyard"). Brigham and his two daughters moved into the Kimball household for eighteen months (I, citing Esplin).

On the Map

- [T1] Inn near the graveyard, which is "just up the road from the inn" (A). Tomlinson Corners on the Mendon-Ionia Road (OSM, geography only).
- [A/T3] John Young's land occupied the northeast and southeast corners of Cheese Factory Road and Mendon-Ionia Road (K, citing deeds of 1827 and 1830). Brigham built "a home and mill back in the property for himself" (I). Home built over the creek, mill on the first level, living quarters on the second (C). The baptism stream is "my own little mill stream" (I), so the callback is to the same stream.
- [T1] Later owners tore down mill and home, pieces left in the stream; Sheldon Fisher excavated "every summer for six years"; found silverware, rings, tools, parts of the home; Fisher sold "a van full" of Brigham's possessions to the Museum of Church History and Art in Salt Lake City (C).

Trivia

- [T1] Kimball: women came from Victor "a distance of three miles, to the town of Mendon where I lived" (B, quoting Journal of Discourses 6:132).
- [T1] Pottery made by Heber C. Kimball found at the site (C).
- [A/T3] John Young house: "Brigham built a home for his father on the road leading south from the village of Mendon" and "John's home was later split in two, and both halves face one another across the Cheese Factory Road" (I, citing an interview with J. Sheldon Fisher, 1992). Half moved across the road; both enlarged into two homes; both still stand (J, 2017). Ensign Peak Foundation (lead) says the same with photos to 2021. Copy does not say who split it or why (J: "for some reason").

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Both John Young house halves "still stand" (J, 2017; Ensign Peak photos 2021). Re-check.
- J and Ensign Peak say the Church owns the two houses and they are used as private residences. Left out of copy as volatile.
- Present state of the Tomlinson Inn building. A (2007) describes it as a private home with the upper room intact. Copy says only where it stood.

## Dropped

- Exact location of the Tomlinson Inn. No source gives a road or address; copy places it only relative to the cemetery.
- Membership of 65 "by 1832" (B). Vague dating against A's spring-1832 figure of about 30; used 30 only.
- Meeting Joseph Smith in Kirtland, Sept to Oct 1832, with Joseph Young and Kimball (I). Verified (A tier) but cut for length.
- Phinehas Young's own year of arrival in Mendon (1828, H) and the Victor residence. Not needed.
- Missionaries' names beyond Miller and Gifford (Elial Strong, Enos Curtis, Daniel Bowen): search summaries and H. Cut for length.
- Kimball's blacksmith shop "about a mile south of Brigham's house" (K only). Single source; omitted.
- K's recollection that the mill was rebuilt as a dwelling on Malone Road in Victor. Author calls it unverified; omitted.
- John Young's barn moved to the Smith farm in Manchester (Ensign Peak; J says "may incorporate"). Hedged; omitted.
- The wood lathe found in a collapsing shed (C). Verified, cut for length.
- Kimball arriving in Mendon "around 1820" (Wikipedia). Lead only; omitted.
