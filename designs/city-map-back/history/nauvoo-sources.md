## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Church years 1839 to 1846, Nauvoo as Church headquarters. F ("church headquarters, 1839 to 1846"); A.
- [T1] Commerce was a small town on the Mississippi; Joseph Smith authorized purchase of a large tract from Isaac Galland that included it. A.
- [T1] Church began buying land in Commerce April 30, 1839. B.
- [T1] Land was wet; malaria sickened many settlers in summer 1839 (the July 22, 1839 healings are the anchor date). Q. Saints drained swamps. A.
- [T1] "At its height, the population reached nearly 10,000 people, making it one of the largest cities in the state." A (quoted exactly on fetch).
- [T1] Post office renamed Commerce to Nauvoo April 21, 1840. B.
- [T1] Name "is of Hebrew origin, and signifies a beautiful situation, or place, carrying with it, also, the idea of rest". G, the 15 January 1841 First Presidency proclamation, text split across JSP pages 1 and 2 (page 1 ends "is of Hebrew origin, and signifies a beauti", page 2 opens "ful situation, or place, carrying with it, also, the idea of rest;"). The opening words "The name of our city (Nauvoo,)" fall on page 1 and the copy paraphrases them, so the quote starts at "is". JSP's footnote 13 confirms the word is real Hebrew (Isaiah 52:7, "are beautiful"). A gives "a Hebrew word sometimes translated as 'beautiful.'"

What Happened Here

- [T1] Act to incorporate the city of Nauvoo, 16 December 1840, chartering the city, a university and a militia; in force Feb. 1, 1841. H. Joseph Smith mayor from 1842 until his death in 1844. A.
- [T1] Cornerstones laid April 6, 1841, on the Church's eleventh anniversary; southeast cornerstone set under Joseph Smith's direction. P (both sources), D.
- [T1] Relief Society organized March 17, 1842 in the Red Brick Store, Emma Smith president. B, A.
- [T1] King Follett discourse April 7, 1844, general conference, about 20,000 present; Follett died in an accident digging a well; funeral a few days earlier. K. Subject: "humankind's divine nature and potential". A.
- [T1] Joseph and Hyrum killed at Carthage Jail June 27, 1844. B, E. Bodies returned to Nauvoo in wagons; about 10,000 attended the viewing at the Mansion House. E. Lay in state in the Mansion dining room, 29 June 1844. I.
- [T1] First wagons left Nauvoo along Parley Street to the ferry February 4, 1846; over 14,000 left during those months. L. About 2,000 Saints in about 400 wagons crossed between February 4 and March 1, 1846. C.
- [T1] Whole temple dedicated May 1, 1846. D.
- [T1] September 1846: "A group of several hundred armed men attacked Latter-day Saints and other defenders of the city"; at least three deaths and a few dozen injuries; a neutral committee negotiated the end of fighting and the remaining Saints were forced to leave; "the remaining hundreds joined the migration in September." C (quoted on fetch).

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- None of the copy is time-bound except the "as of" date. Site operation and the 2024 transfer are left out on purpose.

## Dropped

- "Nauvoo rivaled or was larger than Chicago." No Tier 1 source makes the comparison. A says only "one of the largest cities in the state." Dropped.
- Peak population above 10,000 (11,000, 12,000, 15,000, 20,000). F gives "about 15,000 by 1846," which conflicts with A's "nearly 10,000." A is the more authoritative and more conservative Tier 1 statement. Structural pass (8 Oct 2026): the copy now gives both Tier 1 figures and says the sources differ, rather than picking one; nothing above 15,000 is used. The 20,000 King Follett crowd is an attendance figure (K), not a population, and includes people from the surrounding area.
- "Nauvoo means beautiful place in Hebrew" stated flatly. The word is Hebrew (G footnote) but the "beautiful place, idea of rest" sense is the 1841 proclamation's own gloss; copy quotes the proclamation exactly rather than asserting a translation.
- "Joseph Smith renamed the town" as a single act. A says Joseph renamed it; B and F date the official change to the April 1840 post office action. Copy uses the post office date and the proclamation, which both sources support.
- Swamp story embellishments ("uninhabitable swamp no one else wanted", Joseph Smith miraculously healing the whole city). Kept only the wet ground, malaria in summer 1839 and swamp draining (A, Q). The July 22, 1839 healings are a documented account (Wilford Woodruff) but are a miracle account outside the brand voice; omitted.
- Exact Battle of Nauvoo days (often given as September 10 to 17, 1846) and the name "Battle of Nauvoo." C gives only September 1846 and does not use the name. Used September 1846.
- Ice crossing story (Saints crossing on the frozen river under mob pressure). C says most went by ferry, a few may have crossed on ice February 25 to March 1, and that accounts of a mob forcing the ice crossing are not supported. Left out.
- Private temple dedication April 30, 1846 (Joseph Young) and public sessions May 1 to 3 (Orson Hyde). D gives only "the entire temple was dedicated on May 1, 1846." Kept May 1 only.
- Charter date as February 1, 1841. That is the in-force date (H); the act is dated December 16, 1840. Copy uses the act date and calls it "approved."
- Martyrdom time of day (5:16 p.m. and similar). E says "late that afternoon." Not stated as a clock time; the trivia relies on N's "even the hour was the same."
- Carthage distance. A says about 20 miles; N says about 15 miles. Conflict, and not needed; omitted.
- 1928 relocation of Joseph's and Hyrum's remains to the present cemetery graves. Only non-official sources (LDS Living, a University of Utah photo collection) found; Mackay's Mormon Historical Studies article not read. Copy says only that they are buried in the cemetery (M).
- Count of people buried in the Smith Family Cemetery. Ensign Aug. 1991 says the Prophet "and twenty-six other family members"; Ensign Nov. 1991 says seventeen other relatives and friends besides those named. Conflict; omitted.
- Fire sale details (David Allred, $5,000; Cabet bought for $2,000 on April 2, 1849). Only Times and Seasons blog and Newberry Library lead pages; J does not name Allred or give the price. Omitted. Icarian arrival kept as "acquired the shell" without a date.
- Tornado exact day (May 27, 1850). J gives May 1850 only.
- King Follett location ("the grove east of the temple"). Only a JSP footnote seen in a search snippet, not on fetch. Omitted.
- 2024 transfer of the Red Brick Store, Mansion House, Homestead and Nauvoo House from Community of Christ (R, $192.5 million). Verified, but left out of customer copy: sensitive for Community of Christ members, and not Nauvoo-era history. Kept in this ledger only.
- Seventies Hall, Brigham Young Home, Times and Seasons building. All inside the frame (OSM: Seventies Hall 40.5433, -91.3947; Brigham Young Home 40.5442, -91.3929) but no Tier 1 fact about events there was fetched; cut to keep the list at six well-sourced sites.
- Rebuilt temple open-house figure (331,849) and 13 dedicatory sessions. Verified (O, N) but temple detail rather than town history; left out to keep temple points to two.
