Mendon, New York

Neighbors
The Youngs and the Kimballs. Brigham Young's father, John, came to Mendon in 1827, and Brigham moved his own family onto his father's farm in the spring of 1829. Heber C. Kimball, a blacksmith and potter, had married Vilate Murray here in 1822.

A Book Passed Hand to Hand
Phinehas Young read the Book of Mormon he bought at the Tomlinson Inn, then passed it to his father and to his sister Fanny. Fanny gave it to Brigham.

Leaving
The Youngs and Kimballs moved to Kirtland, Ohio, in 1833. By that September the inn's part in Church history was over.

What Happened Here

  • April 1830: At the Tomlinson Inn, Samuel Smith, the Prophet's brother, sold a copy of the new Book of Mormon to Phinehas Young, Brigham's brother.
  • Sunday, April 15, 1832: Eleazer Miller baptized Brigham Young. Brigham remembered it as "in my own little mill stream," and Miller confirmed him at the water's edge. Heber C. Kimball was baptized by Alpheus Gifford the same week, and Brigham's wife, Miriam, about three weeks later.
  • Spring 1832: The Mendon Branch was organized with about 30 members. Eleven were Youngs. They met in an upstairs room at the Tomlinson Inn.
  • September 8, 1832: Miriam Young died of tuberculosis. Brigham buried her in the Tomlinson Corners cemetery, then moved with his two daughters into Heber and Vilate Kimball's house for about a year and a half.

On the Map

  1. John Young's farm took in the corners where Cheese Factory Road meets the Mendon-Ionia Road. At the back of the property Brigham built his own house over the creek, the same little mill stream he was baptized in: the mill downstairs, the family upstairs. After the Youngs left, later owners tore the mill down and left the pieces in the stream. Sheldon Fisher dug there for six summers and found silverware, tools and parts of the house. He later sold a van full of Brigham's belongings to the Museum of Church History and Art in Salt Lake City. Sources don't say where on the farm the mill stood, so the marker is at the corners.
  2. Tomlinson Corners, south of Mendon village on the Mendon-Ionia Road. The inn stood a short way down the road from the little graveyard where Miriam is buried. No source gives the inn's exact site; the marker is at the graveyard.

Trivia

  • Heber C. Kimball later recalled women walking three miles from Victor to meetings in Mendon.
  • Fisher's dig also turned up pottery made by Heber C. Kimball.
  • Brigham built a house for his father on the road south of the village. It was later split in two and half of it moved across Cheese Factory Road. Both halves still stand there facing each other, each one enlarged into a house of its own.
