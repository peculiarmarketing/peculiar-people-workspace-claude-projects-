## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Which Town: JS-H 1:3, "in the town of Sharon, Windsor county, State of Vermont" (F, quoted on fetch). E: birth on a farm in Sharon township. C: memorial address 357 LDS Lane, South Royalton, Vermont 05068. OpenStreetMap places the visitors' center (397 LDS Lane) in Sharon and 357 LDS Lane just inside Royalton, so the town line runs through the property. Copy states only that the mail goes through South Royalton and the birthplace is in Sharon township.
- [T1] The Farm: farm owned by Lucy's father Solomon Mack (B, D); Joseph Sr. rented a 68-acre farm from him (E); "this 68-acre farm" (D). "Spent the previous three years renting from Lucy's parents in Sharon" (D). Farming in the growing season and teaching school in winter (D, describing the family's renting years; Lucy Mack Smith's history says this of Sharon specifically, seen via a Meridian transcription, lead only).
- [T1] The Monument: single stone cut to 38½ feet long, one foot per year of the Prophet's life, four feet square at the base (A). 38½ feet, one foot per year (B, G, H). "Tallest polished granite shaft in America at that time" (G); B: "reported to be the largest spire in America to be cut from a single piece of granite." Twelve-foot-square base stone (A). Whole monument 50 feet: "a 50-foot granite obelisk" (C); "50-foot monument" (J).

What Happened Here

- [T1] Born December 23, 1805 (C, F, E), in a cabin on the farm (E).
- [T1] Left Sharon in the winter of 1807 to 1808, moved to Tunbridge, then Royalton; Samuel born March 13, 1808 in Tunbridge (D, footnote 2). "Joseph Jr. likely remembered little of the farm he left as a toddler" (D).
- [T1] Autumn 1894 visit: guide Harvey Smith; Junius F. Wells with Spencer Clawson and daughter Clara; hearthstone of gray Vermont granite and rectangular outline of the cabin; Wells: "Sometime we ought to mark this place with a monument" (A).
- [T1] 1905: on First Presidency assignment, Wells researched land titles and residents' memories to establish the farm's perimeters and completed the purchase (A; B also says he was authorized to purchase the old Solomon Mack farm in 1905). July 24 contract with R. C. Bowers Company of Montpelier (A). Barre quarry (A). Stones shipped by rail, hauled six miles from the railhead; first load 13 days, second 20 days (A; B: "transported by rail and wagon"). Shaft raised December 8 (A).
- [T1] December 23, 1905 dedication by President Joseph F. Smith on the 100th anniversary (A, G, H); service in a small cottage built over the hearthstone; party came by special train from Salt Lake City (A).
- [T1] December 23, 2005: President Gordon B. Hinckley spoke from the visitors' center; the hearthstone "only a couple of feet away" (I); address transmitted by satellite into the Conference Center program and to meetinghouses Church-wide (H).

On the Map

- [T1] Obelisk on the site; "A birthplace marker denotes the site of the Smith home" (H). Hilltop: A ("up a slight rise to the hilltop"; last two miles rose more than 800 feet).
- [T1] Cottage torn down in the 1960s when "today's two modern buildings" went up (A). Hearthstone now part of the visitors' center fireplace (B, H).
- [T1] Evening meeting in South Royalton on the dedication day for Church dignitaries and area residents; Wells thanked "the people of this village and the adjoining ones" (A).

Trivia

- [T1] Guide named Harvey Smith; "It was coincidental that his name was Smith" (A).
- [T1] Twenty horses from a Mr. Ellis of the Bethel quarries plus two more stalled at the first incline; Wells wrote but did not send a telegram asking permission to send the monument to Salt Lake City for the temple block; waited over Sunday (A, quoting Proceedings p. 12).
- [T1] Mud hole at the foot of Haines's hill; temperature fell 35°F in three hours; frozen ground, wagons "split the planks into kindling wood"; citizens of Royalton called it "Mr. Wells's weather" (A). B gives "35 degrees in three and a half hours" (site director quote); copy says "about three hours."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- "Present buildings" and the hearthstone in the visitors' center fireplace: true as of the 2005 and 2011 sources; any rebuild of the visitors' center would change it.

## Dropped

- Weight of the shaft. Two Church sources disagree: A says the block weighed sixty tons when loaded at the quarry (before cutting); B's captions say the shaft weighs 39 tons. Deseret News 1997 says nearly 60 tons and 100 tons for the monument. Omitted.
- "Seven weeks to make the 35-mile trek" (B). A's account (13 and 20 days over six miles from the railhead) is more detailed and cites the Proceedings; the totals do not line up cleanly. Used A only.
- Wells's first visit in 1884 (Wikipedia, Atlas Obscura). A, citing the Proceedings, says 1894. Used 1894.
- The town line through the cabin, "born in the kitchen, he was born in Royalton" (Deseret News 1997 quoting a site director; Meridian; blogs). No Church source; folklore-shaped. Omitted. Copy only states the birthplace is in Sharon township with a South Royalton address.
- Years in Sharon. E gives 1804 to 1807; D gives three years ending winter 1807 to 1808; B says the Smiths left when Joseph was three. Copy says "about three years" and dates the departure from D.
- "Crop failures forced the Smiths to leave" (B only). D gives no such reason. Omitted.
- 100-acre farm (B, J) versus 68 acres (D, E). J says the Smiths rented part of a 100-acre farm, which reconciles them, but copy uses only the 68 acres the Smiths rented. B's "nearly 200 acres" of later adjoining purchases also left out (no date).
- B's claim that the 1905 purchase "marked the beginning of procuring other sites" (a "first historic site" superlative). Single source, phrasing too loose to state as a first. Omitted.
- Hyrum Smith: born February 9, 1800, in Tunbridge, not Sharon (K). Correct, but Tunbridge village is outside the frame and the brief warns against ending on a myth correction; not used in copy.
- Joseph F. Smith's dedicatory blessing ("Peace be with you, and unto this place..."). Verified (A, G) but left out to avoid ending on a reverent note.
- Hinckley's membership and Book of Mormon figures at the 2005 visit (H). Not Sharon history.
- "Solomon Mack Home" ruins (OSM, 43.82494, -72.46907) and "Solomon & Daniel Mack Farm Site" in Tunbridge (OSM, 43.85496, -72.45062). No Church source ties a specific event to either point; the Tunbridge one is a different Mack farm (D, footnote 1). Not listed.
- Annual Christmas lights and nativity at the memorial. Ongoing event, not history; volatile.
- Ephraim Smith born and died in Royalton, March 1810 (D). Verified, left out for length.
