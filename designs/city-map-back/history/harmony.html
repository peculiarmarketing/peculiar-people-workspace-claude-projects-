Harmony, Pennsylvania

On Today's Maps
Oakland Township, Susquehanna County. Harmony village no longer exists, and it shouldn't be mixed up with the Harmony near Pittsburgh, across the state.

Emma's Family
Emma Hale was born in what became Harmony on July 10, 1804, the seventh of Isaac and Elizabeth Hale's nine children. Isaac farmed, hunted and kept an inn, and shipped meat downriver to Philadelphia and Baltimore. In late 1825 Josiah Stowell's crew of silver diggers boarded at the Hale place, Joseph Smith among them.

Scripture From Here
Most of the Book of Mormon was translated in Harmony, and 15 sections of the Doctrine and Covenants were received here.

What Happened Here

  • December 1827: Joseph and Emma arrived from Manchester, New York, with the plates. In February 1828 they moved into a small house on a lot next to her parents' land.
  • June 15, 1828: Martin Harris had been Joseph's scribe since April, and by mid-June the manuscript ran to at least 116 pages. Harris took it home to show his family. The day after he left, Emma gave birth to a son who died soon after, and she nearly died herself. Joseph went north to find Harris and learned the pages were lost. Back in Harmony that July, he received the revelation now in section 3.
  • April 7, 1829: Oliver Cowdery, a schoolteacher who had boarded with Joseph's parents, arrived on foot on April 5 with Joseph's brother Samuel. Two days later he started writing as Joseph dictated, and the pages piled up at six to eight a day.
  • May 15, 1829: Joseph and Oliver went into the woods to pray about baptism. John the Baptist appeared and conferred the Aaronic Priesthood on them, and they baptized each other in the Susquehanna.
  • August 26, 1830: Joseph paid Isaac Hale the last of what he owed for the farm. Soon after, he and Emma left for Fayette, New York. Emma never saw her parents again.
  • September 19, 2015: President Russell M. Nelson, then President of the Quorum of the Twelve, dedicated the renovated Priesthood Restoration Site after three years of work. The state highway that had split the two homes was rerouted first, with the Church's help.

On the Map

  1. The Joseph and Emma Smith Home and the Isaac and Elizabeth Hale Home are reconstructions, built on the footprints of the originals. The old foundation stones now face the Hale home's foundation and make up a fence around the Smith home. A short walk away, McKune Cemetery holds Joseph and Emma's first child and Emma's parents. Next to it is the Aaronic Priesthood monument the Church put up in 1960, sculpted by Avard Fairbanks.
  2. A trail runs into the sugar maple grove, or sugar bush, beside the visitors' center, where the Aaronic Priesthood was most likely restored. Joseph's history says only that they went into the woods. The exact spot isn't known; the marker shows the grove historians point to.
  3. Another trail goes down to the bank of the Susquehanna where Joseph and Oliver were baptized. Sources don't say exactly where along the river they waded in; the marker shows where the trail meets the bank.

Trivia

  • For years the John the Baptist scene was pictured on the riverbank, a habit that traces back to an early painting. Historians now place it in the maple grove.
  • Oliver later wrote that on Monday, his first full day in Harmony, he helped Joseph with "some business of a temporal nature." It was the agreement to buy the house, barn and land from Isaac Hale for $200, with $64 down, written out in Oliver's hand and witnessed by him and Samuel. The date on it is April 6, 1829, one year to the day before the Church was organized.
