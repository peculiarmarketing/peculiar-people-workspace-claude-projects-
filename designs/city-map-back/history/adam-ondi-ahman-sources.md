## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] In Scripture: D&C 107:53, "Three years previous to the death of Adam, he called Seth, Enos, ... with the residue of his posterity who were righteous, into the valley of Adam-ondi-Ahman, and there bestowed upon them his last blessing"; heading dates section 107 to "late winter or spring 1835" (G). D&C 116 quoted in full from the fetched text; heading: "near Wight's Ferry, at a place called Spring Hill, Daviess County, Missouri, May or June 1838" (G). No interpretation beyond the verse text; the copy adds nothing about when or how.
- [T1] Back in Church Hands: "The Church announced the purchase of Spring Hill in Missouri ... Final deeds for the purchase were dated June 27, 1944, the 100th anniversary of the martyrdom of the Prophet Joseph Smith. The deed to the land was passed on to the Church by Eugene Johnson, whose family had been in possession of the property for a century" (J, entry headed "March"). K: "One century after Joseph and Hyrum were killed, the Church purchased land in Spring Hill."

What Happened Here

- [T1] Early 1838: "Early in 1838, a small group of Latter-day Saints moved to Daviess County"; named Spring Hill "after the freshwater springs they found on the bluffs" (B); springs "flowing from the bluff down into the river" (E). Wights among the first settlers; "Lyman operated a ferry across the Grand River" (C). Settlement "at the base of a bluff" (H).
- [T1] May 19, 1838: J dates the visit May 19. Journal quotation as given on B ("a plenty of wild game in this land, such as deer, turkey, hens, elk, etc."); copy starts the quotation at "plenty" and stops at "elk." Rode along the river ten miles to Spring Hill (H). Surveyed a site for a city on the bluffs of the Grand River near Wight's home and ferry, May 1838 (I). Revelation followed during the planning (B, D, H); G dates it May or June.
- [T1] June 28, 1838: stake organized; John Smith president, Lyman Wight and Reynolds Cahoon counselors (C). "The church's third stake of Zion" (B); A says "one of the first in the Church," which agrees. Over 520 surveyed blocks, four square miles, over 200 homes through summer and fall (B). A: "as many as two hundred homes." Copy says "more than 200" per B; see Dropped for the difference.
- [T1] November 1838: "In early November, Missouri militiamen ordered all Latter-day Saints to leave the county in accordance with the governor's extermination order" (B). C dates the order November 2; I says the militia arrived November 8 with a ten-day ultimatum. Copy says "early in the month." "Town almost completely abandoned, by 20 Nov. 1838" (I). Lyman Wight "arrested and spent five months in jail with Joseph Smith in Liberty" (C).

On the Map

- [T1] Spring Hill Summit: "A simple sign marks the summit"; the bluff "overlooks a valley bordering a bend in the Grand River" (E).
- [T1] Valley Overlook: "a short walking path southeast to the overlook"; view of "the valley between Spring Hill and a bend of the Grand River" (F).
- [T1] Tower Hill and Wight home site: trail "roughly a 0.5-mile (0.8 km) round trip" with "a significant incline"; "the two-story log home no longer exists, the home's location is marked" (C).

Trivia

- [T1] Nickname "Diahman" (I; B: "nicknamed 'Diahman' by its residents").
- [T1] Tower Hill "from a noticeable stack of rocks, which they supposed were the remains of a tower left behind by people living here in the past" (D, citing Joseph Smith's 1838 journal).
- [T1] Senior missionaries serve on the grounds: L (2025, a couple serving at the site since April 2025, "on the grounds"); N (2012): "Missionaries tend the grounds." "Open daily, dawn to dusk" (B, D, F).
- [T3] Church land farmed: M (1972): "the valley is planted with field corn in the summer"; N (2012): "rolling farm land along the Grand River," "In the summer, the corn is tall"; O (1983): Church land "much of it under cultivation," missionary families "work on the farms." Three independent sources across forty years (a BYU Studies article, a local newspaper feature, a public-radio report). Copy says only "much of the Church's land around them is farmed," not the crop.

## Dropped

- Total acreage today (3,000 acres per KBIA 2012, Wikipedia and a Fox 2 page that returned HTTP 403; 1,500 per the 1983 article; "several hundred" per Matthews 1972). No T1 figure; numbers disagree by era.
- Size of the 1944 purchase (about thirty acres including Tower Hill and the Wight home, M; 38 acres per Ensign Peak Foundation, a lead). Not T1; J gives no acreage.
- Wilford C. Wood as the buyer in 1944 (M; Ensign Peak lead). Not in J; omitted.
- Altar legends at Tower Hill and Spring Hill (M discusses them as legends; O repeats a "triple-tiered stone altar" 36 feet long and stones shipped away in 1922). Folklore screen: not shipped. D's neutral wording about the rock pile used instead. Joseph Smith's journal phrase "the remains of an old Nephite altar or tower" (quoted in M) left out as interpretive.
- Garden of Eden in Jackson County (N, O). Interpretive and unsourced to scripture here; outside the brief.
- Any statement about future events at the site beyond the D&C 116 text itself.
- Population growing from 400 to over 1,000 (I, citing Encyclopedia of Mormonism). Secondary-sourced on I; not needed.
- Raids from Adam-ondi-Ahman in mid-October 1838 (I). Verified on a T1 page; omitted for length, with the copy keeping to the settlement and its abandonment. Evan may expect some mention of Gallatin and Millport; B covers them.
- A's "as many as two hundred homes" against B's "over 200 homes." Copy follows B; small difference noted here.
- W. W. Phelps's hymn "Adam-ondi-Ahman," sung at the 1836 Kirtland Temple dedication (A, G heading note). Kirtland history, not this map.
- Proposed Pattonsburg dam on the Grand River (M, 1972). Proposal only; omitted.
- 2025 senior missionary death (L). Used only to confirm missionaries serve on the grounds; not mentioned in copy.
