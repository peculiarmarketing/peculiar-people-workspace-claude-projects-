Afton, New York

Then Called
South Bainbridge, a village in the town of Bainbridge, Chenango County. Joseph Smith's own history uses that name for where he married Emma Hale.

Josiah Stowell
Born in New Hampshire in 1770, he settled in the town in 1791, farmed, and owned a sawmill. His house stood near the Susquehanna, just southwest of the village. He hired Joseph in 1825 and stood by him in court twice.

What Survives
Not much on paper from 1826. The justice's docket book and the case papers from Joseph's first hearing are lost, and what remains comes from later printings. Even the outcome is uncertain.

What Happened Here

  • November 1825: Joseph and his father signed on with Stowell to dig for a Spanish silver mine rumored to lie near Harmony, Pennsylvania. After nearly a month Joseph talked him out of it. He stayed on to work Stowell's farm here, 28 miles from Emma's home.
  • March 20, 1826: Joseph went before Albert Neely, a justice of the peace in Bainbridge, on a disorderly person charge. The published docket names Stowell's nephew Peter Bridgman as the complainant. Stowell told the court Joseph had not swindled him. The record names Bainbridge and stops there, so which village heard the case is still open.
  • January 18, 1827: Emma was visiting the Stowells when Joseph proposed. Her parents opposed the match, so the two were married "at the house of Squire Tarbill, in South Bainbridge," as Joseph put it. Stowell then helped them move to Manchester to live with Joseph's parents.
  • June 29, 1830: Arrested in Colesville the evening before, Joseph was brought here by Constable Ebenezer Hatch and kept overnight at a tavern. Justice Joseph Chamberlin heard twelve witnesses, Stowell among them, and Joseph was acquitted.

On the Map

  1. The Josiah Stowell Home is on State Route 7 southwest of the village. The Church doesn't own or run it, and docents give tours.
  2. The Tarbill house stood where the Afton Fair grounds are now. The house is gone, but a roadside marker shows the spot. For years the sign called the bride "Emily Hale." The newer one gets her name right.
  3. The Joseph Knight Sr. Home, over the county line in Colesville, is where Constable Hatch arrested Joseph on the evening of June 28, 1830.
  4. South Bainbridge, the village now called Afton, is where Joseph spent that night at a tavern and was tried the next day. Which tavern, and which building held the trial, isn't known, so the marker shows the village center. Where the 1826 hearing met is not known either; its record says only Bainbridge, which then named the whole town.

Trivia

  • Joseph's two defenders in 1830, James Davidson and John S. Reed, were farmers, not lawyers.
  • The ground here was called Jericho when Stowell arrived. The town became Bainbridge in 1814, and in 1857 South Bainbridge broke off as its own town and took the name Afton, after Afton Water, the Ayrshire stream Robert Burns wrote about in "Flow gently, sweet Afton."

History verified as of October 9, 2026.
