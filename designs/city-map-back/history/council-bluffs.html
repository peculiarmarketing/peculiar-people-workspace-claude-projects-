Council Bluffs, Iowa

Three Names
Miller's Hollow first, after Henry W. Miller's camp. Then Kanesville, for Thomas L. Kane, a friend of the Saints who was not a member and who helped arrange the Mormon Battalion. The town became Council Bluffs after the Saints left.

The Kanesville Years
1846 to 1852. Kanesville was where the Saints on the Iowa side of the Missouri rested, organized and got ready for the trail west.

What Happened Here

  • June 14, 1846: The first pioneer company reached the Missouri River. They made camp on Mosquito Creek, on Potawatomi land, and in about two weeks had built landings and a boat to ferry the wagons.
  • July 16, 1846: Nearly 500 men were mustered into the Mormon Battalion for the war with Mexico. Captain James Allen had come in late June to enlist them. Brigham Young called it "the first offer we have ever had from the government to benefit us."
  • December 27, 1847: More than 1,000 Saints crowded into a new log tabernacle and sustained Brigham Young as President of the Church, with Heber C. Kimball and Willard Richards as his counselors. Nine of the Twelve had agreed to it on December 5 at Orson Hyde's home. Henry W. Miller and about 200 men had put up the 60-by-40-foot hall in three weeks.
  • 1848: Oliver Cowdery, out of the Church for ten years, came to Kanesville and rejoined it. Orson Hyde rebaptized him.
  • 1852: The remaining Saints left for Utah at the First Presidency's direction, and Kanesville's years as a Latter-day Saint town ended.

On the Map

  • The Kanesville Memorial is at 222 East Broadway, near where the log tabernacle stood. It has gardens, a visitors' center and statues of Brigham Young with his two counselors, Henry W. Miller and Sophronia Standage, whose husband marched with the battalion.
  • On the campus of the Iowa School for the Deaf, a marker stands where the battalion was mustered in. It was dedicated on July 16, 1988, 142 years to the day. The Grand Encampment site of 1846 is on the same grounds.

Trivia

  • The 1847 tabernacle was built of cottonwood, a soft wood that went up fast. Spring runoff in 1848 and 1849 soaked it, and by the fall of 1849 it had been taken down.
  • A replica, again in cottonwood, was dedicated by President Gordon B. Hinckley on July 13, 1996, with beams from Plattsmouth, Nebraska, chipped by hand to look hand-hewn. The Church took it over in 1999 and closed it in 2020. Demolition started April 4, 2022, and the memorial garden went in where it stood.
