Mount Pisgah, Iowa

Who Found It
Parley P. Pratt. Riding ahead of his company in May 1846, about 25 miles northwest of Garden Grove, he came on grassy hills crowned with groves above a branch of the Grand River and called out, "This is Mount Pisgah." Pisgah is the height where Moses stood to look over the promised land.

The Second Camp
A way station for Saints who could not yet go on, kept from 1846 to 1852. At its height between 2,000 and 3,000 people lived here. They built homes and two log meetinghouses on a hillside and farmed the fields below.

What Happened Here

  • May 21, 1846: The camp was called together and organized. William Huntington was appointed to preside, with Ezra T. Benson and Charles C. Rich beside him. Plowing started within days.
  • June 26, 1846: Captain James Allen of the U.S. Army rode in to recruit men for a battalion. He met stiff opposition here and was sent on to put the request to Brigham Young at the Missouri.
  • Summer of 1846: Sickness spread through the camp in July and August. At least 80 people died in the settlement's first year. William Huntington was one of them, on August 19.
  • 1852: Church leaders called the remaining Saints in Iowa to gather to Utah, and the settlement was left.
  • May 3, 1886: The Church bought the acre that held the graves. Albert C. White, one of the landowners, had written to President John Taylor suggesting the graves be marked, and Taylor gave the job to Oliver B. Huntington, William's son. Oliver raised donations in Utah and wrote the inscription. The monument was in place by the fall of 1888.

On the Map

  • The Mount Pisgah cemetery has the 12-foot marble shaft from 1888. It carries 63 names, though the cemetery likely holds about 150 Latter-day Saints. The Church calls it its first effort to mark its history at the place where it happened.
  • Next to the cemetery is a county park with interpretive markers and a log cabin the Des Moines Iowa Stake built in 1996.

Trivia

  • Brigham Young turned 45 here, on June 1, 1846.
  • Wilford Woodruff was at Mount Pisgah the day Captain Allen arrived. He wrote in his journal that he had "some reasons to believe them to be spies."
  • Parley's brother Orson gave the name a plainer reason. His journal says the Twelve called the place Mount Pisgah because the ground was "more hilly and elevated than the prairies" they had been crossing.
