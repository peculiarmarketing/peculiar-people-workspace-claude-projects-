Carthage, Illinois

County Seat
Carthage was the seat of Hancock County and the site of the county jail. Nauvoo, where Joseph Smith was mayor and his brother Hyrum vice mayor, lay about 26 miles to the northwest.

The Jail
Begun in 1839 and finished about two years later, a two-story limestone building with cells for prisoners and rooms for the jailer, George Stigall, and his family. It held mostly people accused of minor crimes.

Why They Came
In June 1844 the Nauvoo city council declared the Nauvoo Expositor a public nuisance and had its press destroyed. Joseph and Hyrum Smith gave themselves up in Carthage on a riot charge, were charged there with treason, and were held in the jail to await trial. They arrived on the evening of June 25.

What Happened Here

  • June 27, 1844: Soon after five in the afternoon, an armed mob stormed the jail and fired into the jailer's upstairs bedroom, where the four men were. Joseph and Hyrum Smith were killed. John Taylor was shot four times and survived; Willard Richards was not hurt. Section 135 of the Doctrine and Covenants, first printed in the 1844 edition, records that they "were shot in Carthage jail, on the 27th of June, 1844, about five o'clock p.m., by an armed mob."
  • May 1845: Five men were tried in Carthage for the killings. The jury acquitted all five.
  • November 5, 1903: The Church bought the jail. It went on serving as a home into the 1930s.
  • 1939: At the urging of Bryant S. Hinckley, president of the Northern States Mission, the Church partly restored the jail and called a missionary couple to give tours.
  • June 27, 1989: President Gordon B. Hinckley dedicated the renovated complex. The jail had been restored as closely as possible to how it looked in 1844, and the visitors' center was enlarged.

On the Map

  • Carthage Jail stands on Walnut Street, a few blocks northwest of the square. Tours start at the visitors' center at 310 Buchanan Street and go through four rooms, two on each floor.
  • On the plaza leading to the jail stands Dee Jay Bawden's statue of the brothers, Hyrum behind Joseph with an arm around him. It was first planned as two separate statues. Slate tablets nearby carry quotations from both men.
  • The courthouse square downtown is listed on the National Register as the Carthage Courthouse Square Historic District.

Trivia

  • The jail was used as a jail for only about 25 years. After that it was a private house, and one of the nicer ones in Carthage.
  • Building it in 1839 to 1841 cost $4,105. In 1903 the Church paid $4,000 for the jail and its two acres.
