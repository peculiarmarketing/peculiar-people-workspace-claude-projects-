Palmyra, New York

Canal Town
When the Smith family came in 1816, Palmyra was a village of about 600 people sitting on the planned route of the Erie Canal. The canal opened in 1825 and turned the place into a trading center. The village incorporated two years later, in March 1827.

The Printer
Egbert B. Grandin, born in 1806 and about the same age as Joseph Smith. He bought the Wayne Sentinel in 1827, and by November 1828 he had moved into the new Thayer and Grandin Row on Main Street, with his bookstore on the ground floor, the bindery on the second and the press on the third.

The First Edition
5,000 copies of a 590-page book, printed and bound by Grandin and about a dozen men over seven months. An ordinary book of the day was printed in runs of less than half that.

What Happened Here

  • 1817: The Smiths rented a house in the village for about two years. Joseph Sr., Alvin and Hyrum hired out as day laborers, the family ran a small shop, and Lucy painted oilcloth covers for tables and chests while they saved toward land of their own.
  • June 1829: Grandin turned the job down, believing the book was a fraud. Joseph Smith and Martin Harris found a printer in Rochester willing to take it, then came back and asked Grandin again. This time he agreed to print and bind 5,000 copies for $3,000.
  • August 25, 1829: Harris, a Palmyra farmer, mortgaged his farm north of the village to Grandin to secure the bill. By the compositor's later account, Grandin wouldn't even order type until Harris had promised the money.
  • Autumn 1829: Hyrum Smith carried the printer's copy of the manuscript to the shop a batch at a time, sometimes buttoned under his vest. It had almost no punctuation, so the compositor, John H. Gilbert, put it in as he set the type.
  • January 1830: Abner Cole, who rented Grandin's press at night for his paper, the Reflector, began printing pages of the unfinished book. Joseph Smith came up from Harmony, Pennsylvania, found Cole in the shop and told him the right to publish it was his. Cole stopped.
  • March 26, 1830: The first copies went on sale in Grandin's bookstore, bound in brown calfskin with "Book of Mormon" in gold on the spine.

On the Map

  • The Grandin building stands at 217 East Main Street. The Church was given it in 1978, by which time it had been a law office, a milliner's, tailor and tin shops, a hardware store and a variety store. After two and a half years of restoration, President Gordon B. Hinckley dedicated it on March 26, 1998, 168 years to the day after the first copies went on sale.
  • The Erie Canal still runs through the village, a few hundred yards north of Main Street.
  • Alvin Smith, Joseph's oldest brother, is buried in the old cemetery on Church Street. He died in November 1823 at 25.

Trivia

  • Loose printed sheets left the shop before the book did. Thomas B. Marsh was handed 16 pages and took them home to Boston, and Solomon Chamberlin got 64 and carried them on to Canada.
  • Harris's August mortgage gave him 18 months to pay. Grandin didn't wait. In October 1830 he sold the mortgage for $2,000 in cash, and its new holder collected the full $3,000 when Harris's land was sold in April 1831.
