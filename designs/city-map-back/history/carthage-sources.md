## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] "Carthage was the county seat of Hancock County and the location of the county jail" (F). Joseph and Hyrum were then mayor and vice mayor of Nauvoo (B). Bodies taken to the Mansion House in Nauvoo, about 26 miles away (B). "Northwest" is the map direction (Nauvoo 40.55, -91.38 vs Carthage 40.41, -91.13).
- [T1] Jail begun in 1839 and completed two years later (F); built in 1839, first jail in Hancock County (C); built 1839 (H). Two-story (H: "two-story stone jail"; B: "two-story building made of red limestone"; C: "two-and-a-half-story brown stone building"). Copy: "two-story limestone building." Jailer George Stigall and family used the rooms (B, C; H spells it Stigal). Mainly people accused of minor crimes (C).
- [T1] Expositor declared a nuisance and press destroyed (D; B: Joseph "called for the destruction"). Warrant on riot charges; the brothers turned themselves in at Carthage; charged with treason; held awaiting trial (B, C, D). Arrived evening of June 25, 1844 (B, C).

What Happened Here

- [T1] Soon after 5:00 p.m. (B); "about five o'clock p.m., by an armed mob" (A v. 1, quoted). Mob fired into the upstairs room / jailer's bedroom where Joseph, Hyrum, Taylor and Richards were (B, C, D). Joseph and Hyrum killed (A, B, C, D). John Taylor shot four times and survived (D); A v. 2 has Taylor wounded and Richards unhurt; D: "Only Willard Richards emerged unscathed." Account published in the 1844 Doctrine and Covenants, now section 135 (C).
- [T1] Five men tried in Carthage in May 1845, all acquitted (D).
- [T1] Purchased November 5, 1903 (G); acquired 1903 (E). Building "continued to function as a home until, in the 1930s" restoration began (J). L (BYU) agrees the Church kept leasing it to tenants who gave occasional tours.
- [T1] 1939: at the urging of Bryant S. Hinckley, Northern States Mission president, partial restoration and a missionary couple to give tours (E, quoted).
- [T1] June 27 dedication of the renovated $1 million complex by President Gordon B. Hinckley, first counselor in the First Presidency; jail restored "as nearly as possible to 1844 standards"; visitors' center expanded (H; year from H's publication date, 1989, and I: "dedicated June 27, 1989").

On the Map

- [T1] Visitors' center address 310 Buchanan St; tour starts there; four rooms, the jailer's living area and a first-floor cell, a second-floor cell and the jailer's bedroom (C).
- [T1] Dee Jay Bawden statue on the plaza leading to the jail and visitors' center; originally planned as separate statues of each brother; Hyrum behind Joseph with an arm around him (I; B: "Hyrum standing behind Joseph, holding his arm"). Six slate tablets with quotations from Joseph and Hyrum, five on the plaza (H, I).
- [NPS] Carthage Courthouse Square Historic District, roughly bounded by Main, Adams, Wabash and Madison Streets (K). OSM places the Hancock County Courthouse on Main Street inside that square.

Trivia

- [T1] "It was used for about twenty-five years. Later it was used as a private residence and became one of the nicest homes in Carthage" (F).
- [T1] "Completed two years later at a cost of $4,105" (F). Purchase price $4,000 for the building and two acres (G).

## Dropped

- Graphic detail of the deaths (where each man was hit, Joseph's fall from the window beside the well, the second shot). In B, D and F; left out under the sensitivity rule. Copy states the deaths in D&C 135's own words plus the plainest facts from D.
- John Taylor's watch stopping (F gives 5:16). Tied to the wounding; left out of trivia per the brief.
- Carthage Greys and Governor Thomas Ford's promise of protection (D, F). Blame-adjacent and not needed; left out.
- "Falsely charged with treason" (B's wording). Copy says "charged there with treason," neutral.
- Trial being "a sham" (D attributes this to some scholars). Interpretive; left out. Copy states only the trial and acquittal.
- Who first offered the jail for sale: Elizabeth M. Browning (L, BYU) vs "Mrs. James Browning" (search lead). Not in a fetched T1 source; omitted.
- Purchase date November 3 (Ensign Peak Foundation, lead) vs November 5 (G). Used G.
- Joseph A. McRae, caretaker for about thirty years and in charge of restoration (J); his 1944 quote that the jail was "the oldest building in Carthage, and the only one built entirely of stone" (G). Verified, but "oldest" was a 1944 superlative; cut.
- When the Church bought it, the well had been filled in and a French window added (G). Verified; cut because the well is where Joseph fell.
- Bryant S. Hinckley as Gordon B. Hinckley's father. True, but no fetched source states it; omitted.
- Summer kitchen added about 1850, present one late 1930s (H). Verified; cut for length.
- Hamilton House inn (F). Site location not established; omitted.
- Courthouse construction date (1908 per Wikipedia and illinoisgenweb, leads only) and George W. Payne & Son as its architect (K names Payne for the district, not the courthouse alone). Not stated.
- 1994 sesquicentennial restoration (search summary only, not confirmed in a fetched T1 source). Omitted.
- Illinois State's role in an earlier restoration (J). Single-source detail; omitted.
