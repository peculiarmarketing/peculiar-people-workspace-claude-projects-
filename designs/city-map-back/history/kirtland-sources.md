## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Why Ohio: December 1830 revelation, "assemble together at the Ohio" (D&C 37:3, quoted on fetch); heading calls it the first commandment concerning a gathering in this dispensation. K, A. Cowdery, Pratt and others baptized many from Sidney Rigdon's congregations near Kirtland in late 1830. A.
- [T1] Headquarters: Kirtland "functioned as the headquarters of the Church" in the 1830s while Joseph Smith's family lived in Ohio; Joseph received "dozens of revelations" there. A (no years given; copy says "through most of the 1830s").
- [T1] Kirtland Camp: "A company of 515 souls, known as the Kirtland Camp, left Kirtland on the 6th of July, 1838"; "arrived in Adam-ondi-Ahman on the 4th of October." J (quoted on fetch).
- [T1] The Temple Since: 1880 court recognized Joseph Smith's heirs as holding title; "two decades later" the RLDS Church (later Community of Christ) secured ownership (by adverse possession); Community of Christ cared for the temple until March 5, 2024, when it transferred responsibility and ownership. B. Transfer dated Tuesday, March 5, 2024. H. First public tours by the Church Monday, March 25, 2024, at no charge. I, H.

What Happened Here

- [T1] Joseph and Emma arrived early February 1831 at the Whitney store. F gives February 4; L (Church News) gives "about the first of February." Copy uses "early February 1831" and datetime 1831-02. The Whitneys hosted them about four weeks in their home. G.
- [T1] D&C 41, February 4, 1831, Kirtland, calls Edward Partridge to be ordained a bishop (K); received in the Whitney home (G lists D&C 41 among four revelations there).
- [T1] School of the Prophets held in the store from January 24, 1833 until sometime in April 1833 (F); met in the upstairs room (F, G). Word of Wisdom received in the store after Joseph Smith inquired about men using tobacco in meetings (F); D&C 89 heading dated February 27, 1833, Kirtland (K).
- [T1] Cornerstone ceremony July 23, 1833, First Presidency (Joseph Smith, Sidney Rigdon, Frederick G. Williams) presiding; about three years of work. B.
- [T1] February 14, 1835 meeting to recognize Zion's Camp (Camp of Israel) members; the Three Witnesses "(apparently in consultation with Joseph Smith)" selected the Twelve; eight of twelve had been in the camp. C.
- [T1] Dedication March 27, 1836; prayer received by revelation, now D&C 109 (B, K). "About a thousand Saints were already in the room, and many more were crowding the front entrances." E. Evening meeting: men spoke in tongues; "Outside, Saints saw a bright cloud and a pillar of fire rest over the temple." E. Copy attributes the sighting as what Saints said they saw.
- [T1] April 3, 1836: Joseph and Oliver lowered the canvas curtains around the uppermost pulpit and knelt to pray (E); visions of Jehovah, Moses, Elias and Elijah, D&C 110 heading dated April 3, 1836 (K, B).
- [T1] Safety Society officially established November 2, 1836; ceased operations by August 1837; Panic of 1837; some investors including Church leaders questioned Joseph's prophetic calling; Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon left January 12, 1838; First Presidency moved to Missouri. D, A.

On the Map (frame lat 41.574 to 41.646, lon -81.381 to -81.309; coordinates from OpenStreetMap Nominatim, all inside)

- Kirtland Temple, 9020 Chillicothe Rd, 41.62529, -81.36215 (matches the brief's 41.6253, -81.3621). [T1] Stone from a quarry a few miles from the temple. B.
- Newel K. Whitney Store, 7800 Kirtland-Chardon Rd (F), 41.62925, -81.36144. [T1] Built 1826 by Whitney with Sidney Gilbert; post office, bishop's storehouse, early Church headquarters (G). Joseph Smith completed the Bible translation while living in the store, July 1833 (F).
- Newel K. and Elizabeth Ann Whitney Home, 41.62946, -81.36202 (about 50 m from the store). [T1] Smiths stayed about four weeks after arriving in 1831. G.
- Johnson Inn, 41.62906, -81.36202. [T1] First brick building in Kirtland; Church bought it in 1833; office of patriarch introduced there December 1833 when Joseph Smith ordained Joseph Smith Sr.; burned 1915, rebuilt where it stood. G.
- Sawmill, 41.62822, -81.36131; Ashery, 41.62826, -81.36108. [T1] Sawmill built to supply temple lumber; Joel H. Johnson, millwright, designed and built it in six months; logs floated down the Chagrin River; rebuilt on original foundations. Ashery owned by Bishop Whitney; potash and pearl ash sent east via the Erie Canal; rebuilt on original foundations. G.

Trivia

- [T1] Sidney Rigdon "spoke with great force for more than two hours." E.
- [T1] Dedicatory prayer, Hosanna Shout and a hymn by William W. Phelps became standard elements of later temple dedications. B.
- [T1] "Children gathered discarded shards of crockery and china for mixing into the stucco finish"; B footnote 4: "No known historical accounts describe early Latter-day Saints intentionally breaking china to decorate the walls."

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Site operation (tours, which buildings are open). Copy only states the 2024 transfer and first tour date, which will not change.

## Dropped

- Glass in the stucco. B mentions crockery and china only, not glass. Dropped "glassware."
- "Saints broke their best china for the temple." B says there is no known historical account of it. Used as a correction in trivia instead.
- Any revelation count. Sources disagree: A says "dozens" in Kirtland; G says 64 sections in Ohio "including two dozen in Kirtland" yet also 16 at the Whitney store alone; popular figures (46 and others) unsourced here. Copy uses A's "dozens" only.
- "I am Joseph the Prophet; you have prayed me here" and the Whitneys seen in vision. L presents it as family folklore, with wording that varies between versions. Omitted.
- Exact arrival day (February 1 vs February 4, 1831). Two Church sources disagree; copy says early February.
- Stone type. B says a limestone quarry; many secondary sources say sandstone. Unresolved without Backman or a geology source; copy says only "a quarry a few miles away."
- When the Smiths moved into the store. F says two years after arrival; G says they lived there 18 months and Joseph Smith III was born there (born November 1832, which fits neither neatly). Conflict; omitted, along with the birth.
- $192.5 million price of the 2024 transfer. H says only "an agreed-upon amount." Omitted. Nauvoo properties and Bible-translation manuscripts in the same transfer (H) left out as not Kirtland history.
- "Adverse possession" wording. Verified (B) but left as "secured title" for a neutral tone.
- Warren Parrish's splinter church and threats on Joseph's life (A, D). Verified but left out to avoid dwelling on the apostasy.
- "Pentecostal season" framing and claims of angels seen. E mentions both, but kept only the tongues and the cloud and pillar of fire as an attributed account; no Pentecost label in copy.
- Kirtland Camp alternate figures (529 people, 105 families, attrition to 260, 800 or 860 miles). Non-official or unfetched sources. Used J's 515.
- Edward Partridge as "first bishop." Not in the fetched heading text; copy says "as bishop."
- Isaac Morley farm, Kirtland North Cemetery, Joseph and Emma Smith Home. No coordinates found (Nominatim returned nothing) and no fetched T1 page for the first two; the Smith home is under renovation per G. Not listed.
- Kirtland Flats Schoolhouse (41.62851, -81.36029; original 1819 building burned in the 1860s, G). Inside the frame, but no Church history event tied to it in a fetched source. Cut.
- Visitors' center (41.62867, -81.36058). No event; cut.
- First tour attendance ("nearly 250"). Single Church News report via search snippet only; omitted.

New facts:

- [T1] Morley farm: Isaac Morley agreed to have a home built for Joseph and Emma on his farm; they moved in early March 1831 (N); lived there March to September 1831 (M); "as many as 13" revelations now in the D&C (M, N); in a small schoolhouse on the property 23 men including Joseph Smith were ordained high priests (M); present house is not the Morleys' or Smiths' home (M); "a small portion of the land is open to the public" (M). Address 8605 Chillicothe Rd (M).
- [T1] Joseph and Emma Smith Home: moved in late 1833; lived there "just over four years," longer than any other home they shared as a married couple; major portions of the Book of Abraham translated during their time there; Emma compiled a book of sacred hymns there (O). Address 8980 Chillicothe Rd (O).
- [T1] Kirtland North Cemetery: predates the Saints (earliest recorded burial 1827); Joseph Smith Jr.'s grandmother and Oliver Cowdery's parents among those buried; owned by the city of Kirtland (P). Copy does not name the grandmother (P does not).
- [T1] Stannard Quarry: supplied stone for the Kirtland Temple; inside the Chapin Forest Reservation; blocks split by hand-drilling holes and using wedges; Joseph Smith oversaw the quarrying for a short time; drill-hole remnants visible at the quarry pond (Q). Not owned by the Church (Q). The old temple li fact "Stone for its walls came from a quarry a few miles away" (B) moved here as "stone for the temple's walls."
- Stone type still omitted: Q itself says "local sandstone" in one sentence and "limestone blocks" in the next.
