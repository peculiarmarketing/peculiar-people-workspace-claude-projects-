Sacred Grove, New York

A Farm Out of Forest
About two miles south of Palmyra village, the Smiths made a first payment on 100 acres of thick woods. By the spring of 1820 they had cleared about 30 of them, and they left large stands of timber on the farm's east and west sides.

Two Houses
First a log home, a story and a half on an 18-by-30-foot foundation, finished in 1818 and soon holding ten people. Then a frame house roughly twice the size, which Alvin started building for his parents and didn't live to see finished. The family lived in it from 1825 to 1829, then moved back to the log home.

The Exact Spot
Unknown. Historians can't place the First Vision any closer than the wooded part of the farm. The old forest on the west side of today's historic site has gone by the name Sacred Grove since at least 1905.

What Happened Here

  • Spring 1820: Joseph, 14, went into the woods near the house to pray, probably close to ground the family was clearing. He later wrote that it was the first time he had tried to pray out loud. There, by his account, God the Father and Jesus Christ appeared to him.
  • September 21, 1823: In the middle of harvest, the angel Moroni came to Joseph three times in the night, in a bedroom of the log home he shared with his brothers. By the third visit it was nearly dawn.
  • September 1827: Joseph and Emma were living in the frame house when he brought the plates home. He hid them under the hearth bricks, then in the loft of the cooper's shop with the empty box buried under the floor. A mob tore up the floorboards and smashed the box, and never looked in the loft overhead.
  • 1907: The Church bought the farm, two years after President Joseph F. Smith visited it. It stayed a working farm until the 1980s.
  • March 27, 1998: President Gordon B. Hinckley dedicated a rebuilt log home on the original site, made from timber cut in a nearby woods.

On the Map

  • The log home stands on Stafford Road, rebuilt on its original foundation.
  • The frame house stands about 125 yards south. It has been restored, and so have the barn and the cooper's shop.
  • The grove lies west of the houses, across the farm, with paths through it.
  • The Palmyra New York Temple sits on a drumlin on the east side of the old farm, and President Hinckley dedicated it on April 6, 2000. Its grounds are edged by a rock wall Joseph and his brothers piled up while clearing fields. Construction crews moved the stones, and local youth put the wall back.

Trivia

  • The farm had as many as 1,500 maple trees, and each spring the Smiths turned the sap into sugar and molasses.
  • The grove's six "witness trees" were put at about 225 years old in 2010, one of them perhaps 300 to 350. Earlier caretakers had filled rotting trunks with cement; since 1998 fallen logs have been left to rot where they land, and wildflowers, woodpeckers and wild turkeys have come back.
