## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Then Called: South Bainbridge in Bainbridge Township, Chenango County (A, B: "South Bainbridge (later Afton), Chenango Co."). I: marker caption "South Bainbridge, now Afton, New York." E 1:58: "married at the house of Squire Tarbill, in South Bainbridge, Chenango county, New York."
- [T1] Josiah Stowell: born 1770 (H, B), in Winchester, New Hampshire (B); moved to Jericho (later Bainbridge) 1791 (B); farmer and sawmill owner in Chenango County (H). Witness for JS's defense at court proceedings in 1826 and 1830 (B), hence "stood by him in court twice." Home near the Susquehanna just southwest of the village (K; also visible in coordinates below).
- [T1] What Survives: Neely's docket book and the case documents are not extant; known texts come from later publications (Fraser's Magazine 1873, Utah Christian Advocate 1886); the outcome "necessarily remains elusive" (C). J agrees accounts of the outcome conflict.

What Happened Here

- [T1] November 1825: JS, his father and other men signed an agreement with Stowell in November 1825 (G; JSP appendix "Agreement of Josiah Stowell and Others, 1 November 1825" cited by G, not fetched). Joseph and his father hired (F). Spanish silver mine in Harmony, Susquehanna county; worked nearly a month; prevailed on Stowell to cease (E 1:56; E gives hiring in October 1825). JS moved to South Bainbridge to work on Stowell's farm; 28 miles from South Bainbridge to Harmony (G).
- [T1] March 20, 1826: appeared before Albert Neely, justice of the peace in Bainbridge, Chenango County, under the disorderly persons statute (C). Published docket names Peter G. Bridgman, Stowell's nephew (C); F: "Josiah's nephew ... brought the young man to court." Stowell "stated that Joseph had not swindled him" (F).
- [T1] January 18, 1827: Emma visited the Stowells' home in January 1827 and Joseph proposed there (F; G adds she also visited her sister in Colesville). Parents opposed (E 1:58, F). Date (E 1:57, F, G, I). Quote from E 1:58. Stowell helped the couple move to Manchester to live with Joseph's parents (H; G: "Stowell took them to Manchester").
- [T1] June 29, 1830: Constable Ebenezer Hatch arrested JS at the Knight home on the evening of 28 June, took him to South Bainbridge, where they spent the night at a tavern; Justice Joseph Chamberlin presided 29 June in Bainbridge Township; twelve witnesses testified, Josiah Stowell among them; JS's history and Reed say acquitted (D). G: "Joseph was acquitted at a trial in South Bainbridge."

On the Map

- [T1] Josiah Stowell Home, 323 NY-7, Afton; not owned or operated by the Church; docents give guided tours (H).
- [T3] Tarbill house site at the Afton fairgrounds: K (Manscill, citing Larry C. Trivia

- [T1] James Davidson and John S. Reed, "farmers who were not professional attorneys" (D).
- [T1] Jericho to Bainbridge: organized as Jericho in 1791, renamed Bainbridge in 1814 (A). Stowell moved to Jericho in 1791 (B).
- [T3] 1857 split and rename: N ("formed from Bainbridge November 18, 1857"); L ("seceded from Bainbridge in 1857 when it was renamed Afton"); O ("1857 South Bainbridge renamed Afton"). B (T1) confirms South Bainbridge became Afton.
- [T3] Named for Afton Water and Burns: M ("The name Afton came from the Scottish poem, 'Afton Water' by Robert Burns"); L ("Named after 'Flow Gently, Sweet Afton' a poem published in 1791 by Robert Burns"); N ("derives its name from Afton Water, a small river in Ayrshire ... immortalized by the Scottish poet Burns"; it says "Ayrshire, England," an error; Ayrshire is in Scotland). L and O may draw on the same county history as N; M and K are separate. Copy names the stream and the poem's first line only.

## Open doubt: where the 1826 hearing was held

The brief asked whether the 1826 hearing was in South Bainbridge. The sources do not settle it:
- C (JSP introduction) says Neely was a justice of the peace "in Bainbridge," and the trial record is headed "Bainbridge, NY." It does not say South Bainbridge.
- J (Church History Topics) says only that Joseph was ordered to appear in Chenango County.
- B (JSP Stowell biography) says Stowell was a defense witness "at court proceedings in South Bainbridge (later Afton), Chenango Co., 1826, 1830."
Bainbridge was then the name of the whole town, which included both villages. Copy states only that the record names Bainbridge and leaves the village open.
