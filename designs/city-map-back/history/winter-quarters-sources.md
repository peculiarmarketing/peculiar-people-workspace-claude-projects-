## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Whose Land: site near the Missouri in the Council Bluffs region under an agreement with the Omaha and Potawatomi tribes and U.S. Indian agents (A). Two-year limit on the Omaha land; residents had to leave by May 1848 (A, note 2). West bank of the Missouri (B heading).
- [T1] That First Winter: about 4,000 at Winter Quarters in winter 1846 to 1847; another 1,700 in Missouri, mostly St. Louis (A, note 3); nearly 3,000 remained in camps along the Iowa trail (A).
- [T1/T2] The Temple: dedicated April 22, 2001 by President Gordon B. Hinckley (G, J, K). On the south side of the Mormon Pioneer Cemetery, "immediately adjacent" (G); "on a hill beside" the cemetery (K). First temple in Nebraska (K, T2). Announced June 14, 1999 and groundbreaking November 28, 1999 (J, K) not used.

What Happened Here

- [T1] September 1846: surveying began September 19, first wagons reached the tableland September 23; plat loosely followed the City of Zion plat at half scale; December survey counted 538 log cabins and 83 soddies (C).
- [T1] Winter 1846 to 1847: food shortages, malnutrition and crowding spread disease (A); "black canker" (scurvy) and "ague" (E); scurvy, fever and chills (C). "Hundreds died" (G). "Mostly the very young and the old" (D).
- [T1] January 14, 1847: D&C 136 given through Brigham Young at Winter Quarters; "The Word and Will of the Lord" (v. 1); captains of hundreds, fifties and tens (v. 3) (B).
- [T1] April 5, 1847 Heber C. Kimball left first with six wagons; April 7 Brigham Young and most pioneers departed; April 16 company organized; 143 men, three women and two boys; 72 wagons (I). A: Young left April 7, 1847. O: company formed April 16, 1847. Copy says "two children" (I says boys; other Church sources say children).
- [T1] July 3, 1848 last wagons rolled out (C). Lease expiring, Saints moved across the river into Iowa and named their headquarters Kanesville (A).

On the Map

- [T1] Cemetery "on a rise just west of the Mormon Trail Center" (E); on the bluff (D). No gravestones intact from the early period; grave spots identified from the sexton's records and researchers' work (D).
- [T1] Fairbanks, "Tragedy at Winter Quarters," bronze, grieving parents who lost an infant child, dedicated September 20, 1936 by President Heber J. Grant (F). E: "a pioneer family enduring grief."
- [T1] Mormon Trail Center at Historic Winter Quarters dedicated by President Hinckley the evening of April 18, 1997 (H). Address 3215 State Street (Church location page via search; OSM confirms). City model; visitors can pull a handcart (D).

Trivia

- [T1] Death toll: Thomas L. Kane's 1850 figure "something over 600"; Lund's estimate 323 burials in this cemetery (304 identified in the sexton's records) and 433 across three cemeteries (C).
- [T1] Leased from Omaha for $1 a year since the mid-1930s; Mayor Hal Daub deeded it to the Church on April 1, 1999, for $1 (E; G also gives April 1, 1999). City council approved the transfer March 30, 1999 (E).
- [T1] Winter Quarters became Florence, Nebraska (A).
- [T3] James C. Mitchell platted a new town at the site of Winter Quarters (L, M, N). Annexed by Omaha in 1917 (L, M, N). Bank of Florence built 1856, 8502 North 30th Street, Florence neighborhood (L, NPS).

## Dropped

- Death toll as a single number. Church sources disagree: "some 600" and "about 600" (E, F), "over 300" (D), "more than 325 unmarked graves" (G), a plaque with 350 names (E). C traces the 600 to Kane and estimates far fewer. Copy says "hundreds" and puts the disagreement in Trivia as an open question with both sides attributed.
- Newsroom (D) says the temple was dedicated in 2002. Error; G, J, K all give April 22, 2001.
- Cemetery dedicated 1931 by Heber J. Grant (G). Single mention, and a different Church News piece says the Fairbanks monument was 1930 rather than 1936. Used F's 1936 for the monument; left the 1931 cemetery date out.
- The gristmill. C dates the Saints' mill to March 20, 1847. Whether today's Florence Mill on North 30th Street is that building is disputed in local sources; no Church or NPS source fetched settles it. Omitted.
- Florence's namesake (granddaughter vs niece Florence Kilbourn) and founding year (1854 vs 1855). Sources disagree; copy names Mitchell only.
- Exact annexation day (May 25, June 1, June 6, 1917 across sources). Copy says 1917.
- Florence as territorial capital for a week in January 1858 (search summary of Wikipedia only). Not verified.
- Mormon Bridge and Mormon Bridge Road. In frame (OSM 41.333, -95.992), but no fetched source on the name. Omitted.
- 23 wards and 700 homes (D) vs Lund's 538 cabins plus 83 soddies and a ceiling of about 700 cabins (C). Used C's December count.
- Mormon Battalion (July 1846). Mustered on the Iowa side, before Winter Quarters existed; outside the frame.
- Council house dedicated January 23, 1847 (C). Verified; cut for length.
- Kanesville First Presidency, December 1847. Iowa side, outside the frame.
- Fetch failures: history.nebraska.gov (Florence and the Winter Quarters marker) and omahamagazine.com both returned 403. Not worked around. history.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/trek/pioneer-story-winter-quarters returned 404.
