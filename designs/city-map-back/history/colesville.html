Colesville, New York

The Township
Colesville split off from Windsor in 1821 and had about 2,400 people by 1830. Harpursville, Nineveh and Colesville were its villages, and the Susquehanna River ran through its eastern side.

Joseph Knight Sr.
A farmer and miller who moved his family in 1811 to the part of Windsor that later became Colesville. He hired Joseph Smith as a day laborer in 1826, and later, while the Book of Mormon was being translated in Harmony, he brought Joseph food and paper.

The Branch
The Knights and their neighbors became one of the Church's first branches, possibly the very first. Hyrum Smith presided over it for several months from late 1830. Newel Knight, Joseph Sr.'s son, led it after him.

What Happened Here

  • April 1830: Days after the Church was organized, Joseph stopped at the Knight farm and preached to the family and their friends. Newel Knight came back from praying alone in the woods seized by a fit. Joseph commanded the devil, in the name of Jesus Christ, to depart from him, and the fit stopped. Joseph called it the first miracle performed in the Church.
  • June 28, 1830: Joseph and others had dammed a stream for a baptism, and someone tore the dam out overnight. Early on the 28th the believers rebuilt it, and Oliver Cowdery baptized thirteen people in the pool, Emma Smith and Joseph and Polly Knight among them. Men on the bank jeered, then followed the group back to the Knight house, where they grew into a crowd of about fifty.
  • June 30, 1830: Arrested that evening and acquitted in Chenango County the next day, Joseph was arrested again by a Broome County constable and held overnight at a Colesville tavern. Justice Joel K. Noble and at least two other justices heard the case and discharged him.
  • April 1831: Told by revelation to gather to Ohio, the branch sold what it could and set out together, poor families included. In Ohio they were sent to Leman Copley's 759-acre farm in Thompson. Copley soon went back on his offer and evicted them.
  • July 26, 1831: About sixty branch members reached Kaw Township in Jackson County, Missouri, roughly a thousand miles from Thompson. On August 2, Joseph and twelve men of the branch laid the first log of the first house in Zion. Within the week Polly Knight had died, the first Church member to die in Zion.

On the Map

  1. Nineveh is probably where Joseph worked for Joseph Knight Sr. in 1826 and 1827. Sources say only that the work was likely near Nineveh, so the marker shows the hamlet, not a known farm or building.
  2. The Joseph Knight Sr. Home sits half a mile south of Nineveh. The Church doesn't own or run it, and docents give tours. The June 1830 baptisms were in a dammed stream that ran through the Knight farm or close by. No source names the stream, and where it ran isn't known.
  3. Nathaniel Cole's tavern stood near where Colesville Road now meets Watrous Road, and pieces of its foundation remain. BYU historians call it the probable place of Joseph's June 30, 1830 trial. Church sources say only "a Colesville tavern," so this spot isn't certain.

Trivia

  • Joseph Knight considered Joseph Smith "the best hand he ever hired."
  • When snow made the road to Harmony hard going, Joseph sometimes borrowed the Knights' sleigh to go courting Emma.
  • Peter, James and John, an 1842 letter of Joseph's says, were heard "in the wilderness between Harmony, Susquehanna county, and Colesville, Broome county, on the Susquehanna river." Scripture gives no closer location than that. It names a river, not a spot.
