## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Orson Pratt and Erastus Snow first pioneers into the valley, 21 July 1847, "riding together on one horse." B. Saints C confirms Pratt and Snow had set up the base camp and begun plowing before Brigham arrived.
- [T1] 22 July: main company caught up with the vanguard and the combined wagons descended and camped near 1700 South and 500 East. B.
- [T1] 23 July: moved to the south branch of City Creek; Pratt's prayer; "consecrated and dedicated the land to the Lord"; "At noon the plowing began." B (quoting Thomas Bullock).
- [T1] Surveyors Orson Pratt and Henry Sherwood, grid inspired by Joseph Smith's plat of the City of Zion, temple space at the centre. A. Ten-acre blocks, eight-rod streets, "beginning at Temple Square." B (28 July 1847 entry).
- [T1] 22 August 1847, the Brethren sustained the names "The Great Salt Lake City" and "The Western Jordan" for the river west of the city. B.
- [T1] Church's worldwide headquarters remain in Salt Lake City. A. Church Administration Building built 1917 to house the Church President's offices and other senior leaders. F.

What Happened Here

- [T1] 24 July 1847: Brigham "too feverish and weak to walk," lying in Woodruff's carriage. C. "It is enough. This is the right place," ... "Drive on." C, whose note for the quote cites "Pioneers' Day," Deseret Evening News, 26 July 1880 (Woodruff's 1880 Pioneer Day account), alongside Woodruff's journal and his 1888 discourse. The 1888 Woodruff account (Deseret Evening News, 25 July 1888) gives "Drive on down into the valley, this is our abiding place." B. The copy attributes both lines to Woodruff's later recollections, not to a contemporary record. Attribution of "right place" specifically to the 1880 telling is my reading of C's note, which pairs the 1880 article with that quote; B confirms the 1888 version is the "abiding place" wording.
- [T1] 28 July 1847: council of the Twelve walked "to about the centre between the two creeks"; "Here is the forty acres for the Temple"; at 8 p.m. the whole camp met on the temple ground and passed the votes; the brethren voted to lay out the city in ten-acre blocks with eight-rod streets beginning at Temple Square. B (Woodruff, Journal History 28 July 1847).
- [T1] Cane: Saints places it on Monday 26 July 1847, "he stopped suddenly and thrust his cane into the ground. 'Here shall stand the temple of our God'"; Woodruff marked the spot with a sagebrush branch. C (citing Woodruff journal 26 July 1847). B also files Woodruff's 1888 cane recollection under 26 July. Copy says "two days earlier" than 28 July, i.e. 26 July. See Dropped for the 28 July version.
- [T1] Crickets late May 1848; fought with "brooms, brushes, sticks, and ropes"; "at least a month"; California gulls in early June; "consumed enough to mitigate the damage"; called the "Miracle of the Gulls." E.
- [T1] 26 June 1858 army marched into Salt Lake City; "around eight thousand people had lived in Salt Lake City" before the move south; "Midway through June, only around fifteen hundred people remained"; temple foundation buried, troops "saw what looked like a plowed field." D. Utah War framing and the move south: O-adjacent topic "Utah War" (https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/topics/utah-war) and D.
- [T1] First general conference in the Tabernacle 6 to 9 October 1867; last there October 1999; first Conference Center session 1 April 2000; Conference Center dedicated 8 October 2000. J; K ("first conference was held in the Tabernacle in October 1867"); U (8 Oct 2000).
- [T1] Utah Central Railroad completion ceremony 10 January 1870; "The last spike was driven by President Young"; Woodruff: "Some 12 to 15,000" at the depot; line Ogden to Salt Lake, about 40 miles. I. Transcontinental completed 10 May 1869 at Promontory Summit; Saints workers began the Salt Lake extension about a week later. H; Saints vol. 2 ch. 24.
- [T1] Temple cornerstones 6 April 1853 (groundbreaking 14 February 1853); dedicatory prayer by Wilford Woodruff 6 April 1893. G. "40 years to the day after its cornerstones" is arithmetic from G.

On the Map (positions checked against the frame lat 40.568 to 40.832, lon -112.005 to -111.745; coordinates from OpenStreetMap)

- Temple Square / Salt Lake Temple, 40.7704, -111.8919 (temple point given in the brief). Inside. [T1] Lot chosen 28 July 1847 (B). [T1] Stone from Little Cottonwood Canyon, hauled "20 miles (32 kilometers) ... by wagon teams" during "the first 20 years of construction"; "arrival of railroads in the 1870s provided a faster and more reliable way to transport stone." G. H adds that an 1871 line "facilitated the hauling of stone from Little Cottonwood Canyon." [Gov't, plus known-myths.md] Quartz monzonite of the Little Cottonwood stock, from the Temple Quarry. R. G itself says "local granite"; the house standard (known-myths.md) is quartz monzonite, which R supports.
- Tabernacle, 40.7704, -111.8931. Inside. [T1] Cornerstones July 1864; regular meetings from 1867; dedicated October 1875. F, K.
- Assembly Hall, 40.7698, -111.8933. Inside. [T1] Construction began 1877, finished 1882; stands where the Old Tabernacle (1851 to 1852) was built. F.
- Beehive House, 40.7697, -111.8886; Lion House, 40.7698, -111.8890. Inside. [T1] Beehive House first built 1855, Brigham Young's primary residence; Lion House constructed 1856. F.
- Ensign Peak, 40.7944, -111.8907. Inside (north edge of frame is 40.832). [T1] Climbed 26 July 1847 by Brigham Young and a small party (eight others per C; B lists seven named men plus Young); named after Isaiah's ensign (Isa. 11:12). B, C.
- Pioneer Park, 40.7617, -111.9011. Inside. [Gov't x2 + T1 + T3] Old Pioneer Fort Site on the National Register (ref. 74001938, 1847), located at Pioneer Park between 3rd and 4th South and 2nd and 3rd West. P. "Within a month of their arrival ... 29 log homes were built around Pioneer Park, making up the Old Fort"; site of the first school in the valley; dedicated as Pioneer Park 24 July 1898. Q. Saints S places a "square-shaped fort" about a mile southwest of the temple block and says a fort was begun within a month. Deseret News, 10 Nov 2003 (Tier 3 corroboration, seen in search results), identifies the fort site with Pioneer Park.
- This Is the Place Monument, 40.7520, -111.8162. Inside (east edge is -111.745). [T1] Dedicated 24 July 1947 by President George Albert Smith; sculptor Mahonri Young, grandson of Brigham Young. L. [Gov't] At the mouth of Emigration Canyon; Orson Pratt and Erastus Snow "at the base of the center column." M.

## Dropped

- "This is the right place" as Brigham Young's words recorded on 24 July 1847. It is Wilford Woodruff's later recollection (1880 telling, per C's note); his 1888 telling has "this is our abiding place" (B). Kept, but framed as Woodruff's memory with both versions.
- Brigham Young arriving first, or the whole company arriving on July 24. Pratt and Snow entered 21 July; the main company 22 to 23 July and began plowing 23 July (B, C). Copy separates the dates.
- Cane-marking dated 28 July 1847. Saints (C) and the Ensign log (B) put the cane on 26 July; 28 July is the council and camp vote on the temple lot. Copy uses 28 July for the vote and 26 July for the cane.
- Temple lot "forty acres." The 28 July vote was for forty acres (B), but Temple Square is far smaller today and no fetched Tier 1 source gives the change. Acreage left out.
- Seagulls "saving the harvest" or "eating every cricket." E says the gulls mitigated the damage and the infestation lingered weeks more; frosts and irrigation worried farmers as much. Copy follows E. Seagull Monument dedication date (often 1913) not fetched; monument not mentioned.
- Gulls circling the temple spires during the 6 April 1893 dedication storm (Saints vol. 2 ch. 44, citing Deseret Evening News). Verified as a contemporary report but reads as a sign story; left out.
- "The temple took 40 years" stated loosely. Copy says dedication was 40 years to the day after the cornerstones (6 April 1853 to 6 April 1893, G). The "45 years, 8 months, 9 days" figure is a listed myth.
- "Granite hauled by oxen" for the whole build. G says wagon teams for the first 20 years and railroads from the 1870s; oxen not named. Copy says wagon teams, then rail. "Granite" replaced by quartz monzonite (R, known-myths.md).
- Utah Central completion described as reaching Little Cottonwood or "the quarry." H says only that an 1871 line facilitated hauling. Copy says rail took over in the 1870s.
- "The Tabernacle was built without nails" / pegs and rawhide roof. K says nails and washers were forged locally and bolts shipped from the East; it mentions wooden pegs placed cross-grain but not rawhide. Rawhide is only on Wikipedia and photo-caption sites. The no-nails claim is refuted by K and the corrected fact is used as trivia; rawhide dropped.
- Tabernacle organ waterwheel: listed myth; not used.
- Utah Central last-spike inscription ("Holiness to the Lord") and "homemade iron." In I but wording varies across non-official sources; cut for length.
- Statehood telegram clock time. N says 9:13 a.m.; a Talmage account (search result only) says 8:03 a.m. Conflict; no time given.
- Tabernacle flag dimensions (158 by 75 ft vs 150 by 75 ft). Conflict between non-official sources; omitted.
- Old Fort construction start date. NRHP nomination (via search) says 9 August 1847 with walls done by 1 September; the site marker says foundation stones 10 August and completion in November. Conflict; copy says "within a month of arriving" (Q) only.
- Army entry date given as "June 1" on the Wilford Woodruff Papers timeline. D (Saints) gives 26 June 1858, which is also the widely reported date; Saints wins.
- "30,000 Saints" in the move south (Woodruff Papers timeline). Not in a Tier 1 source fetched; copy uses Saints' Salt Lake City figures (8,000 and 1,500) instead.
- Name shortened from Great Salt Lake City to Salt Lake City (often given as 1868). No Tier 1 source fetched; omitted.
- Brigham Young's death in the Lion House (1877). Not fetched from a Tier 1 source; omitted. Lion House described only by its 1856 date (F also says it was built as a home for Brigham Young's wives; left out as not needed).
- Mountain Meadows Massacre and other Utah War violence: happened elsewhere, out of scope per brief.
- Address grid trivia (street numbers counting from the temple block). Not sourced to Tier 1 on fetch; omitted.
- Assembly Hall listed as its own landmark: folded into the Tabernacle item to keep the list at six.
- Endowment House (1855 to 1889, F): demolished, nothing on the map; omitted.
