## Verified

Spec rows

- [T1] Ray County Seat: platted as county seat 1827; population in 1840 about 500; "location of courthouse and jails"; about sixty Saints held here awaiting the hearing by Judge Austin A. King, November 1838 (C).
- [T1] The Charges: treason, murder, arson, robbery, burglary and larceny (A). Criminal court of inquiry, or preliminary hearing, to decide whether evidence sufficed for a full trial (B).

What Happened Here

- [T1] Arrived in Richmond November 9 after five days in Independence; JS and companions held in "an old log house"; forty-six other defendants in the unfinished Ray County Courthouse (B). Each man with a shackle around his ankle and a chain binding him to the others; hard cold floor, no fire (A).
- [T1] Rebuke: told by Parley P. Pratt (A, citing Pratt's 1853 letter and Autobiography; E: "the famous incident reported by Parley P. Pratt"). Guards "boasting of atrocities committed against the Mormons" (E; F similar). Pratt wanted to rebuke them and kept silent; Joseph rose in chains; quoted words; guards lowered their guns, begged his pardon, and were silent until their replacements came (A). No exact night given in A; copy dates it November 1838.
- [T1] Hearing November 12 (A, B) through November 29 (JSP document "Minutes and Testimonies, 12 to 29 November 1838 [State of Missouri v. Gates et al. for Treason]", fetched: https://www.josephsmithpapers.org/paper-summary/minutes-and-testimonies-12-29-november-1838-state-of-missouri-v-gates-et-al-for-treason/6 ; A says "more than two weeks"). More than forty prosecution witnesses; defense "a few witnesses" (A). Five Saints including Pratt jailed in Richmond on murder charges tied to Crooked River; Joseph, Hyrum, Rigdon, Wight, Baldwin and McRae sent to the Liberty jail to await trial on treason (A).
- [T1] Oliver Cowdery died March 3, 1850 (J); died in Richmond at the home of Peter and Mary Musselman Whitmer, David's parents (D, quoting Richard E. Turley Jr.); rebaptized at Kanesville, Iowa, November 12, 1848; visited his in-laws at Richmond intending to go west, contracted tuberculosis there (D); tuberculosis also in G.
- [T1] David Whitmer died January 25, 1888 (J); Richmond his residence for the last four decades of his life (E); "last surviving member of the Three Witnesses" interviewed in Richmond by Orson Pratt and Joseph F. Smith on September 7, 1878 (D).

On the Map

- [T1] Bronze statue of Alexander W. Doniphan in front of the Ray County Courthouse; he refused the illegal order to execute Joseph Smith (E, F; I says he "challenged the order"); Joseph Smith's attorney (E; B names Doniphan and Amos Rees as counsel); statue honors him for heroism in the 1846 war with Mexico and civic deeds (E). Copy says the statue was "put up for his service in the war with Mexico in 1846" (E: "honors him ... for his heroism in the 1846 war with Mexico and his civic-minded deeds").
- [T1] Oliver Cowdery buried in Pioneer Cemetery; President Joseph F. Smith directed that a monument be erected near Oliver's grave; Junius F. Wells located the then-unmarked grave in August 1911 (D). Dedication service in the Ferris Opera House with the Tabernacle Choir, Elder Heber J. Grant presiding (D). Church has maintained the cemetery since 1949 (E).
- [T1] David Whitmer buried in the city cemetery (D, E, F). Austin A. King's grave is "the most prominent monument in the cemetery"; he later became Missouri governor and was elected to Congress (E). West Main Street location is from Ensign Peak Foundation (lead) and is used only as a street name.
- [T1] "Mormon History Room" at the Ray County Historical Society and Museum, refurbished and reopened May 30, 2010 (E).

Trivia

- [T1] Judge King: "Write that down ... It is a strong point for treason." Attorney: "you had better make the Bible treason." (A, citing Pratt, History of the Late Persecution.)
- [T1] Four-sided monument; names of Cowdery, Whitmer and Harris on respective panels; Joseph Smith's name on the remaining side, since he was with them in 1829; granite from the same Vermont quarry as the Joseph Smith monument (D). The Liberty Church News piece of the same date names the quarry as Barre (see liberty-sources.md, source F).

## Volatile (re-check before reprint)

- Ray County Museum room (2010 refurbishment). Exhibits can close or change.
- Church maintenance of the Pioneer Cemetery (lease since 1949; Ensign Peak says a fifty-year lease, lead only).

## Dropped

- Exact dedication date of the Three Witnesses Monument. D (Church News 2011, quoting the assistant Church historian and a dated photo caption) says November 27, 1911. Ensign Peak Foundation says November 22; a Mormon Historical Studies caption (via search) says November 21. Copy says "that November." Evan may expect a day; it is genuinely unsettled across sources.
- Contents of the casket sealed under the monument (Ensign Peak, lead only; no Church page fetched lists them).
- Witnesses who testified for the prosecution after striking deals (John Corrill, William Phelps, John Whitmer) (A). Verified; omitted to stay neutral about people who left the Church.
- Sampson Avard's testimony and the Danites (A, I). Verified; left out as it needs more context than the format allows.
- The guards' specific boasts (rape and killing) (A). Left out as graphic; copy uses "atrocities" per E.
- Cowdery's excommunication details and the reasons (G). Copy states only the 1848 rebaptism. David Whitmer's 1838 departure from the Church (H) not mentioned in copy.
- Jacob Whitmer, one of the Eight Witnesses, buried in the Richmond city cemetery (liberty-sources.md source F, Baugh quoted in Church News). Verified; cut for length.
- Rededication of the monument for its centennial. Ensign Peak says November 19, 2021, which is wrong on its face; D shows a commemoration on November 19, 2011. Not used.
- Doniphan statue sculptor and date. Not in fetched sources.
- The courthouse standing today is not the 1838 building (the 1838 one was unfinished, B). Copy does not claim the present courthouse is the one used in 1838.
- "Richmond is some 40 miles east of Independence" (E). Not needed.
