## PRINCIPLES

- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): "Optical weight is how human eyes perceive the size of an object, and it doesn't necessarily equal its pixel size."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): Perceived size ranking at equal dimensions: square looks "Biggest", rounded squares progressively smaller, triangle in the middle, circle "Smallest".
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): Overshoot: "When curved and pointed shapes/letters are drawn past the capline or baseline to achieve an optical effect of being the same size." Shown on the triangle apex, the circle top and bottom, and the letters A and O against a flat H.
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): "Non-square shapes need to be higher and wider to be optically balanced with squarish shapes."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): "If we directly inscribe icons into square areas, the more square-like icons will look larger."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): "Compensate the weight of differently shaped icons by allowing visually smaller icons to hang beyond the icon area." "Leave some space between visually heavier icons and the icon area."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): "Areas for icons should have some space reserved for optical balancing."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): Optical adjustment matters "To create a visually appealing piece", "To reinforce understanding of the message", and because "Without optical adjustments, reader can be impeded from comprehension."
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): Applies to logos side by side: two wordmarks on the same capline and baseline look unequal when one has a round O; the round one is scaled up to balance.
- SINGLE SOURCE (reader A, slide): Applies to UI: a circular button beside a rectangular field is made larger than the field height so it overshoots top and bottom.
