# Claude pass (reader A): Best Practices for Setting Up a 1-Color Screen Printing Job

Source: source/transcript.txt (auto captions) plus 5 storyboard sheets (160x90, every ~5 s). Ryonet, part of the "how to screen print with a kit, the 150 Edition" course. Press: Riley Hopkins 150 4x1. Content is press and workspace setup, not artwork design.

## PRINCIPLES

- Print head clamp knobs "allow you to adjust the screen left to right and front to back this allows you to align the design to the perfect location on the shirt" [00:32]
- Off contact defined: "off contact is the distance between the screen and the [platen]" [00:32]
- Setting off contact: "place about four cleanup cards between the platen and printhead to create that space ... let the screen level out naturally as it rests on the cleanup cards once it's aligned tighten the bolts again" [01:04]; "you should be able to see and feel an even space between the screen and platen" [01:04]
- Keep off contact consistent: "it may be a good idea to place a shim on the front edge of the screen frame a washer or quarter works great for this" [01:35]
- Registration gates: "make sure the print head fits snugly into the registration gate" [01:35]
- Platens: wood is "most affordable ... sturdy and durable but are prone to warp over time"; aluminum has "a thinner profile and offer more advantages" [02:05]
- Platen placement: "if you're too close to the registration gate the screen won't be aligned"; on multi-station presses "measure to make sure the platens are the same distance away from the registration gate" [02:35]
- Registration lines: "draw a line down the center of the platen this will be your main reference point to make sure the shirt is lined up with the center of the design on the screen" [03:06]; horizontal lines "will help line the collar of the shirt up in the right place" [03:06]
- Platen tape: without it platens "get sticky and dirty with every job"; "trim the platen tape cleanly instead of tucking it under the platen tucking the tape ... creates places where the shirt can catch or snag" [03:36]-[04:09]
- Tape screens: "you want the ink to only pass through your design instead of the edges of the screen that don't have Emulsion"; use "specific screen printing tape", other tapes "can break down when introduced to chemicals or leave a sticky residue" [04:09]-[04:40]
- Taping hack: lay tape all clockwise or all counterclockwise so "you can pull it off all in one go" [05:10]
- Workspace: keep ink, adhesive, "a laser temp gun", towels, spatulas, squeegees on a cart within reach; flash dryer close enough to "swivel it when it's time to flash or cure a shirt"; press and conveyor dryer close together [05:10]-[05:42]
