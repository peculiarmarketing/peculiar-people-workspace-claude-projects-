- "you're also making the H very hard to read. Somebody might look at that and see it as a symbol ... and then read it as Udlam. So, legibility should supersede any kind of decorative element that you do." [01:35]
- "please please please make sure it reads. Reading the your word mark on your logo is very important. It supersedes all the other decisions that you're making." [02:05 to 02:35]
- Type pairing: "the type pairing with the mark feels a little bit off ... the lockup is important. Here's where I'm thinking you can play around with mixing two weights where you do book in a lighter weight and then worm is in a bolder typeface." [02:35] "Break it up." [03:07]
- "You don't want to illustrate what it does ... It's it's abstract, but it's clear what it is." [03:07 to 03:38]
- "you can't own a triangle. You can't own a square or trapezoid cuz it's for everybody to use." [03:38]
- "Always work in black and white. No colors, no textures. This will make it easier for you to focus on the important aspects of a logo. Things like hierarchy, contrast, and legibility." [04:10]
- "As you design your logo, you are making lots of little decisions, chopping letters, changing fonts, etc. It's easy to get carried away to the point in which the mark becomes really difficult to read. The solution? Always ask yourself, is it legible? Does it read well? Legibility should supersede every other consideration." [04:41]
- Critique method ("peeling an onion"): "I start by simply describing what I'm seeing ... The typeface, the shapes, the curves, the points, the line weights, etc. No judgments. Then I start to notice how certain things make me feel ... Finally ... I can start sharing ideas" [05:12]
