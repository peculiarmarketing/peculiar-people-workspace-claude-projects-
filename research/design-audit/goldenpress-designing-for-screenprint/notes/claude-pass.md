## PRINCIPLES

- Work in vector: "you're going to want to be working in adobe illustrator or another vector-based program ... it doesn't matter how large you ... stretch your design or how small you shrink your design down it's always gonna have perfect resolution and when it comes to screen printing we want our artwork as crisp and as clean as possible" [01:05]-[01:36]
- Tip 1, expand fonts: "we want to turn it into a shape instead of just being a font ... so everyone can use this and work with it on any computer" [02:36]-[03:08]; method "object ... expand ... have object and fill checked ... hit okay" [03:08]; reason: a printer without the font "can't open it" [03:38]
- Tip 2, expand strokes: a stroked circle scaled up does not keep its proportions; "as i make it bigger and bigger ... now it's it's not the same it's kind of changed its its shape" [04:39]; after Object > Expand "it's going to maintain its width" relative to the shape [05:09]; "we don't want uh to switch up a design or change it we want to maintain exactly its shape" [05:42]
- Preference for solid shapes: "i like the final product to always just be solid shape just because strokes and those kind of things can get hairy at times" [06:12]
- Variable-width strokes: Expand is greyed out, use "expand appearance" [06:12]; unexpanded, when scaled down "we don't want it to get all fat and weird we want it to maintain its actual shape" [06:42]
- Tip 3 (stated as personal): "i strongly feel that you should not use clipping masks in adobe illustrator and that's pertaining to screen printing design" [07:13]; "clipping masks often will cause problems and they're confusing" [07:13]; replacement: draw a box, Pathfinder "minus front" to punch the shape, then minus front again with the texture compound path [09:46]-[10:16]; "just take the proper steps to make your file ... just a solid shape" [10:47]
- Tip 4: "know how many colors the screen printer you're using can print" [10:47]; ask the printer before designing [13:53]
- Tip 5: "give screen printers high resolution artwork preferably vectored images" [13:53]; JPEG/PNG pixelated files are unusable [14:23]
- Bonus: placing a JPEG inside an Illustrator file and saving it "doesn't mean that it's a proper illustrator file" [19:05]
- Goal state: "i just have this nice clean shape of a file it's just like bam all i got to do click on it make it black we're good to go" [19:35]
