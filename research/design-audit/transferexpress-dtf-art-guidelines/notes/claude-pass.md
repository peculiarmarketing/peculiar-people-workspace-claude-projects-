# Claude pass (reader A): Art Guidelines for DTF and Screen Printed Transfers

Source: source/transcript.txt (clean captions with [music] tags) plus 6 storyboard sheets (160x90, every ~5 s). Speaker: Dave, Transfer Express (STAHLS'). Process: screen printed plastisol heat transfers ("Goof-Proof") and DTF ("UltraColor Max"), both applied with a heat press. Not direct screen printing on garments.

## PRINCIPLES

- What minimums mean: "the minimum line weight or text size in your artwork file that you could print on your apparel. So, the finished print matches how you see it on screen. This is also going to account for our show-through areas or negative space. So, these areas don't disappear completely in those final prints." [01:02]
- Font stems count as lines: "this applies to font sizing, too, with the stem or line of each character no thinner than 1 point" [01:02]-[01:32]
- Below-minimum lines may print but fail durability: "our 0.5 point line, it looks pretty good printed, and it did in fact print. However, we may run into some durability issues where it's not going to meet that standard of 50-plus wash-dry cycles" [02:33]-[03:04]
- Font choice changes the safe size: "The actual size of your text will differ depending on the specific font you use as some are thicker or thinner than others" [03:35]
- Counters close up on screen-print transfers: "you're going to notice this the most with letters like P or O here closing up into solid areas of ink" [03:35]-[04:05]
- Knocked-out (reversed) text needs to be much larger than positive text: "as knocked-out text, we lose a lot of the text, pretty much all the way to ... 18 point font is really the smallest I feel safe going where we could actually read the words. You might even want to go larger." [04:05]
- Press pressure spreads plastisol ink into gaps: "with increased pressure on press ... it may cause the inks to spread a little bit more when you're on your heat press, and that's going to cause it to completely fill in. And that's why there's a clear set standard of 2.8 points where it's never going to fill in." [04:35]
- Triage by importance: "You're going to want to ensure that text is readable, of course, but say for some fine detail, it might not be mission-critical ... it may be okay if you miss a few seeds in a strawberry" [05:06]
- DTF ink does not spread: "the inks do not spread like our plastisol screen printing inks do under the pressure of the heat press ... it's a little bit more like heat transfer vinyl where it's just going straight down. Now, this allows us to achieve finer show-through areas" [05:37]-[06:07]; "much better definition in small characters, for example, here in our knocked-out text" [06:07]
- But DTF minimum line weight is larger than screen print: "our minimum with DTF transfers is 1.29 points" [06:07]; below it "we're actually going to have some breakup when we just start rubbing it with our finger" and "wash durability issues" [06:07]-[06:37]
- Mechanism for DTF thin-line failure: "the adhesive needs ink to hold on to, along with a white backing to ensure colors are fully opaque on light or dark garments ... the fine lines and small text on our black ink transfer here aren't thick enough to have white printed behind them, and that means they're probably not going to have an adequate amount of adhesive on them" [06:37]-[07:08]; "they break down with just rubbing, and they're definitely going to wear prematurely" [07:39]
- Fine details may be acceptable, text is not: "if it's just a little corner here or there, most likely it's going to be completely fine ... if it's text or important elements that are being flagged ... it might be time to modify your artwork" [08:10]

## NUMBERS

- Screen print line minimum: "general industry minimums for screen printing are 1 point for line thickness" [01:02]; "1 point or 0.012 inches, the screen printing industry standard" [02:33]
- Font equivalent: "that's right around 16 points with most fonts when you're printing on fabric" [01:32] (on-screen overlay at 1:36 reads "16 POINTS")
- Test chart: "line weights from 0.5 point to 15 point" [02:02]; halftone drawdown "starting at 2 points for the dot size" [02:33]
- Below-minimum test line: "0.5 point" [02:33]
- Durability standard: "50-plus wash-dry cycles" [03:04]
- Positive text on their chart: "eight or 9 point font is right where we're clean and clear using a standard Arial typeface" [03:04] (chart labelled "Arial Regular Bold", sizes from small up to "48 Point Font", readable on storyboard)
- Screen print minimum show-through (negative space): "2.8 points or 0.04 inches" [03:35]; repeated "a clear set standard of 2.8 points where it's never going to fill in" [04:35]
- Below-minimum show-through: "1.5 point" (gap not visible in print, "almost closed up" on the white version) [04:35]
- Knocked-out text: "18 point font is really the smallest I feel safe going" [04:05]
- DTF line minimum: "1.29 points" [06:07]
- DTF minimum show-through: not stated in the transcript; only "finer show-through areas" [05:37]. Value: not given (chart value UNREADABLE).
- Chart row labels in inches (e.g. next to show-through rows and line rows, 4:19 to 4:52, 6:04 to 6:24): partly visible, look like "1.5 pt | .02"", "2.8 pt | .04"", "5 pt | .06"" for show-through and "1 pt | .012"", "1.29 pt | .017"" for lines, but at 160x90 these are not reliable: UNREADABLE beyond what is spoken.
- Heat press display at 4:38 to 4:43: digits "362" then "004" readable, with a small label (likely pressure) UNREADABLE. Speaker refers to "a Hotronix press with that exact number" without saying it [04:35].
- Paid art help: "a $26 charge for any additional file help" for DTF [08:40]; free for screen printed transfers.
