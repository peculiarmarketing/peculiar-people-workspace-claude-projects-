# Etsy

Research date 3 September 2026.

**Why this platform was added.** It is not a social platform and it does not belong in a tier list of feeds. It earns a file for one reason: it is the only place I found where this exact product category already sells in public, with prices, shop ages, sales counts and review counts visible to anyone without logging in. Four prior rounds of research could not answer whether a buyer exists for Latter-day Saint temple art at a premium price. Etsy answers it, for free, in about fifteen minutes. It is also, incidentally, a real distribution channel that the brand does not currently use.

## 1. Who is actually there

**Company-reported.** Etsy Marketplace gross merchandise sales were $2.6 billion in the quarter ended 30 June 2026, up 7.5% year over year, a third consecutive quarter of growth. Revenue $668 million, take rate 25.9%. In the quarter ended 31 March 2026, revenue was $631 million on GMS of $2.5 billion, up 5.5%, with active buyers and sellers returning to growth. App GMS is roughly 47% of the total (CONFIRMED, Etsy Inc. investor materials and SEC filings).

**Demographics.** Etsy does not publish a current buyer age or sex breakdown and Pew does not measure it. **UNKNOWN.** Etsy's buyer base is widely understood to skew female, which would match BRAND.md's gift-buyer avatar, but I have no Class A source for that and will not assert it.

## 2. How a listing reaches someone who does not follow the shop

**Search, entirely.** Etsy is a marketplace with a search engine attached. Followers are close to meaningless. A listing is found because someone typed "lds temple shirt", which is exactly the behaviour a per-temple catalogue is built for.

This is the same structural advantage Pinterest has, with the difference that the person searching on Etsy has already decided to buy something.

## 3. Shelf life

Years. A listing works until it is deactivated.

## 4. Searchability

Excellent, on two surfaces: Etsy's internal search and Google, which indexes Etsy listings heavily. Of all channels examined, only Pinterest, YouTube long form and Reddit match this.

## 5. Link behavior

Not applicable in the referral sense. Etsy is the destination. It does not send traffic to peculiarpeopleco.com, and Etsy owns the customer relationship, the email address and the review.

## 6. Native commerce

It is entirely native commerce. The cost is a 25.9% take rate on the marketplace side, which against BRAND.md section 17's already-tight print-on-demand margin is a serious problem. A $44.99 tee cannot absorb both print-on-demand cost of goods and an Etsy take rate.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Listing photography, keyword-rich titles, and a review base. **Partly met, and blocked by the same thing everything else is blocked by.** Every current store image is a mockup and the garment range is unsettled (BRAND.md section 7).

## 8. What it is bad at

- **The take rate destroys the margin** on a premium print-on-demand product.
- **It is not a brand.** BRAND.md section 2's vision is a known brand across the Latter-day Saint community. Etsy builds Etsy's brand.
- **It owns the customer.** No email list, no repeat-purchase machinery, no Klaviyo flow.
- **It is a price-comparison environment**, and this brand's price loses that comparison badly. See section 11.
- **No content, no community, no word of mouth**, which BRAND.md section 5 names as the growth engine.
- **It answers the demand question and then stops being useful.** Its main value here is as evidence, not as a channel.

## 9. Real cost per week

As research: about fifteen minutes, once. As a channel: several hours to set up and an hour a week to maintain, against a margin that probably does not survive it.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Etsy requires handmade, designed-by-seller, or vintage. Print-on-demand with original artwork qualifies as designed-by-seller with a production partner declared. Religious items are unrestricted. No elevated risk.

**But there is a premium counter-example, and it matters.** PaperTempleArt of Dallas, Texas: 16 years on Etsy, 113 sales, 5.0 rating from 28 reviews, last active 1 September 2026.

- SLC Temple Art Shadow Box, $80.00, free shipping
- Dallas Temple and Washington D.C. Temple shadow boxes, $80.00 each
- "Any Temple Art Shadow Box, Custom Paper Temple Design", $150.00, "Only 2 left"
- "Bulk Temple Ornament, Christmas Gift for Wards and Relief Society", $50.00
- Custom Temple Night Light, $45.00
- Custom State Mission Necklace, $55.00, "3 people have this in their cart"

**Premium, per-temple, art-led temple products do sustain $80 to $150. What they do not demonstrate is volume: 113 sales in 16 years.** Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing. The price ceiling is not the constraint people assume. The demand depth might be.

Note also the explicit bulk product aimed at wards and Relief Societies, which independently corroborates the group-order play the Playbook calls the fastest realistic route to a first order from a stranger.

**Who the shirt sellers actually are.** AllegrowDesigns has 168 sales, 43 reviews and four years on Etsy, and its catalogue is mostly viola and orchestra joke shirts. The LDS temple shirt is one item in a generic print-on-demand spread across unrelated niches. That supports BRAND.md section 5's claim that nobody is doing this well: the supply is mostly niche-spraying sellers, not dedicated temple brands.

**One finding that is not about Etsy at all.** Multiple sellers are already merchandising a **Salt Lake Temple Open House in 2027**, including matching family shirts. An Instagram post in #ldstemple at 180,000 views (@anticipatingchristsreturn) states "Next year there will be 6 months of public tours! All are invited to come and see inside a house of the Lord!" The "Rejoice in Christ" 2027 youth theme is also already on shirts. Neither BRAND.md nor either artifact mentions any of this. **SINGLE SOURCE, not verified against a Church source**, and it should be checked, because a six-month open house at the brand's most obvious beachhead temple would be the largest occasion-driven demand event available in the planning window.

## Verdict

Do not sell here. The take rate breaks the margin and the brand vision requires owning the customer. But use it, once, as free market research, and treat the two findings above as real evidence: the competitive price band is $13 to $27, and premium temple art sells at $80 to $150 in small volumes.
