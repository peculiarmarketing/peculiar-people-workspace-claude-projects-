# YouTube: long form

Research date 3 September 2026.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 84% of US adults, the highest of any platform in this study (CONFIRMED, Pew, fielded 5 February to 18 June 2025). 48% daily. Age: 95% of 18-29s, 92% of 30-49s, 85% of 50-64s, 64% of 65+. Sex: men 86%, women 83%. Daily use skews male (58% of men, 39% of women) even though total use does not.

**It is also television.** YouTube held the largest share of US television viewing of any single media distributor for three consecutive months in 2026, reaching a platform-best 13.8% of TV watch time in May 2026 (CONFIRMED, Nielsen The Gauge). It was 12.7% in January and February 2026, when NBCUniversal-Versant briefly passed it on Super Bowl LX and the Winter Olympics.

**Global.** Google does not break out YouTube revenue or users by region in a way that is useful here. UNKNOWN.

Read against BRAND.md section 10: this reaches every avatar, including the 65+ grandmother buyer at 64%, better than anything except Facebook.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

Both, and that is unusual. YouTube runs a recommendation feed like the others, but it also runs the second largest search engine in the world, and long-form videos accumulate search traffic for years. A video is discovered by title and topic long after publication.

Metricool's 2026 study found YouTube views per video up 30% year over year while weekly posting rose 25%, the only platform in that study where views grew faster than supply. SINGLE SOURCE, vendor data, but the contrast with TikTok (views down 31%, posting up 72%) is stark.

## 3. Shelf life

**Years.** The longest of any platform here except Pinterest. A practitioner's framing: "the content lives for years, not just for a day or two or a week ... and the longevity is just unbeatable" (Erin On Demand, 6 January 2026). SINGLE SOURCE, but it matches how the platform demonstrably works.

## 4. Searchability

**Excellent, and doubly so.** YouTube's own search plus Google, which surfaces YouTube results prominently. A video titled for a specific temple will be findable by that temple's name indefinitely. Of everything in this study, only Pinterest matches this and only YouTube combines it with depth.

## 5. Link behavior

Description links are clickable and not throttled. Pinned comments carry links. Cards and end screens work. This is a real referral channel, though the click-through habit on YouTube is weaker than on Pinterest.

## 6. Native commerce

YouTube Shopping affiliate connects to Shopify but requires Partner Programme membership: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months, rising to 8,000 hours for new applicants from 1 February 2027 (CONFIRMED, support.google.com). Out of reach in the near term, though far more reachable than the Shorts route of 10 million views in 90 days.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Depth, a title that answers a search, a thumbnail, and typically eight to twenty minutes. It is the most expensive content in this study to produce and it does not fit the Posting Rota's batching model at all.

**Partly met, and the honest answer is uncomfortable.** The raw material is perfect: 119 temples of source-verified research, original line art, and a documented body of knowledge about which official sources serve the wrong building. That is genuinely a channel's worth of subject matter and nobody owns it.

But three constraints bite hard. BRAND.md forbids showing how the line art is made and forbids filming reference gathering, which removes the two most natural long-form formats (process and research). Neither operator should be assumed willing to appear on camera (section 19). And YouTube long form is where narration and a presenter do the work; slow pans over still art for twelve minutes is not a format that holds.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It is the most expensive content in the study per unit**, and it does not splinter from the two production sessions the Posting Rota is built around. It would be a third pillar, not a splinter.
- **It cannot be done without a voice, and probably not without a face.** Both are unresolved (BRAND.md section 19).
- **The two best long-form formats are banned.** No process, no reference gathering.
- **The Church owns this space.** See section 11. Competing for "Salt Lake Temple" against the institution Evan must never imply affiliation with is a structural problem no other platform has.
- **Payback is measured in quarters, not weeks**, which is the wrong shape for a brand whose only constraint is getting a first order.

## 9. Real cost per week

**Six to ten hours for one video a week**, realistically, for someone who is not a full-time creator: scripting from research, recording narration, editing, thumbnail, title. That is more than the entire rest of the plan combined.

Going quiet for a month costs almost nothing here, because the back catalogue keeps working. YouTube is the most forgiving platform in the study for dormancy, which is a genuine argument for building a small library slowly rather than not at all.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted. Standard copyright rules apply to any third-party footage, which Evan does not use.
