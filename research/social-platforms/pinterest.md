# Pinterest

Research date 3 September 2026.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 36% of US adults used Pinterest as of Pew's 2024 wave, fielded through 10 June 2024. **Pew stopped asking about Pinterest in 2025**, so there is no 2025 or 2026 survey figure. Prior waves: 35% in 2023, 31% in 2021. Pinterest's ad tools reported 96.9 million US users, 27.9% of the population, in October 2025 (DataReportal, 5 November 2025).

That is the weakest Class A position of any platform in this study, and it should be stated plainly: **the best survey number for Pinterest in the US is more than two years old.**

**Company-reported, global, quarter ended 30 June 2026 (CONFIRMED, Pinterest Q2 2026 earnings release filed with the SEC on 4 August 2026).** Global monthly active users 640 million, up 11%. US and Canada 106 million, up 4%. Europe 157 million. Rest of world 377 million. ARPU: US and Canada $8.30, Europe $1.35, rest of world $0.23.

The shape of that matters. Pinterest's growth is almost entirely outside North America, but almost all its revenue value per user is inside it. US and Canada is 17% of the users and generates 75% of the revenue.

**Sex.** 71.4% female, 19.2% male, US, October 2025 (DataReportal ad tools; the remainder is unspecified). SINGLE SOURCE, because Pew no longer measures it. This is by far the strongest female skew of any platform here.

**Age.** No current US age breakdown from a Class A source. Pinterest said on its Q2 2026 earnings call that Gen Z is over half the user base and the fastest-growing cohort. SINGLE SOURCE, company statement, not in the filing. UNKNOWN by survey.

Read against BRAND.md section 10: the 71% female skew maps directly onto the "women buying gifts" avatar, which BRAND.md identifies as the Q4 buyer. That is the single best demographic-to-avatar fit in this research.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

**Search media, which is a third category and better than either for a brand with no audience.** Pinterest's own documentation is explicit: "When you add the right text to your Pins, more people will discover them", and it instructs sellers to fill in every Pin field because "some fields don't display to Pinners but power recommendation engines behind the scenes" (help.pinterest.com, current).

Followers are close to irrelevant here. A pin is found because someone searched for a thing, not because they follow you. For a brand with 119 temples of keyword-shaped subject matter, that is structurally favourable in a way no feed platform is.

## 3. Shelf life

**Years, and this is the headline fact.** Pinterest states directly: "There's no set engagement window for Pins, so it's possible that a Pin can gain engagement hours, days, months, or even years after it's first published" (help.pinterest.com, current). CONFIRMED, corroborated independently by a practitioner: "the content lives for so long, literally years" (Erin On Demand, 6 January 2026).

Nothing else in this study has this property. An Instagram carousel about the Manti Temple is finished in three days. A Manti pin is still working in 2028.

## 4. Searchability

**The best of any platform here, on its own terms.** Pinterest is a search engine and its users behave like searchers. Pinterest's help documentation describes people searching "general ideas or phrases, then using suggested results to narrow down to final plans or products". Google also indexes Pinterest pins, so a pin has two search surfaces.

This is the direct answer to "can someone find this later by typing a temple name". On Pinterest, yes. Nearly everywhere else, no.

## 5. Link behavior

**Every pin carries a link, and it is not throttled.** This is the only platform in this study where the native, intended behaviour of a post is to send someone to a URL. The Playbook's existing instruction is correct and worth repeating: link to the specific product page, never the homepage.

On whether that traffic buys: I could not find a trustworthy channel-level conversion benchmark. The figures circulating (Pinterest ROAS 6.2:1, 4.7% social commerce conversion) come from SEO content farms with no primary source and I am throwing them out per the rules of this job. Salesforce's Q2 2026 Shopping Index is the credible source and it measures social in aggregate, not Pinterest specifically: social drove 29% more ecommerce visits year over year while order volume rose 1% and cart abandonment sat at 82%. **Whether Pinterest traffic converts better than other social traffic for a store like this: UNKNOWN.**

What is not unknown is intent. Pinterest's own agency told the Playbook that people build boards "months or years before the event", which is a genuinely good match for a gift-driven, occasion-driven product.

## 6. Native commerce

Product pins and a catalogue feed connect to Shopify. There is no in-app checkout to speak of in the US and no creator commission programme. Pinterest is a referral channel, not a marketplace.

**Pinterest ads were ruled out in the prior research and I am not reopening that.** The reasoning held: the agency's own starting budget was $100 to $200 a day, results took three to four months, and their best results came from brands already proven on Meta or Google. Nothing I found contradicts it.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Vertical images, 2:3, keyword-rich titles and descriptions, fresh pins weekly, one link per pin. No video required. No face. No cadence pressure of the kind feeds impose.

**Yes, fully met, and with the least new work of any platform here.** The line art is already vertical-friendly, already per-temple, and already paired with written, source-verified facts that supply the keywords. The Playbook already scoped the automation: three pins per product, 45 pins for five temples across three garments, over a thousand for the full catalogue.

One caution from Pinterest itself: "Try not to repeatedly save the same Pins or upload content that already exists on Pinterest. You may get flagged as spam." A generator producing a thousand near-identical pins is exactly the pattern that rule is aimed at.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It is slow, and it will not produce the first customer.** The Playbook's own conclusion, and I found nothing to overturn it. Expect nothing readable for one to two months.
- **No community, no conversation, no relationship.** Nobody gets to know a brand on Pinterest. It cannot do the credibility job that section 4 of the cross-cutting file describes.
- **The data foundation is weak.** Pew abandoned the platform. The best US survey number is from June 2024. Age composition is UNKNOWN. A brand betting a channel on Pinterest is betting on ad-tool figures and company statements.
- **I could not verify the audience.** Pinterest is gated to logged-out visitors. See section 11.
- **It rewards volume of pins**, which is machine work, and machine work at volume is exactly what its spam rule watches for.
- **US and Canada growth is 4% a year.** The platform is growing in markets where a US premium apparel brand cannot sell.

## 9. Real cost per week

**Thirty minutes a week, and less once automated.** This is the cheapest platform in the study by a wide margin, because pins are generated from assets that already exist rather than produced. The Playbook's "three pins per product, one per week over three weeks, rotating across products" is about right.

Going quiet for a month costs almost nothing. Pins keep working. This is the only platform in the study where dormancy is nearly free, which is a strong argument for starting it and then ignoring it, exactly as the Playbook says.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted. The spam rule on repetitive pinning is the only real exposure and it is avoidable by varying image, title and description per pin rather than swapping the temple name into a template.

## 11. Is there a Latter-day Saint audience here today

I am not going to estimate. What can be said from adjacent evidence: LDS temple imagery is heavily saved on Pinterest according to BRAND.md's own fact-integrity section, which lists Pinterest among sources "never counted" for temple facts precisely because so much unsourced temple content circulates there. That is evidence the content exists, not evidence a buyer does.

This is the largest single gap in the research, and it sits on the platform with the best structural fit. If Evan creates the free Pinterest business account the Playbook already put in week one, the keyword data behind that login answers this question in about twenty minutes.
