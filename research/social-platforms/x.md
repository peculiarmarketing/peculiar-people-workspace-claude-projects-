# X

Research date 3 September 2026.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 21% of US adults use X, flat since 2024 and down from 23% in 2021 (CONFIRMED, Pew, fielded 5 February to 18 June 2025). Only 10% of US adults use it daily, the lowest daily figure Pew measured. X's ad tools reported 99 million US users, 34.3% of adults, October 2025 (DataReportal). CONFLICT, resolved toward Pew.

**Age, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** 18-29: 33%. 30-49: 25%. 50-64: 16%. 65+: 10%.

**Sex, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** Men 25%, women 16%. X's ad tools put it at 65.6% male, 34.3% female in the US, October 2025 (DataReportal). Both sources agree on a strong male skew, which is the strongest male skew in the study. **CONFIRMED direction.**

**Politics.** Pew found 24% of Republicans and Republican leaners use X versus 19% of Democrats and Democratic leaners, a reversal from two years earlier when Democrats led 26% to 20% (CONFIRMED, Pew, June 2025).

**Global.** X is private and publishes nothing. UNKNOWN.

Read against BRAND.md section 10: the male skew fights the "women buying gifts" avatar, which BRAND.md identifies as the Q4 commercial buyer, and the platform's overall reach is a fifth of Facebook's.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

X publishes its ranking code, which is more transparency than any other platform here offers (github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm, current). Out-of-network recommendations are real and a meaningful share of the timeline. So yes, interest media in principle.

In practice, X's discovery is conversation-driven and news-driven. Its out-of-network surface rewards engagement and reply volume, which is a poor fit for a brand that has ruled out both jokes and argument.

## 3. Shelf life

Hours. The shortest of any text platform here. X is a real-time system.

## 4. Searchability

X search works well inside the platform for recent content and poorly for old content. Google indexing of X posts has been unreliable since the platform restricted crawling. A logged-out visitor cannot search at all, which I verified. UNKNOWN for practical purposes.

## 5. Link behavior

**This is X's known weak point.** The published algorithm includes link penalty modelling, with separate handling for Premium versus non-Premium accounts (visible in the open-source repository, community analysis). The precise magnitude is not something I can state from a primary source. **The existence of link handling in ranking is CONFIRMED from the code; the size of any penalty is UNKNOWN.**

Either way, X is not a channel anyone builds ecommerce referral traffic on in 2026.

## 6. Native commerce

None relevant. No shop, no checkout, no creator commission programme for physical goods.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Text, frequency, opinion and conversation. Threads of short posts. Reply engagement.

**Poorly met, and for a reason that goes to the brand's core.** X rewards taking positions. BRAND.md section 13 rules out politics absolutely and rules out jokes absolutely, which removes the two engines of X reach. A reverent, non-combative, apolitical brand posting temple facts into X is posting into the one platform whose economy runs on the opposite.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **Links are handled adversely and it sends little traffic.**
- **Hours of shelf life and no search memory.**
- **The most male audience in the study**, against a gift-buying avatar that is female.
- **It is the most politically charged environment available**, in a category where BRAND.md's entire guardrail section exists to keep the brand out of arguments.
- **Its US reach is a fifth of Facebook's and its daily use is a fifth of Facebook's.**
- **Logged-out visitors cannot see anything**, which damages even the credibility argument for claiming a handle. A buyer who searches and lands on X gets a login wall.
- **No commerce.**

## 9. Real cost per week

Zero if the handle is claimed and left. Two to three hours a week to do properly, and the properly is not worth doing.

Going quiet for a month costs nothing.

## 10. Rules and account risk

The specific risk here is not policy, it is context. Religious content is unrestricted, but X is where religious topics attract argument fastest, and a brand that must never imply Church affiliation while also never taking a position is unusually exposed on a platform built for taking positions.

Skip, or claim the handle and post nothing. The reach is small, the audience is the wrong sex and the wrong temperament, links are penalised, shelf life is hours, and the logged-out login wall means it does not even pay off as a credibility surface.
