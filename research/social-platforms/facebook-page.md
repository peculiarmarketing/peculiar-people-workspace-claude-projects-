# Facebook: the Page

Research date 3 September 2026. Facebook is split into three files because the three surfaces behave differently enough that treating them as one platform produces a wrong answer. See also `facebook-groups.md` and `facebook-marketplace.md`.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 71% of US adults use Facebook (CONFIRMED, Pew, fielded 5 February to 18 June 2025). Meta's ad tools reported 198 million US users, 71.4% of adults, October 2025 (DataReportal). Those two agree almost exactly, which is unusual and increases confidence. **52% of all US adults use Facebook daily**, the highest daily figure of any platform measured, including 37% who use it several times a day (Pew, n=5,123, fielded 24 February to 2 March 2025).

**Age, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** 18-29: 68%. 30-49: 80%. 50-64: 74%. 65+: 57%. Facebook is the only platform besides YouTube that a majority of every age group uses, and the only one that peaks in middle age rather than youth.

**Sex, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** Women 78%, men 63%. Daily use: women 60%, men 44%. Meta's ad tools agree on direction, 53% female.

Read against BRAND.md section 10: the 30-49 peak at 80% and the 65+ figure of 57% cover the two avatars every other platform misses, which are the gift-buying women and the grandmother buyer. Combined with the 78% female usage and 60% female daily usage, this is the best fit in the study for the Q4 gift buyer that BRAND.md's own Google Trends work identifies as the commercial moment.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the Page

Increasingly, interest media. Meta reported that views and time spent watching original reels on Facebook approximately **doubled** in the second half of 2025 compared with the same period in 2024 (about.fb.com, 13 March 2026). Metricool's 2026 study independently measured Facebook reach up 51%, impressions up 57% and interactions up 56% year over year, "one of the strongest performance rebounds across all platforms". **CONFIRMED**, two independent sources, one of them the company. A third source, marketers on Social Media Examiner (16 April 2026), cites the same Meta doubling figure.

This is the most surprising finding in the whole study. Facebook Page organic reach has been the industry's standing joke for a decade, and in 2025 into 2026 it went up sharply while Instagram and TikTok reach fell.

The reach is going to reels and video specifically, not to text or link posts. Facebook is also testing a "User True Interest Survey" that asks viewers directly what they want more of, feeding reel recommendations (Social Media Examiner, 16 April 2026, SINGLE SOURCE).

## 3. Shelf life

Days for a reel. Longer than Instagram for a Page post, because Facebook still surfaces older posts through shares into groups and through resurfacing. UNKNOWN in precise terms.

## 4. Searchability

Poor to middling. Facebook's internal search is weak for content. Public Page posts are sometimes indexed by Google but not reliably. A temple name typed into Facebook next April will surface groups, not Evan's post. UNKNOWN.

## 5. Link behavior

**A Facebook Page post can carry a clickable link, and this is the one meaningful advantage it has over Instagram.** Link posts have historically received less distribution than native video, which is folklore repeated everywhere but which I could not confirm from a Meta source. Mark that specific claim UNKNOWN.

What is confirmed is that reels are where the reach increase went, and reels do not carry a clickable link in the caption in the way a feed link post does.

## 6. Native commerce

Facebook Shops connects to Shopify. More interesting: **Facebook has rolled out affiliate tools**. A brand submits products, creators browse the catalogue through the professional dashboard, self-serve the products that fit their content, and attach an affiliate link to a reel, earning commission (Social Media Examiner, 16 April 2026, SINGLE SOURCE). Practitioners describe it explicitly as "a play for TikTok marketplace".

This matters because the Playbook's admiration for Comfrt and Bloom rested on TikTok Shop's creator-commission mechanism, and flagged as an open question whether that mechanism was available for this category. It now has a Meta equivalent, on the platform where this brand's buyer demographically lives. The caveat the same source gives: these are not brand partnerships, creators say what they want, so the brand needs an online presence that supports being talked about.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Native uploads, vertical reels for the reach, square or 4:5 for stills, and original content. No face required.

**Yes, fully met.** The Posting Rota already sends both shows to Facebook. Its instruction not to use the Instagram cross-post toggle ("it compresses and strips") is right and is now backed by Meta's own originality policy: a natively uploaded original file is what earns distribution.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It is where the audience is, not where the discovery is, for a brand.** The 51% reach rebound is real but it is measured across all accounts, most of which have existing audiences. A Page from zero still starts at zero.
- **A Page is the weakest Facebook surface for this brand.** The audience on Facebook is gathered in groups, not around brand Pages. See `facebook-groups.md`, where the numbers are an order of magnitude better.
- **It reads as the least premium surface.** A brand positioned as "premium, reverent, understated" (BRAND.md section 5) has to accept that Facebook's visual environment is not.
- **The originality rules carry an unresolved ambiguity.** Meta says it promotes content "filmed or produced directly by the creator or page owner" and takes a "fingerprint" of uploaded video that is pattern-based rather than facial. Marketers on Social Media Examiner (16 April 2026) speculated that "original" may quietly mean "original to Facebook", which would penalise reposting from TikTok or YouTube. **They did not resolve it and neither can I. UNKNOWN.** It is marketer speculation, not a Meta statement, and it is the single most consequential open question for the cross-posting plan.
- **No search memory**, same as Instagram.

## 9. Real cost per week

**Ten to twenty minutes a week if it receives what Instagram already produces**, uploaded natively rather than cross-posted through the toggle. As a standalone platform with its own content it is another two to three hours, and I see no reason to spend them.

Going quiet for a month: harmless. A dormant Page with a few posts still does the credibility job described in the cross-cutting file.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted organically. Meta removed religion-related detailed ad targeting on 19 January 2022 and bars ads that request information about religious belief or discriminate on it, which is an advertising constraint rather than an organic one and does not affect this brand's plan since paid Meta is deferred until roughly $100 a day (BRAND.md section 17).

The originality policy of 13 March 2026 is the live risk and it is a risk only for accounts that repost other people's work. Evan's material is his own.

## 11. Is there a Latter-day Saint audience here today

Yes, overwhelmingly, but it is in groups rather than around Pages. The full evidence is in `facebook-groups.md`. In summary: public Latter-day Saint groups with 267K, 162K, 45K, 44K, 37K, 36K, 28K, 22K and 21K members, including two temple-specific groups and one LDS art group, all verified on 3 September 2026.

I did not find a dominant Latter-day Saint apparel or temple art Page. Page-level reconnaissance was limited because I confined the logged-in session to group search only.
