# LinkedIn

Research date 3 September 2026.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 32% of US adults used LinkedIn as of Pew's 2024 wave, fielded through 10 June 2024, up from 30% in 2023 and 28% in 2021. **Pew stopped asking about LinkedIn in 2025**, so as with Pinterest there is no current survey figure. SINGLE SOURCE and two years stale.

LinkedIn's ad tools reported 270 million US "registered members", which DataReportal itself renders as 98.3% of US adults (October 2025). **That figure is not credible as a measure of who is actually there.** Registered members includes dormant and duplicate accounts, and a claim that 98 out of 100 American adults are on LinkedIn fails a basic sanity check. Discarded.

**Microsoft reports no LinkedIn user or member count in its FY26 quarterly segment reporting.** LinkedIn revenue grew 12% year over year in the quarter ended 31 March 2026 and 10% in the quarter ended 30 September 2025 (CONFIRMED, microsoft.com investor relations). Revenue only.

**Age and sex.** No credible current US breakdown exists from a Class A source. DataReportal's ad tools put the US split at exactly 50/50 female to male, which given the registered-member problem should not be leaned on. **UNKNOWN.**

**This is the thinnest Class A position of any platform in the study.** The honest summary is that roughly a third of US adults had a LinkedIn account two years ago and nobody publishes how many use it.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

Follower media with a modest recommendation layer. LinkedIn's own help documentation states content "is shared with a subset of your connections and followers, depending on your connection strength, your connection's notification settings, and notification state", and that a connection's activity may further affect distribution (linkedin.com/help, current). The three named ranking signals are personal connections, interest relevance and engagement probability.

That is closer to a follower graph than to interest media. A brand with no connections starts genuinely from nothing here, unlike Instagram or TikTok.

A practitioner counterweight: Erin On Demand describes LinkedIn as having "very strong organic reach" and being under-supplied, "the LinkedIn pie, very few people have a bite of it" (6 January 2026). SINGLE SOURCE, and she is describing business-to-business storytelling, not consumer products.

Metricool's 2026 LinkedIn Study (673,658 posts, 63,108 accounts, January to February 2025 versus January to February 2026) found impressions down 23% and interactions down 14%, describing saturation. SINGLE SOURCE, vendor data, and it contradicts the "under-supplied" reading. **CONFLICT.** My read is that both can be true: LinkedIn is under-supplied in some professional niches and saturated in the mainstream, and neither observation tells us anything about selling apparel.

## 3. Shelf life

Days. Longer than X, shorter than YouTube. LinkedIn posts occasionally resurface but do not compound.

## 4. Searchability

Poor for content. LinkedIn profiles rank well in Google; LinkedIn posts do not reliably. A logged-out visitor cannot search content at all, which I verified.

## 5. Link behavior

Outbound links are widely believed to be downranked, with the common workaround of putting the link in the first comment. Practitioner sources repeat it. **I could not confirm it from a LinkedIn source. UNKNOWN.**

## 6. Native commerce

None for physical consumer goods. LinkedIn has no shop, no checkout and no creator commission programme relevant here.

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Professional framing, written storytelling, and a person rather than a brand at the centre. Native video performs better than linked video.

**Met mechanically, but pointing at the wrong audience.** Evan could write well about building an automated product pipeline, about verifying historical facts, about print quality control. That is genuinely good LinkedIn content. It would reach people interested in ecommerce operations and automation. It would not reach a woman in Idaho buying her son a Manti Temple sweatshirt for Christmas.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It is the wrong context entirely.** People are on LinkedIn in a professional frame of mind. A reverent, personal, faith-linked purchase is not a professional-frame purchase.
- **Follower media, so a zero-audience account genuinely starts at zero**, which is the exact problem the interest-media finding was supposed to solve.
- **No commerce, weak links, no search memory.**
- **Fully gated to logged-out visitors**, so it fails the credibility test as well: a buyer who searches the brand and lands on LinkedIn hits a sign-in wall.
- **Its data is the worst-documented in the study.** No current survey figure, no company-reported user count, no credible demographics.
- **It puts the operators' professional identities in front of the brand**, which collides with the unresolved attribution question in BRAND.md section 19.

## 9. Real cost per week

Two to three hours a week to do properly, for an audience that is not the buyer. Zero if the handle is claimed and left dormant.

Going quiet for a month costs nothing.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted. No elevated risk identified. Nothing here is a policy problem; it is a fit problem.
