# Instagram

Research date 3 September 2026.

## 1. Who is actually there

**United States.** 50% of US adults use Instagram (CONFIRMED, Pew Research Center, National Public Opinion Reference Survey, n=5,022, fielded 5 February to 18 June 2025, published 20 November 2025). Meta's own advertising tools reported 182 million US users, 64.1% of adults, in October 2025 (DataReportal, Digital 2026: United States, published 5 November 2025). Those two disagree and the gap is the usual one: ad reach counts accounts, Pew counts people. CONFLICT. My read is Pew, because a person with a personal and a brand account is one buyer, not two.

**Age, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** 18-29: 80%. 30-49: 62%. 50-64: 40%. 65+: 19%.

**Sex, US, June 2025 (Pew, CONFIRMED).** Women 55%, men 44%. Meta's ad tools said 54.5% female in October 2025, which agrees.

**Global.** Meta does not publish an Instagram user count in its quarterly release. A figure of over 2 billion daily actives was stated on the Q2 2026 earnings call (SINGLE SOURCE, company statement, not in the SEC filing).

Read against BRAND.md section 10: this is the closest demographic match in the whole set. Three of the four avatars (returned missionaries 20 to 35, women buying gifts, the newly sealed and endowed) sit inside the 18-49 bands where Instagram runs 62% to 80%, and the female skew matches the gift-buyer avatar.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

**Interest media.** CONFIRMED. Meta's own ranking documentation states the recommendation system "gathers a portion of the public content available on Instagram", scores it against a viewer's inferred interests, and ranks it, independent of whether the viewer follows the author (Meta Transparency Center, Instagram Feed Recommendations, current as of 3 September 2026). Signals named include content quality assessed by a language model, the viewer's reshare and profile-visit history, time spent on reels, and how often other people engage with the author.

I verified this first hand. On TikTok, the account @shopzmci ("modest clothing and temple dresses", links to Shopzmci.com) has **169 followers** and its post still surfaced in the #ldstemple tag alongside far larger accounts. That is TikTok rather than Instagram, but it is the same mechanism and it is the closest observed analogue to a zero-audience brand.

One correction to the optimism, from a practitioner: Erin On Demand ("In 2026, which platform should you focus on?", 6 January 2026, 27,007 views) argues Instagram "discovery is limited unless you already have some type of established community" and that "Instagram works better as more of a hub and not necessarily a launchpad". SINGLE SOURCE and it is opinion, but it is a direct counterweight to the settled interest-media finding and it deserves to be on the record.

Format matters. Metricool's 2026 Instagram Study (24.3 million posts across 375,000 accounts, published 2026) found carousels generate roughly nine times more saves than single-image posts and reels roughly four times the interactions. SINGLE SOURCE, vendor data. Saves are the metric the Posting Rota already told Evan to watch, and carousels are already the Rota's main format, so this corroborates rather than changes the plan.

## 3. Shelf life

Days. A reel can be resurfaced later by recommendations but there is no search-driven long tail comparable to Pinterest or YouTube. UNKNOWN in precise terms; Meta publishes no shelf-life figure.

## 4. Searchability

Weak. Hashtag pages exist and are public (I read #ldstemple logged out), but Instagram is not a place a stranger types "Manti Temple" into a year later and finds Evan. Google indexes public Instagram profiles and some posts, but not reliably enough to plan around. UNKNOWN in any measurable sense.

## 5. Link behavior

One link in bio, plus link stickers in stories. No clickable link in a feed post or reel caption. This is the single biggest structural weakness for a store. Traffic must be pulled through a profile visit, which means the profile itself is the conversion surface, not the post.

Salesforce's Q2 2026 Shopping Index (1.5 billion shoppers, 67 countries) reports social drove 29% more ecommerce visits year over year while overall order volume rose 1% and cart abandonment sat at 82%. CONFIRMED for the direction: social sends more traffic than it used to, and that traffic converts worse than it used to.

## 6. Native commerce

Instagram Shopping exists and connects to Shopify. Checkout inside the app has been progressively wound down in the US. There is no creator commission marketplace on Instagram equivalent to TikTok Shop's affiliate programme. Meta has, however, rolled out affiliate tools on Facebook where creators self-serve products and attach affiliate links to reels, described by practitioners as "a play for TikTok marketplace" (Social Media Examiner, 16 April 2026). Whether that extends to Instagram is UNKNOWN.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It cannot send traffic from a post.** No link in captions. Every click costs a profile visit.
- **It has no memory.** A post about the Manti Temple stops working within days and cannot be found by someone searching for Manti next April.
- **The winning format in this niche is one Evan has banned.** In #ldstemple the two highest-view posts I observed were both jokes: @orthocardia at 229K ("Pros and Cons of having a girlfriend who goes to the temple with you") and @purefamilyjoy at 16.1K ("The secret life of this Mormon wife"). BRAND.md section 13 rules out jokes and meme humour absolutely. Evan will be competing in a tag whose top performers use a tool he has taken off the table.
- **Reach is falling.** Metricool's 2026 study found Instagram reels reach down 35% and post reach down 31% year over year. SINGLE SOURCE, vendor data.
- **It rewards frequency and polish**, which is expensive for a two-person operation.

## 9. Real cost per week

Two to three hours a week for five posts once templates exist, which is the Posting Rota's own estimate and it is realistic for carousels built from an existing research file. Add roughly twenty minutes a day for the commenting practice the Rota mandates, which is the larger number: that is another two hours a week. **Call it four to five hours a week all in.**

If the account goes quiet for a month, nothing is destroyed. Reach is per-post, not per-account, so an interest-media feed will still test a new post against interested viewers. What is lost is the recurrence that makes people follow a slot, and the comment relationships. Recoverable.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted. Meta removed religion-related detailed ad targeting options on 19 January 2022 (Meta for Business), and its Discriminatory Practices ad standard bars ads that discriminate on religion or request information about religious belief. Neither touches organic posting about temples.

The real rule is originality. Meta stated on 13 March 2026 that "content that is duplicative or involves minor edits to another creator's post will be considered unoriginal and deprioritized", and that accounts posting mostly unoriginal material can be "deemed non-recommendable and demonetized" (about.fb.com, CONFIRMED against Instagram's own creator guidance). Instagram separately states it will show only the original when it finds identical content, and that aggregator accounts posting ten or more copies in thirty days "will not be shown in surfaces where we recommend content" (creators.instagram.com, 30 April 2024). None of this bites Evan, whose material is entirely his own. It does mean a TikTok-watermarked file must never be uploaded here, which the Rota already says.
