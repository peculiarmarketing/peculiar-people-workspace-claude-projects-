# Facebook: Groups

Research date 3 September 2026.

**Disclosure on method.** The in-app browser used for this research was already signed in to Evan's personal Facebook account. I did not log in and I did not know this until I began. All Facebook figures below were read from group search results as that signed-in account. Nothing was joined, opened, posted in, or messaged. Everything recorded is a public group name, privacy setting, member count and posting velocity as Facebook displayed it. Anyone else running the same search would see substantially the same list, minus the "friends are members" annotations.

## 2. How a post reaches someone who does not follow the account

**It does not need to.** This is the only surface in the study that is not an algorithm problem. Membership is the distribution. A post in "Latter-Day Saint Temples" reaches a room of 10,000 people who joined specifically because they care about temples.

The variable that decides whether the post is seen is posting velocity, not follower count. In the 162K group a post competes with 90 or more others a day and is gone within the hour. In the 10K temple group at 6 posts a day, a single post sits visibly on the page. **Small and slow beats large and fast**, and that inverts the instinct to chase the biggest group.

## 3. Shelf life

Hours in a fast group, days in a slow one. Group content is not archived usefully.

## 4. Searchability

Groups themselves are searchable and that is how I found them. Posts inside groups are effectively not searchable from outside. Private group content is not indexed at all.

## 5. Link behavior

A link in a group post is clickable. Whether it survives is a moderation question rather than an algorithmic one. Meta's Pages, Groups and Events policies note that admins can decline posts containing specific links and can give the author feedback to edit and resubmit (facebook.com/policies_center, current). Many LDS groups will have explicit no-selling rules.

## 6. Native commerce

None inside a group. Selling in groups is governed by Meta's commerce policies and, more practically, by whatever each group's admins allow. Meta states admins "are solely responsible for including all necessary disclosures ... including those required to indicate the commercial nature of content they may post" (Pages, Groups and Events policies, current).

## 7. What it demands, and can Evan meet it

Participation, not publication. A group post that reads as an advertisement dies or gets removed. A group post that is genuinely interesting to the room works.

**Yes, and better than anywhere else.** Evan's actual asset is a source-verified research library on 119 temples, and "LDS temples" at 22K members and "Latter-Day Saint Temples" at 10K are rooms full of people who would read a well-sourced fact about a specific building for its own sake. The Posting Rota's Temple Study is not adapted content here. It is native content.

The constraint is that this is manual, human work. It cannot be scheduled, splintered or automated, which means it does not benefit from the pillar-and-splinter method that makes the rest of the plan cheap.

## 8. What it is bad at

- **It does not scale and it cannot be automated.** Every unit of reach costs a unit of Evan's or Bailee's attention.
- **Self-promotion is the fastest way to be removed.** Group rules commonly ban it outright, and admins have tools to decline posts by link.
- **It builds no owned asset.** A hundred hours in groups leaves nothing behind. The group belongs to its admins.
- **It is the highest-risk surface for the brand's reputation**, because it is the one where a misjudged post is seen by a named, gathered community that talks to itself. BRAND.md section 5 says growth is expected to come from word of mouth, and word of mouth runs both directions.
- **No search memory, no shelf life, no compounding.**
- **The admins are gatekeepers with no obligation to Evan.** The Playbook's seeding methodology already identifies group moderators as targets. That is an outreach job with a low hit rate, not a channel.

## 9. Real cost per week

**Highly variable, and easy to underestimate.** Genuine participation in three or four groups is roughly two to four hours a week, and it is attention-expensive work rather than production work: reading threads, answering questions, judging tone. It is closest in character to the Posting Rota's daily commenting practice, which the Rota already budgets twenty minutes a day for.

Going quiet for a month costs the relationships, which are the only asset here. Unlike a feed, this one does decay.

## 10. Rules and account risk

Religious content is unrestricted. Meta's Spam community standard and Pages, Groups and Events policies both apply. The practical risk is not a Meta ban, it is being removed and remembered by a moderator in a community where the same moderators run several of the largest rooms.

One structural note: posting into groups is done from a personal profile, not a Page, which means this work is attributed to a person. BRAND.md section 19 lists "voice and attribution once Bailee runs social" as an open decision. Facebook Groups is the surface where that decision becomes unavoidable first, because a group post carries a real name and a real face by default.

## 11. Is there a Latter-day Saint audience here today

**Yes. This is the largest concentrated, addressable, verified Latter-day Saint audience found anywhere in this research.** The table in section 1 is the evidence, all of it read directly on 3 September 2026.

The two temple groups and the LDS Art group are the ones that matter. 22K, 10K and 21K people who have self-selected into exactly the three subjects this brand is about.
